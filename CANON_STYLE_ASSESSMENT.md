# Canon Style Assessment — DBZ Twins vs Supergirl+Adrian

*Date: 2026-09-27*
*Based on reading DBZ Twins Ch1–Ch16 and Supergirl Ch1–Ch26*

---

## What I Have Learned About the DBZ Twins Voice

### 1. The Cold Open Is Always a Concrete Image

DBZ Twins cold opens are ALWAYS a concrete visual image — two sentences, external camera, no interiority:

- Ch11: "A length of red string hangs from the fork of a fallen tree. Beneath it, the river has left a dark line on the stones."
- Ch13: "A leaf lies beneath a stone in the yard. Its tip lifts each time the wind reaches it."
- Ch16: "A drop of rain rests on the Four-Star Ball. It trembles when the wind reaches the hat, then rolls down the brim and falls onto an open book."

The image is specific, physical, and carries story meaning. It is NOT a feeling, a thought, or a summary.

**Supergirl gap:** Ch1 cold open is "Kara had been given someone to look after." — This is interiority (a mental state), not a concrete image. The prose then continues with more interiority: "she could understand", "she could tell him", "she could make sure", "that small, necessary certainty".

### 2. No Interiority — Ever

DBZ Twins NEVER describes what a character thinks or feels. The camera is external. If a character processes something, the chapter shows the ACTION, not the thought.

- Ch13: "Gohan sits up and looks at the empty place beside him, then finds Goshin's folded blanket at the foot of the bed." — external action, not "Gohan felt lonely"
- Ch16: "A fourth bowl stands at the end of the table. Goshin put it there for Goku the day before." — held frame, external, carries meaning without saying what it means

**Supergirl gap:** The prose tells us what characters feel:
- "that was the part she could understand"
- "she could tell him about it"
- "she could make sure he wasn't alone"
- "carrying that small, necessary certainty"
- "she could still remember exactly how it had felt to be needed"
- "Kara felt the absence more sharply than another warning"

### 3. Section Headings in Every Chapter

DBZ Twins uses section headings (## Title Case) in EVERY chapter to structure the episode:

- Ch11: ## A Telephone for C-6, ## The High Path, ## The Ballpark
- Ch13: ## The Next Morning, ## At the Tower, ## The Waterfall, ## The Ring
- Ch16: ## The Third Basket, ## Two Piccolos, ## A Book for C-6, ## Grandfather's Pot

The headings are concise, evocative, and scene-based.

**Supergirl gap:** 10 of 26 chapters have ZERO section headings (Ch5, Ch6, Ch10, Ch12, Ch16, Ch17, Ch19, Ch20, Ch22, Ch24, Ch25). Chapters that do have them are inconsistent.

### 4. Hard Cuts Between Every Scene

DBZ Twins uses `---` to hard-cut between every scene change. Every chapter has them.

**Supergirl gap:** 0 hard cuts in Ch5, Ch6. Inconsistent elsewhere.

### 5. Summary vs. Scene

DBZ Twins dramatizes. It does NOT summarize. If something happened in the past, it shows a specific moment, not a paragraph of summary.

- Ch13 does NOT summarize "Gohan trained with Goku." It shows the morning scene, the breakfast, the conversation, the walk to the basin.

**Supergirl gap:** Ch1 summarizes Kara's entire backstory: "Kara learned English. She learned which sounds she was supposed to hear... Twenty-four years passed elsewhere... He found her a home with the Danvers family." This is summary, not scene.

### 6. The Hold

DBZ Twins uses held frames — the camera holds on a detail that carries meaning:

- Ch16: "A fourth bowl stands at the end of the table. Goshin put it there for Goku the day before. When Chi-Chi reaches across, he watches to see whether she will move it. She moves Gohan's book instead, and the bowl keeps its place."

This is a held frame: the bowl, the watching, the decision, the result. All external. All carries meaning.

### 7. Repeat-It-Back

DBZ Twins uses repeat-it-back for clarity — a character repeats an instruction to confirm understanding:

- Ch16: "I can make sure twice." / "Then I'll make sure you remember you did."
- Ch13: "Wake me if you have that dream again." / "I will."

### 8. One Design Line Per Chapter

DBZ Twins shows ONE character detail per chapter and says nothing about the rest.

- Ch13: Gohan checking the Four-Star Ball on his hat — once, then it's done.
- Ch16: The scouter on the bench — one insert, then it's done.

### 9. Character Voice Distinction

Each character in DBZ Twins has a distinct voice:

- Goku: casual, enthusiastic, "Sure!", "That's a good morning!"
- Chi-Chi: practical, measured, "Those are for all of us."
- Gohan: earnest, curious
- Goshin: thoughtful, precise
- C-6: formal, precise

**Supergirl gap:** Kara and Adrian's voices are sometimes hard to distinguish. The prose uses similar sentence structures for both.

### 10. The Stranger Test

DBZ Twins: "If someone can't summarize the paragraph in one plain sentence, it's wrong."

Every paragraph in DBZ Twins passes this test. The Supergirl prose has paragraphs that are hard to summarize because they mix action, interiority, and summary.
