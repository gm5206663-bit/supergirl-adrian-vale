# Local prose implementation

Status: assistant implementation of the author’s GitHub craft method; amendable by the author.

- Close-third scene viewpoints, with clear transitions. Multiple viewpoints are allowed; do not restrict the reader to the OC’s access.
- Plain, fluent prose with varied rhythm. Avoid chains of one-line fragments or repetitive quips that substitute for characterization.
- Dialogue must reflect the speaker’s goals and relationships, not make everyone equally witty.
- Give scenes a place, objective, resistance, change and consequence. Do not replace the canon characters’ scenes with an OC overhearing their summaries.
- Show earned expertise through choices and work. Under the seal, do not sneak in blocked supersenses to advertise power.
- Do not explain “canon ownership,” file names, gate instructions or numerical comparisons inside story narration.
- Spell ordinary count-numbers in prose; technical names and necessary identifiers may receive explicit exceptions. R1’s standalone flight number remains flagged rather than silently altered.
- No fixed word quota has been chosen. Sentence and paragraph metrics are advisory diagnostics, never permission to break grammar.
- No story footers containing ledgers or research links. Keep them in coverage/audit files. A reader-facing scene heading may locate time/place without exposing secrets to characters.
- Original writing must transform the premise and develop the OC; source research is not permission to reproduce a screenplay.

Manual review must check repetitive phrasing, emotional truth, readable paragraphs, character voice, causality and whether the OC actually has an arc. An automatic PASS is not this review.

## Author-selected chapter heading format

Episode first, then chapter:

```markdown
# Season 1, Episode 1 — Pilot

## Chapter 1 — Adrian Vale
```

Use the actual canonical episode title and the current chapter number/title. Digits in these headings are intentional and separate from prose style rules. A heading-format change does not accept a chapter or advance episode coverage.

## Character-consistent consequences — U23
A butterfly must follow an action the character would plausibly take. Do not make an experienced, secrecy-conscious character forget a mask, cover or routine preparation just to obtain a dramatic identity reveal. Show preparation concretely, then let changed information change other people’s decisions. Do not paste a costume onto a plot that still assumes everyone identified its wearer.
