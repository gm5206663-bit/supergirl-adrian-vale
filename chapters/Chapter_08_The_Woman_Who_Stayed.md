# Chapter 8: The Woman Who Stayed
*Episode: S01E05 - Livewire*
The apartment smelled like coffee and reheated soup when Kara arrived.
Alex was standing by the window with her arms crossed, watching the street as if she could see their mother coming from three blocks away.
“She texted that she landed,” Alex said without turning. “She took a cab. She didn't want us to pick her up.”
“That sounds like her,” Kara said.
“It sounds like she wanted time to prepare what she'll say about you being on television.”
Kara set the bag of groceries on the counter. “She knows I'm—”
“She knows Supergirl exists. She doesn't know you work for the DEO.” Alex finally turned. “Or that I do.”
Before Kara could answer, her phone buzzed. Winn: *Leslie Willis just did her annual Thanksgiving list. You are the only item. And she isn't grateful.*
Kara sighed. “I need to go to CatCo for an hour. Cat wants Leslie in her office.”
Alex nodded. “Go. I'll stall Mom until you get back. Tell CatCo I said happy Thanksgiving.”
At CatCo, the newsroom was louder than usual. On every screen, a woman with sharp eyeliner and a headset was talking into a microphone.
“This is Leslie Willis, coming to you live and wired from CatCo Plaza. The week of Thanksgiving, which means it's time for my annual list of things I'm not grateful for. And this year's list is only one item: Supergirl.”
James looked up from his desk. “She does this every year?”
“Apparently,” Winn said. “Last year it'd the mayor. Year before that, kale.”
Kara slipped past them toward Cat's office. Inside, Cat was listening with the expression of someone who had mentored someone and was now regretting the curriculum.
Leslie finished her rant with a comment about Supergirl's costume and a speculation about her love life that made Kara wince.
Cat pressed the intercom. “Ms. Willis. My office. Now.”
Leslie arrived still wearing the headset. She smiled as if she expected applause.
“You warned me not to go after Supergirl,” Cat said.
“I went after a public figure who flies around in—”
“You insulted her body, how she dresses and her sexuality,” Cat said. “You crossed a line when I specifically told you where it'd. That was part of our dynamic? No. Our dynamic was that I pushed you to be better and you decided being worse got better ratings.”
Leslie's smile thinned. “People like honesty.”
“People like optimism, hope and positivity. They're sick of uncalled-for negativity. You have had a positive impact on this city? No. Supergirl has. All you're doing is spouting negativity that people are tired of.”
Cat stood.
“I'm not buying out your contract because you would enjoy the vacation. You are transferred to traffic. Cat-Copter. You will report traffic from the chopper for the foreseeable future. You will be the highest-paid traffic reporter in National City.”
Leslie stared. “You can't—”
“I can. You have a chopper to catch.”
After Leslie left, Kara lingered.
“Ms. Grant—”
“Not now, Keira. Go help Winn cover the phones. Leslie's fans are already calling.”
Outside, Winn handed Kara a second phone. “Your friend Adrian has a visitor. She is waiting in the lobby. She said she's family?”
Kara frowned. “Adrian doesn't have family here.”
“She said to tell him Evelyn is here. She has a duffel and a chipped mug.”
In the lobby, a woman stood near the elevators. She was in her early thirties, dark wavy hair pulled back, wearing a soft sweater and a long coat, holding a duffel and a paper cup. She was beautiful in a way that made people look twice and then look again because she smiled at them.
When Adrian came down from the executive floor, his face changed.
“Ev.”
She set the cup down and hugged him. Not a quick office hug. She put both arms around him, squeezed, then pulled back to look at him, hands on his shoulders, then one hand briefly on his cheek.
“Kid,” she said. “You are thinner. Are you eating?”
“I'm eating,” Adrian said, and for the first time that week he sounded unguarded.
“I brought soup. Actual soup, not the thing you call soup that's just hot water with hope in it.” She glanced at Kara, who was standing by the reception desk with phones in both hands. “You must be Kara. He talks about you. All good things, I promise.”
Kara managed to smile. “Hi. I'm—”
“Kara Danvers. Cat's assistant. The one who's almost as good at Settlers of Catan as Cat.” Evelyn extended a hand, warm. “she Hart.”
Adrian looked between them. “she helped me when I first came to National City. When I was—” He searched for a word that wouldn't require an explanation. “Figuring out how to live here.”
“She means when he was terrible at being human,” she said lightly. “He still is, but less.”
Kara laughed before she could stop herself. Adrian gave she a look that was half exasperation, half affection.
“I've to get back to the desk,” Kara said. “Nice to meet you.”
“You too.” she picked up her duffel. “I'll steal him for an hour. Family dinner. He promised he wouldn't work through Thanksgiving.”
Kara watched them go toward the elevators, she's hand briefly on Adrian's arm as she told him about Vancouver, about a director who made everyone do trust falls. The gesture was casual, caring, familiar.
It looked like something else if you didn't know the history.
***
The call about the helicopter came while Kara was at her apartment with Alex and Eliza.
Eliza Danvers was exactly as Kara remembered and not at all as she remembered. She hugged Kara tightly, held Alex a fraction longer and then looked around the apartment as if checking whether the windows locked.
“You left the stove on last time I visited,” Eliza said to Alex.
“That was six years ago.”
“And you left the news on about Supergirl saving Leslie Willis,” Eliza said, turning to Kara. “I asked Alex to turn it off.”
Kara glanced at Alex, who mouthed *sorry*.
Eliza had brought a pie. She set it on the counter and began asking about work. Alex said she worked in a lab. Kara said she worked for Cat Grant. Both answers were true enough to pass until Eliza asked Alex why she had let Kara reveal her powers.
Alex went still.
“Mom—” Kara began.
“No,” Alex said quietly. “Let her say it.”
Eliza looked at her older daughter. “You were supposed to look after her. I thought I could count on you to keep her safe.”
“I've,” Alex said. “Every day.”
“By letting her fly around in a cape?”
“By working where I can actually protect her.”
The room went quiet. Outside, wind rattled the windows. Kara thought about the porch flashback Eliza didn't know she remembered.

Two girls on a roof. One convincing the other to sneak out for a flight. Holding on tight while the city fell away beneath them.

Then the worried faces of Eliza and Jeremiah when they returned. The way Jeremiah had said *someone will see you and come to take her away*.
Alex's phone buzzed. She looked at it, then at Kara.
“Helicopter. Cat-Copter. Pilot losing control in lightning.”
Kara was already moving toward the balcony.
“Eliza, I need to—”
“Go,” Eliza said, surprising her. “Go help.”
On the roof, rain had started. Kara changed, felt the seal-less air for a second— she wasn't sealed, she never had been— and launched.
Below, the helicopter spiraled, door hanging loose. The pilot was shouting. Leslie Willis clung to the frame, headset gone.
Kara caught the pilot first, set him on the sidewalk, told Alex to clear the area, then caught the helicopter before it hit a building. Metal groaned under her hands. She grabbed Leslie's hand to pull her out.
Lightning struck.
It hit Kara first, passed through her, and deflected onto Leslie. For an instant, both of them lit up. Kara felt the charge go through her and out, felt Leslie convulse.
Then Leslie went limp, hair turning silver-white in a split second before Kara lowered the chopper to the street.
At the hospital, Cat arrived with Kara still in her assistant clothes, hair damp.
“How long do we've to stay before it's appropriate to leave?” Cat asked, looking at Leslie's still form.
Kara stared at the monitors. “She doesn't have family here. No gifts from fans.”
“Supergirl must feel awful,” Cat said.
“She saved her,” Kara said. “If anyone should feel bad, it's—”
“It is me,” Cat finished quietly. “I put her in that helicopter.” She stepped closer to the bed, hesitated, then said, “Fight, Leslie. I know you're stronger than any lightning bolt.”
They left before Leslie woke.
In an alley across town, Leslie woke on wet concrete, gasping. A man approached, hand outstretched, not kindly.
She flinched. Electricity crackled from her fingers and threw him back.
She looked at her hands, at the light crawling over them, and smiled evilly before vanishing into a bundle of energy that rode the city's electrical lines.
***
Adrian had taken she to a small diner near his apartment, the kind that served soup that actually counted as soup.
“You are still doing the thing where you work through holidays,” she said, stirring her tea.
“I'm not. Cat gave us tomorrow off.”
“Tomorrow. Today you were at CatCo with two phones and a look that says you haven't slept.” She reached across and fixed his collar, a motherly gesture. “When did you last eat something that wasn't from a bakery bag?”
“This morning. Kara—” He stopped. “I'd breakfast.”
she studied him. “Kara is the one you mentioned? The coworker who's—” She searched for his phrasing. “Trying very hard?”
“Yes.”
“She is lovely. Nervous, but lovely.”
Adrian nodded.
she leaned back. “You like her.”
It wasn't a question. Adrian didn't answer immediately.
“She is—” He looked out the window at the rain. “She is trying to do a lot. Like you said I was.”
“That isn't what I asked.”
“I know.”
she smiled, soft. “Kid, you're allowed to like people. Even after—” She didn't finish. She didn't need to. She knew he had come to National City wanting ordinary life after something terrible, knew he used a device to stay at human-level, knew he wanted to be normal. She didn't know the city he had destroyed, or the name Kael-Varr, or the prisoners who feared him.
“I brought you something,” she said, changing subject. She pulled a small, chipped ceramic magnet from her bag, shaped like a theater mask. “For your fridge. So you remember you've a fridge.”
He took it. “Thank you.”
They finished eating. On the way out, she linked her arm through his, leaning a little because the sidewalk was slick.
Kara, coming from the hospital with Cat, saw them from across the street.
She had gone to find Adrian to tell him about Leslie, about the lightning, about the way Cat had looked at the hospital bed. She had expected to find him at his desk, or in the break room with a folder.
Instead, she saw him with the beautiful woman from the lobby, arm in arm, her head briefly on his shoulder as she laughed at something he said. She saw the way he carried her duffel, the way she fixed his collar again, the way he looked down at her with an openness Kara had rarely seen.
It looked, to anyone who didn't know, like a couple who had known each other a long time. Like a girlfriend who had come to surprise him for Thanksgiving.
Kara stopped at the edge of the crosswalk, rain on her glasses, and felt something awkward and tight settle in her chest.
She had thought — she hadn't really thought, but she had begun to consider that Adrian might be someone she could —

The man who brought glazed donuts and remembered she liked pastries. Who had stood beside her at the DEO and told her she could call for help. Who had held a damaged reactor housing with care while she removed its core.
She turned away before they could see her.
She didn't go to the diner. She went back to CatCo, told Winn she would cover the evening phones and tried to focus on the screens.
***
At CatCo that evening, the building lost power.
Every screen went black, then lit up with a face Kara recognized, hair white, eyes crackling.
“Gobble, gobble. Miss me?” Leslie's voice came through the speakers, distorted. “No. Leslie Willis is dead. D-E-A-D dead. This is Leslie 2.0. Livewire. And we're going to have words.”
Cat was in her office with Kara when the lights went out. She looked at the screens, then at Kara.
“Leslie?”
Kara was already moving toward the window.
“Cat, you need to go to security, twenty floors down, while I—”
“You want me to leave you here with her?”
“I'll be fine. Go.”
Cat looked at her for a long second, then did something Kara didn't expect. She put a protective arm over Kara's shoulders and pushed her toward the door.
“Go to security,” Cat said loudly, for Leslie's benefit. “Now.”
Kara ran. In the stairwell, she changed, cape snapping, and came back through the window as Supergirl, shielding Cat from a bolt of lightning.
“Leslie, stop!”
Livewire laughed, electricity whipping from her hands. “You made me! You and her!”
Supergirl tried heat vision. Livewire absorbed it, grin widening.
“You can't—”
Kara tried punching her. Livewire was injured, but used the moment to drop Cat's elevator. Supergirl caught it at the last second, lowering it gently while Livewire escaped into the wiring.
Later, at the DEO, Henshaw examined the readings.
“Kara was the Element X,” he said. “Lightning passed through you to her. She has a physical form despite being energy-based. She can be fought.”
Kara nodded, still thinking about the alley, about Cat's arm over her shoulders.
“It is my fault,” she said.
“It is mine too,” Cat said from the doorway, surprising them both. She had come to the DEO because Supergirl had asked the FBI for a direct line. “I encouraged her nastiness because it got ratings. I should have pushed her to be better.”
She looked at Kara, then at Henshaw.
“I want to help.”
***
Kara found Adrian in the break room the next morning. He was alone, making coffee, the theater mask magnet now on the fridge.
she wasn't there.
“Hey,” Kara said, trying to sound normal.
“Hey.” He glanced at her. “You were at the hospital yesterday?”
“Yes. Leslie— she—” She stopped. “How was your dinner?”
Adrian poured coffee. “Good. she leaves tomorrow morning. She was filming in Vancouver.”
Kara stared at the magnet. “She is—” She searched for a casual way to ask. “She is your—”
“My family,” Adrian said simply.
The word landed differently than Kara expected.
“She helped me when I first came here,” he said. “When I was figuring out how to live like this. How to be—” He touched his cuff, where the band sat beneath. “Ordinary. She taught me how to shop, how to use the bus, how to not answer every question as if it were an interrogation. She made sure I ate.”
Kara felt her face warm. “Oh.”
“She calls me kid,” Adrian added, a small smile. “She is four years older than me, but she decided that made her responsible.”
Kara thought about the collar-fixing, the arm-linking, the head on shoulder. Seen again with this explanation, it looked like an older sister fussing over a younger brother who never learned to wear a scarf correctly.
“I saw you together yesterday,” Kara admitted quietly. “Outside the diner. I thought—”
Adrian looked at her.
“I thought she was your girlfriend,” Kara said, the words rushing out. “She is beautiful and you looked— you looked happy and I didn't know and I—”
“You thought I hadn't mentioned a girlfriend,” Adrian finished for her, not unkindly.
Kara nodded, glasses slipping.
“She isn't my girlfriend,” Adrian said. “She is my sister. Not by blood. By—” He searched. “By staying.”
From the doorway, she's voice: “By being stubborn enough to stay when he was terrible at being human.”
She stood there with two paper cups, hair still damp from rain, smiling at Kara with an actress's perceptiveness.
“You must be Kara,” she said again, as if they hadn't met. “The one he talks about when he thinks I'm not listening.”
Kara flushed deeper. “I— I misunderstood. I'm sorry. I saw you and I—”
“You saw a beautiful woman fussing over him and assumed the obvious,” she said warmly. “Anyone would. He has that face that makes people assume the obvious.” She handed Kara a cup. “I'm she. I'm not his girlfriend. I'm the person who taught him that soup comes from a pot, not a vending machine. And that he should wear a coat when it rains.”
Kara took the cup. “I thought—”
“I know what you thought,” she said gently. “It is all right. I've been mistaken for his girlfriend, his mother, his sister and once, memorably, his parole officer. I've been all of those except the first and last.”
Adrian made a small sound that might have been a laugh.
she looked at Kara more closely. “You like him.”
Kara choked on tea. “I— we work together—”
“That isn't what I asked either,” she said, echoing her earlier words to Adrian, and smiled. “It is all right. I like him too. That is why I'm hard on him.”
She reached out and fixed Kara's collar, just as she had fixed Adrian's, a quick motherly tug.
“There,” she said. “Now you both look presentable.”
Kara looked at Adrian, who was watching them with an expression that was somewhere between exasperation and gratitude.
“I should go,” Kara said. “Cat wants—”
“Cat wants you to stop apologizing for things that don't need apologies,” she said. “But go. We will be here when you get back.”
After Kara left, she turned to Adrian.
“She likes you,” she said.
“I know.”
“And you like her.”
Adrian didn't answer.
she squeezed his arm. “Kid, you're allowed to be happy that someone likes you. Even if you're still figuring out how to be ordinary.”
***
That evening, Cat called Leslie out on her radio show.
“Leslie, meet me where it all began,” Cat said into the microphone, voice steady. “Your grudge isn't with National City. It is with me.”
At the old radio studio, Livewire appeared, energy crackling, city in blackout behind her.
“You and I got through you not supporting Hillary in 2008, we can get through this,” Cat said.
Livewire sneered. “You demoted me.”
“I protected you from yourself and failed. I'm here now.”
Supergirl arrived as Livewire raised her hands. The fight was brief and bright. Livewire, bolstered by a city's worth of energy, formed whips of lightning, knocked away the DEO's industrial capacitor trap that Kara had compared to Ghostbusters.
Kara remembered what she had learned: water and electricity don't mix.
She used her x-ray vision to spot a water main, grabbed a pipe under the street and shorted Livewire out. The villainess fell, comatose, energy fading.
The city lights flickered back on.
At the DEO, Alex and Eliza sat in Kara's apartment, illuminated by candlelight from the blackout. Eliza apologized.
“I wanted you to be better than me,” Eliza said to Alex. “That is why I was hard on you. Kara was a child from another planet who had lost everything, so I couldn't do anything else but accept her. You were my daughter, so you could take it.”
Alex, booze and honesty mixing, told her mother what she really did for a living: not in a lab, but at the DEO, protecting Kara.
Eliza was quiet for a long time, then said, “You have always been my super girl.”
Later, Eliza told them about Jeremiah. How Hank Henshaw had come to their house after Kara arrived, wanting to take Kara because Superman refused to work with them. How Jeremiah had agreed to work for the DEO and give them all his Superman research if Hank left Kara alone. How he had died under mysterious circumstances and all she was told was that it had been an accident.
Kara and Alex looked at each other. Outside the DEO, Hank's eyes glowed red in the dark corridor.
At CatCo the next day, Kara brought Winn leftovers from Thanksgiving. He told her what he was grateful for: her. His father was in prison, he wasn't a fan of holidays, but she had allowed him to be part of her family. He kissed her on the cheek, awkwardly.
Kara reacted as awkwardly as expected, then saw James and Lucy return from Ojai, diverting her attention.
Cat was in a holiday spirit, deciding to run photos of people volunteering on Thanksgiving instead of paparazzi shots. She looked at Kara for a long moment.
“There is a lot I don't know about you,” Cat said.
“That should probably change,” Kara replied.
she was leaving that afternoon. Adrian walked her to the small hotel lobby, duffel over his shoulder.
“You will call?” she asked.
“Yes.”
“And eat?”
“Yes.”
“And tell that girl that you like her back, or at least that you aren't dating your sister?”
Adrian smiled, genuinely this time. “I'll try.”
She hugged him, fierce and brief, then turned to Kara, who had come to say goodbye.
“Take care of him,” she said to Kara, not as an order but as a trust. “He is good at taking care of other people. He is bad at letting people take care of him.”
Kara nodded. “I'll.”
After she left, Kara and Adrian stood in the lobby.
“I'm sorry I misunderstood,” Kara said again.
“You didn't do anything wrong,” Adrian said. “Anyone would.”
“I felt—” Kara stopped, looking at the magnet on his fridge in her mind, at the way she had fixed both their collars. “I felt jealous. And then I felt stupid for feeling jealous.”
Adrian looked at her.
“It is normal to feel that,” he said quietly. “When you see someone important to someone you care about and you don't know where you stand.”
Kara looked up at him. “Where do I stand?”
He considered.
“With me,” he said. “As someone I trust. As someone she was glad to meet because I talk about you.”
Kara felt the tightness in her chest ease, not completely, but enough to breathe.
“Good,” she said. “Because I talk about you too. To Alex. She tells me I should be honest.”
“Alex is wise.”
“She also says I shouldn't keep telling people who I'm.” Kara smiled a little. “I'm working on that.”
Outside, a siren began to rise. Kara listened, then checked her phone.
“I'll call it in,” she said.
Adrian nodded. “I'll cover the desk if Cat asks where you went.”
She left the lobby, cape hidden beneath her coat, and flew toward the sound.
Behind her, Adrian picked up she's mug she had left as a gift, placed it on his desk and went back to work.
At CatCo, Cat said, "Keira, where is my coffee?" and Kara brought it with both hands and didn't spill, back in her own coat.
Winn came over and put bagel on her desk without comment. James left contact sheet face-down of workers safe with families.
Adam Foster came by with coffee for everyone because he heard what happened and wanted to make sure Kara was okay. He was friends, not date, because Kara had told him clearly months ago that she had boyfriend, and he had listened. He stayed ten minutes and left.
Adrian reached for his water glass. He drank. He put it down. The glass was ordinary.
That evening, Kara went to the DEO.

Alex was at her desk with the guard log open. The paper pages were stacked beside the monitor, each one handwritten, each one checked.

Kara sat across from her.

"How many nights?" she asked.

"Twelve," Alex said. "Twelve nights with a guard walking the corridor. Twelve nights with the same pattern. The same time. The same rounds."

Kara looked at the pages. "And nothing on the video."

"Nothing," Alex said. "The video shows nothing. The paper shows the person. That's why we switched."

"And you found a name."

"A name," Alex said. "Not a reason yet."

Kara leaned back in the chair. "What do you think the reason is?"

Alex was quiet for a moment. "I think someone is using the DEO to get to Lord," she said. "I think they're walking through the building, doing their job, and feeding information to someone who's building a story. And I think they've been doing it for a while."

"And we don't know who."

"Not yet," Alex said. "But we're getting closer. The paper doesn't lie. The paper shows who was where and when. And someone is making a mistake — they're using the ghost code on nights when they shouldn't."

Kara looked at the stack of paper. "And when we find them?"

"We wait," Alex said. "We hold the pattern. We don't confront them. We wait for Lord to make his move. And when he does, we use the whole pattern."

"And then?"

"And then we stop him."

Kara looked at her sister. Alex was careful. She was methodical. She was the person who had taught Kara to control her powers, to hold her strength, to wait.

"I'm tired," Kara said.

"I know."

"I'm tired of waiting."

"I know that too." Alex closed the guard log. "But the waiting is the work. The waiting is the part that matters. Because when we move, we move with everything. And we move together."

Kara nodded. She stood up.

"I'll go home," she said.

"Be careful on the way."

"I always am."

"I know. That's what worries me."

Kara left the DEO and went to her apartment. The city went on outside the window. The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved.

She sat on the sofa. The television was off. The city lights moved across the ceiling.

She thought about the pattern. She thought about the paper. She thought about the person walking the corridor who was feeding information to Lord.

She thought about the day she would stop waiting.

Adrian came home later. He sat at the kitchen table. His hands were on the surface. He looked at her across the space.

"Are you okay?" he asked.

"I'm holding," she said. "I'm waiting. And I'm tired."

"I know."

"Alex says to wait for Lord to make his move. And when he does, we use the whole pattern."

"And you'll use it?"

"I'll use it," she said. "All of it. With Alex and Winn and you. Together. And we'll stop him."

Adrian nodded. He reached across the table and put his hand on her wrist. The band was beneath his cuff. He was human-level. He was ordinary.

"Do you think we'll always stay together?" she asked.

"If you want," he said. "I want if you want."

"I want," she said.

She closed her eyes. The city went on outside. The night went on.
