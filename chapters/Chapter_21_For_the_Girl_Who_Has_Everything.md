# Chapter 21: For the Girl Who Has Everything

*Episode: S01E13 - For the Girl Who Has Everything*

The morning started with a box.

A box sat on Kara's desk when she arrived at CatCo. It was a medium-sized cardboard box with a shipping label on it. The label was addressed to her, at CatCo, through the building's mail system.

She picked it up. It was light. It didn't rattle. It didn't move.

She set it on the corner of her desk and looked at it.

---

"Another box," Adrian said from across the bullpen. He didn't look up from his work.

"Another box," Kara said.

"The last box had a yo-yo in it."

"I know."

"This box doesn't look like the last box. It is a different size. It is a different label. It came through the mail, not through a courier."

"It came through the building's mail system. That means it went through the front desk. That means somebody at the front desk signed for it. That means somebody at the front desk knows it's here."

"And somebody at the front desk knows it's here and doesn't know what's in it. Because if they knew what was in it, they wouldn't have signed for it."

Kara looked at the box. She didn't open it.

"Cat said if another box comes, leave it at the front desk. Don't touch it. Don't carry it. Don't open it. Leave it where it's and tell her."

"You aren't going to leave it at the front desk."

"It is already at my desk. It is already in my hands. I didn't carry it from the front desk. It was already here when I arrived."

"Then put it back. Put it on the front desk. Don't open it. Tell Cat."

"I'll tell Cat. But I want to know what's in it."

"You want to know what's in it because you're curious. And curiosity is what Lord counts on. He sends you a box. He waits for you to open it. He waits for you to react. He learns your pattern from how you react."

"The last box had a yo-yo. I picked it up. I carried it to Cat. I watched the drop box for three hours. He learned my pattern from that."

"And now he's sent another box. And he's waiting to see what you do with it. If you open it, he learns that you open boxes. If you don't open it, he learns that you don't open boxes. Either way, he learns something. And you don't want him to learn something."

"Then what do I do?"

"You put it back. You put it on the front desk. You tell Cat. You do nothing else."

Kara picked up the box. She carried it to the front desk and set it on the counter.

The receptionist looked at it. "Is this yours?"

"It came to my desk. I'm putting it back here. Don't open it. Don't touch it. Just leave it."

"Who sent it?"

"I don't know. That is the point."

The receptionist looked at the box. "It is just a box."

"It is just a box. And I don't want it opened. So leave it alone."

Kara went back to her desk and called Cat.

"Another box," she said.

"I know," Cat said. "The front desk called me. They said there was a box on your desk. I told them to leave it."

"I put it back on the front desk."

"Good. Don't open it. Don't carry it. Don't touch it again."

"What is in it?"

"I don't know. And I don't want to know. Because if I know, and if Lord knows I know, then the box isn't a message anymore. It is a trap."

"The last box was a message."

"The last box was a message. This box is a message too. But it's also a test. Lord is testing whether you've changed your pattern. He is sending a box to see if you open it. If you do, he knows you haven't changed. If you don't, he knows you've."

"I didn't open it."

"Good. You didn't open it. You put it back. You told me. You did nothing else. He learned that you didn't open it. And now he knows you've changed your pattern. And he'll change his."

"What will he change?"

"He will send something else. He will send a message that doesn't look like a box. He will send a message that you can't put back on the front desk. He will send a message that you can't ignore. And when he does, you'll have to decide what to do with it."

"And what do I do with it?"

"You do nothing. You let it be. You let it sit. You let it exist. You don't react. You don't pick it up. You don't carry it. You don't watch anything. You do nothing. Let him send the message and get nothing back. Let him wonder what you're doing."

"And if the message is something I can't ignore?"

"Then you tell me. And we decide together what to do. You don't decide alone. You don't react alone. You tell me, and we decide."

"I'll tell you."

"Good. And you don't open the box on the front desk."

"I won't open the box on the front desk."

---

The next day, the box was still on the front desk.

The receptionist hadn't opened it. The mail system hadn't moved it. It sat on the counter, untouched, unopened, unclaimed.

Kara walked past it in the morning and didn't look at it. She went to her desk and started working.

At noon, she went to the front desk to get coffee from the machine in the break room. The box was still on the counter.

"It is still here," the receptionist said.

"It is still here," Kara said. "Leave it."

"I'm not touching it. I'm not looking at it. I'm not thinking about it."

"Good. That is what you should do."

Kara went back to her desk.

---

The day after that, the box was gone.

The receptionist told her in the morning. "The box isn't on the counter anymore. It was there at eight. It wasn't there at nine. I don't know who took it. I was in the break room. The lobby was empty. Anyone could have walked in and taken it."

"Anyone could have walked in and taken it," Kara said.

"Anyone. The door was open. The lobby was empty. The receptionist was in the break room. Anyone could have come in, walked to the counter, picked up the box, and walked out."

"And nobody saw who did it."

"Nobody saw who did it. I was in the break room. The lobby was empty. The cameras were green. But you told me not to look at the cameras. And I didn't. So I don't know who took it."

Kara stood at the front desk. "The box is gone."

"The box is gone."

"And we don't know who took it."

"We don't know who took it. But we know who sent it. And we know the person who sent it also sent the person who took it. Or the person who sent it took it themselves. Or the person who sent it told someone to take it. The box was a test. Lord sent the box. Lord watched whether Kara would open it. Kara didn't open it. So Lord had the box taken. He didn't need it anymore. He got what he wanted from the test."

"He learned that I didn't open it."

"He learned that you didn't open it. And he learned that you put it back on the front desk. And he learned that you told Cat. And he learned that you did nothing else. He learned your pattern. And now he knows you've changed it. And he'll change his."

"What will he send next?"

"I don't know. But I know this. He will send something that you can't ignore. He will send something that you can't put back. He will send something that you can't walk away from. And when he does, you'll have to decide what to do. And you'll decide with me, not alone."

"I'll decide with you."

"Good. And you'll do nothing until then. You will go to work. You will do your job. You will go home. You will live your life. And you'll wait for the next message."

"And when it comes, I'll tell you."

"You will tell me. And we'll decide together what to do."

---

The next message came three days later.

It wasn't a box. It wasn't a yo-yo. It wasn't a courier delivery.

It was a person.

Kara was at her desk when a man walked into the bullpen. He was ordinary. He was unremarkable. He was the kind of man you wouldn't notice if you didn't look twice.

He walked to her desk and stood in front of it.

"Kara Danvers," he said.

"Yes," Kara said. She didn't stand up. She stayed in her chair.

"I've a message for you," he said.

"What message?"

"A message from Lord."

Kara looked at the man. He was ordinary. He was calm. He was the kind of man who delivered messages without worrying about what the message was.

"What is the message?"

"Lord says: the game is changing. He says you've changed your pattern. He says he respects that. He says he wants to offer you a new pattern. He says he's something you want. He says if you want it, you come to him. He says if you don't, he'll take it anyway. He says the choice is yours. But he says it isn't really a choice."

Kara sat in her chair. She didn't say anything.

The man waited. He stood there, calm, ordinary, unremarkable.

"Is that the whole message?" Kara asked.

"That is the whole message."

"Tell Lord I heard it."

The man nodded. "He said you would say that. He said you wouldn't say anything else. He said you would sit there and think about it. He said you wouldn't come to him today. He said you wouldn't come to him tomorrow. He said you would think about it for a while. And then he said you would come."

"He is wrong."

The man smiled. It wasn't a warm smile. "He said you would say that too."

The man turned and walked out of the bullpen. He walked through the lobby and went out the door.

Kara sat at her desk. Her hands were on the edge of the desk.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was typing. He didn't look up when the man left.

"You aren't going to him," he said.

"I'm not going to him."

"Good. Because if you go to him, you give him what he wants. He wants you to come to him. He wants you to ask for what he's. He wants you to make the choice he says is yours but isn't. If you go, you give him the choice. And he'll take it."

"I'm not going."

"Good. And you aren't going to react. You aren't going to go to Cat. You aren't going to go to the DEO. You aren't going to do anything. You are going to sit at your desk and do your work. And you're going to wait."

"Wait for what?"

"Wait for the next message. He will send one. He is testing you. He sent a man to your desk. He will send something else. He will keep sending messages until you react. And when you don't react, he'll change the message. And when he changes the message, he'll make a mistake. And when he makes a mistake, we'll see it."

"And then?"

"And then we use it. We use the pattern. We use the paper. We use the names. We use the marks and the prints and the places. We use all of it. And we stop him."

Kara looked at Adrian. "He said he's something I want."

"He said that because he knows what you want. He knows you want to stop him. He knows you want to protect the city. He knows you want to keep the people you care about safe. He is offering you what you want in exchange for what he wants. And what he wants is you, in his story, making his choices, doing his work."

"I won't do his work."

"I know. And that's why he'll keep sending messages. And that's why he'll make a mistake. Because men like Lord don't stop. They keep pushing. They keep sending. They keep testing. And when they think they've you, they move. And when they move, they leave a trail. And the trail leads to them."

Kara opened her email. She started working.

She didn't go to Cat. She didn't go to the DEO. She didn't do anything. She sat at her desk and did her work.

And she waited.

---

That evening, Kara went back to her apartment.

Non was at the kitchen table. He looked up when she came in.

"Lord sent a man to your desk," he said.

"Lord sent a man to my desk," Kara said. She sat down across from him.

"What did the man say?"

"He said Lord has something I want. He said the choice is mine. He said it isn't really a choice."

"It isn't a choice. Lord doesn't offer choices. He offers tests. He tests whether you'll come to him. He tests whether you'll ask for what he's. He tests whether you'll make the move he wants you to make. And when you don't, he changes the test."

"I didn't go to him."

"Good. You didn't go to him. You sat at your desk and did your work. And you waited. And that's the right thing to do. Because when you don't react, he doesn't know what to do. He expected you to react. He expected you to go to Cat. He expected you to go to the DEO. He expected you to do something. And you did nothing. And he's nothing to work with."

"He will send another message."

"He will send another message. And you'll do the same thing. You will do nothing. You will wait. You will hold the pattern. You will hold the paper. And you'll wait for him to make his move."

"And when he makes his move?"

"When he makes his move, he'll leave a trail. He will go to a place. He will meet a person. He will do a thing. And he'll do it in the open. Because men like Lord don't hide. They act in front of people. They act in front of cameras. They act because they want everyone to see. And when they act, we'll see it. And we'll use it."

Kara leaned back in her chair. "He said he's something I want."

"He has what you want. He has the pattern. He has the names. He has the marks. He has the prints. He has the pieces of the story. He is offering to give you the pieces if you come to him and give him what he wants. But he doesn't have all the pieces. He doesn't have the paper. He doesn't have the names we wrote down by hand. He doesn't have the marks we found with our eyes. He has some of the pieces. And he's offering them to you. And if you go to him, he'll give you the pieces he's and take the pieces you've. And you'll have less than you've now."

"So I don't go."

"You don't go. You hold what you've. You hold the paper. You hold the pattern. You hold the names. And you wait. And when he makes his move, you use everything you've. And you stop him."

Kara looked at Non. The lamp was on. The city lights were on outside the window. The apartment was quiet.

"I'm not going to him," she said.

"I know. And that's why he'll make a mistake. Because men like Lord don't expect people to hold. They expect people to move. They expect people to react. They expect people to come. When you don't, they're confused. And when they're confused, they act. And when they act, they leave a trail."

"And we'll be watching."

"And you'll be watching. With your eyes. With the paper. With the names. With the pattern. And you'll be ready."

Kara went to the sofa and sat down.

The television was off. The city lights moved across the ceiling.

She sat there. She thought about the man at her desk. She thought about the message. She thought about Lord, waiting, testing, expecting her to come.

She didn't know when he would make his move. She didn't know what it would look like. She didn't know what he would do.

She knew one thing. She wasn't going to him. She was holding the pattern. She was holding the paper. She was holding the names.

And she would be ready.

She sat there. The night went on. The city went on outside the window.

---

The next morning, Kara arrived at CatCo at eight.

She walked through the lobby. The box was gone from the front desk. The receptionist was reading her book. The lobby was quiet.

She went to her desk and sat down.

She opened her email. She started working.

She didn't think about the box. She didn't think about the man. She didn't think about the message. She thought about her work. She thought about the files on her screen. She thought about the emails she needed to answer.

She did her work.

At ten, Cat called her into the office.

"The man came to your desk," Cat said.

"He did," Kara said.

"You didn't react."

"I didn't react. I sat at my desk and did my work."

"Good. That is what you should have done."

"He said Lord has something I want."

"He said that because Lord knows what you want. And Lord is offering to give it to you if you go to him. And Lord isn't actually offering you anything. He is testing whether you'll go. And you didn't go. And that's the right answer."

"What do I do now?"

"You do nothing. You do your work. You go home. You live your life. You wait for the next message. And when it comes, you tell me. And we decide together what to do."

"And if the next message is something I can't ignore?"

"Then you tell me. And we decide together. You don't decide alone. You don't react alone. You tell me, and we decide."

"I'll tell you."

"Good. And you don't go to Lord. You don't go to the man. You don't go anywhere. You stay here. You do your work. You wait."

Kara left Cat's office and went back to her desk.

---

The next day, Kara went to the DEO.

She went to Alex's office. Alex was at her desk with her notebook open. The notebook had the names written in it.

"I'm going through the paper again," Alex said. "I'm going through the names. I'm going through the times. I'm looking for a pattern in the pattern. I'm looking for who's behind Marcus."

"What does the paper show?"

"The paper shows Marcus was in the corridor the night Astra left. The paper shows Marcus is the only person who was in the corridor. The paper shows Marcus is the person who opened the door."

"And who's behind Marcus?"

"The paper doesn't show who's behind Marcus. The paper shows the person. It doesn't show the person behind the person. We need to find who's behind Marcus."

"And we find them by watching Marcus."

"We find them by watching Marcus. Without the cameras. With our eyes. We watch who Marcus talks to. We watch who Marcus meets. We watch where Marcus goes. We watch what Marcus does. And we write it on paper."

"And Marcus doesn't know we're watching."

"Marcus doesn't know we're watching. Because we aren't using the cameras. Because we aren't using the systems. Because we're watching with our eyes. And Marcus isn't looking for eyes. Marcus is looking for cameras. And there're no cameras."

Kara sat down across from Alex. "Marcus is a guard. He walks the corridor. He does his job. And he works for Lord."

"Marcus works for Lord. Or Marcus was paid by Lord. Or Marcus was told by someone who works for Lord. The paper shows Marcus. It doesn't show who told Marcus to open the door."

"And we need to find who told Marcus."

"We need to find who told Marcus. And we need to find them without Marcus knowing we're looking. If Marcus knows, he'll run. He will go to Lord. He will tell Lord what we've. And Lord will change everything."

"So we watch Marcus quietly. We watch without the cameras. We watch with our eyes. We watch who he talks to. We watch who he meets. We watch where he goes."

"And we write it on paper."

"And we wait."

Alex closed the notebook. "And you? How are you holding up?"

"I'm holding. I'm doing my work. I'm waiting for the next message."

"And Non?"

"Non is at my apartment. He is staying there. He isn't going to the DEO. He isn't going to Lord. He is staying there and waiting."

"Do you trust him?"

"I don't trust him. I watch him the same way I'm watching Marcus. I watch him with my eyes. And if he does something that isn't right, I'll see it."

"And if he doesn't?"

"And if he doesn't, I'll see that too. And I'll know he's telling the truth."

Alex nodded. "Keep him at your apartment. Don't let him leave. Don't let him do anything. I'll come back tomorrow. We will talk again."

"I'll keep him there."

Kara left Alex's office and walked down the corridor.

She stopped at the holding area. She looked through the glass at the empty cell. The bed was empty. The floor was clean. The door was open. The panel was open.

Marcus had walked this corridor. Marcus had gone to the panel. Marcus had rewired the door. Marcus had opened the cell. And Astra had walked out.

And Marcus was still here. Still walking the corridor. Still doing his job. Still not knowing that somebody had written his name on paper.

Kara walked past the holding area and went to the lobby.

She stood by the door and looked at the city outside.

The city was the same. The traffic moved in the same direction. The people walked the same streets. The buildings stood the same way.

And somewhere in this city, Astra was holding the dagger and thinking about what she would do with it. And somewhere in this building, Marcus was counting the days until he was free. And somewhere in this city, Lord was choosing which piece to move next.

She stood by the door and watched the city.

She didn't go to Marcus. She didn't go to Astra. She didn't go to Lord. She held the pattern. She held the paper. She held the names.

And she waited.

The city went on outside the window.

And she watched.
