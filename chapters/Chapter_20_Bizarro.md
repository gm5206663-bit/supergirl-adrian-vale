# Chapter 20: Bizarro

*Episode: S01E12 - Bizarro*

The truck went through the guardrail at 1:47 p.m.

The driver had been asleep for two seconds after twelve hours behind the wheel. The truck dropped through the air toward the river below. The cab was dark. The wheels were still turning. The driver woke up somewhere in the fall and started screaming.

---

Kara heard the crash from above.

She was flying over the river on her way back from a DEO run when the sound came up through the air. It was a metallic crunch, followed by the long, low sound of metal tearing and water taking over.

She turned. She saw the truck in the water below. The cab was half submerged. The wheels were still turning. The driver was inside, banging on the window.

She dropped.

She reached the cab in seconds. She put her hands on the door and pulled. The door was jammed. The frame had bent. She put her shoulder against it and pushed. The metal gave.

The driver was inside, hanging onto the steering wheel, water rising around his knees.

"Get out," Kara said.

"I can't," he said. "My leg is stuck."

She reached in and grabbed the steering column. She pulled. The metal bent. The driver's leg came free.

She grabbed him under the arms and pulled him out. She carried him to the shore and set him down on the grass.

"Are you okay?" she asked.

He was shaking. He looked at her. He looked at the truck in the water. He looked at her again.

"You—" he said. "You—"

"You are okay," Kara said. "Stay here. Help is coming."

She flew back to the truck. She reached into the water and lifted the cab. She turned it so the driver wouldn't be trapped underneath when the divers arrived. She set it on the shore.

Then she flew up. She went high above the river and looked at the road.

The guardrail was gone. The truck had gone through it. The guardrail had been there for years. It had been there to stop trucks from going through it.

Someone had taken the guardrail out.

---

She reported the crash to the DEO. She landed at the DEO and went to Alex's office.

"The truck that went into the river," she said. "The guardrail was taken out. Someone removed it before the truck got there."

Alex looked up from her desk. "Are you sure?"

"I saw the guardrail. It was gone. The truck went through the space where it should have been."

"When did you see it?"

"After the crash. I flew back to the scene. The guardrail wasn't there. The posts were still in the ground, but the rail was gone. Someone had removed it."

"How long before the crash?"

"I don't know. But it'd before. The truck went through an empty space. It didn't break the guardrail. It went through where the guardrail wasn't."

Alex stood up. "I'll send a team to the scene. They will look at the posts. They will look at the ground. They will look for how the rail was removed and when."

"And who removed it."

"And who removed it."

---

The next morning, Kara went to CatCo.

She walked through the lobby and went to her desk. Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was typing. He didn't look up when she sat down.

"There was a truck crash yesterday," she said.

"I saw it on the news," Adrian said.

"It wasn't an accident. The guardrail was removed before the truck got there."

Adrian stopped typing. He looked at her. "How do you know?"

"I was there. I flew to the scene. The guardrail wasn't there. The posts were still in the ground. The rail was gone."

"And the driver?"

"He survived. I pulled him out. He is okay."

"And the rail?"

"Someone took it out. Someone who wanted a truck to go through that space."

Adrian leaned back in his chair. "A truck going into a river is a story. A truck going into a river because someone removed the guardrail is a different story. The first story is an accident. The second story is an event."

"And Lord manufactures events."

"Lord manufactures events. He doesn't wait for them. He makes them. He removed the guardrail. He waited for a truck to come. He let the truck go through. And then he had a story."

Kara opened her email. "What is the story?"

"The story is a dangerous road. The story is a broken guardrail. The story is a community that needs someone to fix it. The story is a man who will fix it. The story is a city that needs a hero."

"Lord is going to fix the road."

"Lord is going to fix the road. He is going to stand in front of cameras and say he'll fix the road. He is going to promise to replace the guardrail. He is going to promise to make the road safe. And the city will say thank you. And the city will ask for more. And he'll give more. And he'll build."

Kara looked at Adrian. "He set the crash up."

"He set the crash up. He removed the guardrail. He waited for the right truck. He let it go. And then he had a story to tell."

"What if the driver had died?"

Adrian was quiet for a moment. "Then the story would have been bigger."

Kara felt her hands tighten on the desk. "He is using people as props."

"He is using people as props. He doesn't care about the driver. He cares about the story. The driver is a character in the story. The driver is there to make the story work. If the driver dies, the story is bigger. If the driver survives, the story is still big enough."

"That isn't okay."

"It isn't okay. But it's what Lord does. He doesn't build things that are okay. He builds things that get him what he wants. And what he wants is attention, fear, and the city asking for him."

Kara stood up. "I need to go to the DEO."

"Go to the DEO. Tell Alex what you know. Tell her the guardrail was removed. Tell her Lord did it. Tell her to look for the person who removed it."

"She is already looking."

"Good. Let her look. And you look too. You look without the cameras. You look with your eyes. You look at the posts. You look at the ground. You look at the marks on the posts. Someone removed that rail with a tool. There will be marks. There will be a pattern. There will be a person who was there."

"I'll look."

Kara walked out of the bullpen and went to the elevator.

---

She drove to the river. She walked along the shore to the road. She stood at the edge where the guardrail should have been.

The posts were still in the ground. Two of them. The rail was gone. She crouched and looked at the posts.

The top of the first post had a mark on it. A scratch. A straight line across the top. The mark was fresh. The paint had been scraped away. The metal underneath was bright.

She looked at the second post. The same mark. The same scratch. The same fresh metal.

The rail had been lifted off the posts. It hadn't been cut. It hadn't been torn. It had been lifted off. Someone had used a tool to pry it up, or someone had used hands strong enough to lift it.

She looked at the ground around the posts. The dirt was disturbed. There were footprints in it. Several of them. They came from the road and went to the posts and went back.

She crouched and looked at the prints.

They were ordinary boots. Size eleven or twelve. The tread pattern was common. The same as the prints at Lord Technologies. The same as the boots in the footage from the fence-line camera.

She stood up. She took out her phone and took pictures. She took pictures of the posts, the marks, the prints, the ground.

She looked down the road. The road went along the river and turned at the bend. The bend was where the truck had gone through.

She walked to the bend. She looked at the road surface. She looked at the edge. She looked at the ground beyond the edge.

There was a scuff mark on the road surface. The truck had hit the edge before it went through. The scuff was long. It was fresh.

She looked at the ground beyond the edge. There were more prints. They went toward the river and stopped at the water's edge.

She crouched and looked at them.

The prints were the same boots. Size eleven or twelve. Common tread. They went to the water's edge and stopped. Then they went back to the road.

Someone had stood at the edge of the road, looked at the river, and gone back.

Someone who had removed the guardrail had stood at the edge and looked at the river and gone back.

She took more pictures. She walked back to her car and drove to the DEO.

---

She went to Alex's office.

"I went to the scene," she said. "I looked at the posts. I looked at the ground. I found prints. The same boots as at Lord Technologies. The same size. The same tread."

Alex looked at the pictures on Kara's phone. "The same boots. The same person. Or the same kind of person. Or someone who wears the same kind of boots."

"The same kind of boots. The kind you can buy at any store. The kind anybody can wear. But the pattern is the same. The prints at Lord Technologies, the prints at the convention, the prints at the river. All the same kind of boot. All the same kind of person."

"Or all the same kind of employer. Lord gives his people the same boots. Or he tells them to buy a specific kind. Or he doesn't care what boots they wear because the boots aren't the point. The boots are just boots. The point is the person."

"The person who removed the guardrail is the same person who set the fire. Or the same kind of person. Or the same employer."

"Lord."

"Lord. Or someone in Lord's company. Someone who does the work that Lord doesn't want to do himself. Someone who removes guardrails and sets fires and walks into conventions and stands in DEO lobbies. Someone who does the dirty work."

Alex leaned back. "And we can't trace the boots. They're ordinary. They're common. They can be bought anywhere."

"We can't trace the boots. But we can trace the person. We can watch the places where Lord's people go. We can watch the river. We can watch the convention center. We can watch the DEO. We can watch the warehouse. We can watch all of it and write it down on paper."

"Paper."

"Paper. The paper can't be changed. The paper can't be erased. The paper shows the pattern."

"And what does the pattern show?"

"The pattern shows the same person, or the same kind of person, or the same employer, going to the same kind of places and doing the same kind of work. And the pattern leads to Lord."

Alex nodded. "And when we've the pattern, we use it."

"We hold it. We don't go to Lord. We don't go to the person. We hold the pattern and we wait. And when Lord makes his move, we use it."

"We hold it."

"We hold it. And we watch."

---

That evening, Kara went back to her apartment.

Non was at the kitchen table. He looked up when she came in.

"You found something," he said.

"I found boots," Kara said. "The same kind of boots at the river, at Lord Technologies, at the convention. Ordinary boots. Common. The kind anybody can buy."

"The boots aren't the point. The person is the point. The person who wears the boots is the person who does the work. And the person who does the work is the person who works for Lord."

"And we can't trace the boots."

"You can't trace the boots. But you can trace the pattern. The person goes to the same places. The person does the same work. The person leaves the same marks. You watch the places. You watch the marks. You watch the pattern. And you find the person."

"We're writing it on paper."

"Good. Paper is the tool that the person can't control. The person controls the systems. The person controls the cameras. The person controls the logs. The person can't control a piece of paper in your hand."

Kara sat down across from Non. "The truck crash. The guardrail. The prints. It is the same pattern as the fire and the convention and the yo-yos."

"It is the same pattern. Lord builds events. He doesn't wait for them. He makes them. He removes a guardrail. He waits for a truck. He has a story. He sets a fire. He has a story. He sends a yo-yo. He has a story. He walks a man into a DEO lobby. He has a story. Every event is a story. Every story is a way to get what he wants."

"And what does he want?"

"He wants the city to ask for him. He wants the city to be afraid. He wants the city to say: we need a man like Lord to fix this. And he'll fix it. And the city will say thank you. And he'll give more. And the city will ask for more. And he'll build."

"And we're holding the pattern."

"You are holding the pattern. You are writing it on paper. You are watching without the cameras. You are finding the marks and the prints and the places. And when the moment comes, you'll use it."

"When is the moment?"

"When Lord makes his move. He won't wait forever. He has been building the story. He has been building the pressure. He has been building the attention. He will act when he's ready. And when he acts, you'll be ready."

Kara leaned back in her chair. "I don't know if I can stop him."

"You can stop him. Not alone. Not with your power alone. You stop him with the pattern. You stop him with the people you trust. You stop him with the paper in your hand. You stop him by knowing what he did and how he did it and who helped him. And you stop him by using all of it at the right moment."

"And you're helping."

"I'm helping. I'm sitting at your kitchen table telling you what I know. I'm not fighting you. I'm not running. I'm not working for Lord. I'm working with you. Because your mother asked me to watch over you. And because I want to stop Lord before he finishes building what he's building."

Kara looked at Non. The lamp was on. The city lights were on outside the window. The apartment was quiet.

"I still don't trust you," she said.

"I know. And that's fine. You don't need to trust me. You need to watch me the way you're watching the leak. You need to watch me with your eyes. And if I do something that isn't right, you'll see it. And if I don't, you'll see that too."

Kara stood up. "I'm going to keep writing the pattern. I'm going to keep watching. And I'm going to wait for Lord to make his move."

"I'll stay here," Non said. "I'll wait with you."

Kara went to the sofa and sat down.

The television was off. The city lights moved across the ceiling.

She sat there. She thought about the boots and the prints and the guardrail and the truck in the river. She thought about the fire and the convention and the yo-yos and the man in the DEO lobby. She thought about the pattern on paper.

She thought about Lord, building his stories, building his events, building his attention, waiting for the right moment.

And she thought about the moment coming.

She didn't know when it would come. She didn't know what it would look like. She didn't know what Lord would do.

She knew one thing. She had the pattern. She had the paper. She had the people. And she would be ready.

She sat there. The night went on. The city went on outside the window.

---

The next morning, Kara arrived at CatCo at eight.

She went to her desk and looked at the bullpen. Winn was at his desk. James was at his desk. The receptionist was at the front desk. Adrian was at his desk.

Everything looked normal. Everything was normal. And somewhere in the city, someone was wearing the same boots as the person who removed the guardrail.

She sat down and opened her email.

At nine, Cat called her into the office.

"Lord is on the news again," Cat said. She had a newspaper open on her desk. "He is talking about the truck crash. He is calling it a tragedy. He is calling it a danger to the community. He is promising to fix the road."

"He removed the guardrail," Kara said.

"I know he removed the guardrail. You told me. Alex told me. We all know. But we don't have proof. We have prints. We have marks. We have a pattern. We don't have proof that Lord ordered the guardrail to be removed."

"We have the pattern. The same boots at the river, at Lord Technologies, at the convention. The same kind of person doing the same kind of work for the same employer."

"A pattern isn't proof. A pattern is a place to start. Proof is a name. Proof is a recording. Proof is something you can put in front of a person and say: this is what you did."

"We don't have a name."

"We don't have a name. We have the boots. We have the prints. We have the marks. We have the pattern. And we've the paper. And the paper will lead us to the name."

"How?"

"By watching. By writing things down. By putting the pieces together. By finding the person who wears those boots and asking them who they work for. And by doing it without Lord knowing we're doing it."

Kara nodded. "I'm already watching."

"Watch quietly. Watch without the cameras. Watch with your eyes. And when you find the name, you bring it to me. Before you do anything. Before you go anywhere. You bring the name to me."

"I'll."

"And don't go to Lord. Don't confront anyone. Don't make a move. You watch. You write. You wait. And when you've the name, you bring it to me."

Kara left Cat's office.

---

She went to the DEO.

She went to the tech room. Winn was at his desk with his notebook open. He had written three pages of names and times.

"I've been going through the guard logs by hand," he said. "I went to the guard station. I talked to the guards. I wrote down who was on duty, who walked the corridor, who was in the tech room, and who came in and went out. I've the names. I've the times."

"And what does the paper show?"

"It shows that the night Astra left, the night guard was Dana. The corridor guard was Marcus. They were both on duty from eleven to seven. Dana was in the security room the whole time. Marcus walked the corridors. He came back at six."

"And the tech room?"

"The tech room was empty. Nobody was in the tech room from eleven to seven. The tech room is locked. Only I, Hank, and Alex have keys. None of us were in the tech room that night."

"Then who opened the door?"

"The door was rewired. The panel was open. The wires were bypassed. The door was opened from the panel, not from the keypad. Someone went to the panel, opened it, and rewired the mechanism. That person didn't need a code. They didn't need a key. They needed access to the corridor and the panel."

"Who has access to the corridor and the panel?"

"The guards. The maintenance crew. The tech team. Anyone who walks the corridor can go to the panel. The panel isn't locked. Anyone in the corridor can open it."

"So the person who opened the door is someone who was in the corridor that night."

"That is what the paper shows. Marcus was in the corridor. He walked the corridors from eleven to six. He could have gone to the panel. He could have rewired the door. He could have opened the cell."

"And Astra walked out."

"And Astra walked out. And Marcus was in the corridor. And Marcus came back at six and said everything was fine. And the cameras were green the whole time. And the logs were clean."

"And Marcus is the leak."

"Marcus is the leak. Or Marcus is the person who let Astra out. Or Marcus is the person who rewired the door. The paper shows that Marcus was in the corridor. The paper shows that nobody else was. The paper shows that Marcus is the only person who could have done it."

Kara stood by Winn's desk. "Marcus."

"Marcus."

"And Marcus works for Lord."

"Marcus works for Lord. Or Marcus was paid by Lord. Or Marcus was told by someone who works for Lord. The paper shows that Marcus was in the corridor. The paper doesn't show who told Marcus to open the door. The paper shows the person. It doesn't show the person behind the person."

"Then we need to find who's behind Marcus."

"We need to find who's behind Marcus. And we need to find them without Marcus knowing we're looking. If Marcus knows we're looking, he'll run. He will go to Lord. He will tell Lord what we've. And Lord will change everything."

"We watch Marcus. We watch without the cameras. We watch with our eyes. We watch who he talks to. We watch who he meets. We watch where he goes. We watch what he does."

"We watch Marcus. And we write it on paper."

"And we wait."

Kara went to Alex's office.

"Marcus," she said. "The corridor guard. He was in the corridor the night Astra left. He is the only person who was in the corridor. He is the person who opened the door."

Alex looked up from her desk. "How do you know?"

"Winn went through the guard logs by hand. He wrote the names on paper. The paper shows that Marcus was in the corridor. The paper shows that nobody else was. The paper shows that Marcus is the leak."

"And Marcus works for Lord."

"Marcus works for Lord. Or Marcus was paid by Lord. Or Marcus was told by someone who works for Lord. We don't know who's behind Marcus. But we know Marcus is the one who opened the door."

"And we don't go to Marcus."

"We don't go to Marcus. We watch Marcus. We watch who he talks to. We watch who he meets. We watch where he goes. We watch what he does. We watch without the cameras. We watch with our eyes. And we write it on paper."

"And we wait."

"And we wait. We hold the pattern. We hold the paper. We hold Marcus. And we wait for Lord to make his move. And when he does, we use it."

Alex leaned back. "And Non?"

"Non is at my apartment. He is staying there. He isn't going to the DEO. He isn't going to Lord. He is staying there and waiting."

"Do you trust him?"

"I don't trust him. I watch him the same way I'm watching Marcus. I watch him with my eyes. And if he does something that isn't right, I'll see it."

"And if he doesn't?"

"And if he doesn't, I'll see that too. And I'll know he's telling the truth."

Alex nodded. "Keep him at your apartment. Don't let him leave. Don't let him do anything. I'll come back tomorrow. We will talk again."

"I'll keep him there."

Kara left Alex's office and walked down the corridor.

She stopped at the holding area. She looked through the glass at the empty cell. The bed was empty. The floor was clean. The door was open. The panel was open. The wires were exposed.

Marcus had walked this corridor. Marcus had gone to the panel. Marcus had rewired the door. Marcus had opened the cell. And Astra had walked out.

And Marcus was still here. Still walking the corridor. Still doing his job. Still not knowing that somebody had written his name on paper.

Kara walked past the holding area and went to the lobby.

She stood by the door and looked at the city outside.

The city was the same. The traffic moved in the same direction. The people walked the same streets. The buildings stood the same way.

And somewhere in this city, Astra was moving through the dark with a Kryptonite dagger in her hand. And somewhere in this building, Marcus was checking the corridor cameras. And somewhere in this city, Lord was reading the files he had been given, and building his story from what he found.

She stood by the door and watched the city.

She didn't go to Marcus. She didn't go to Astra. She didn't go to Lord. She held the pattern. She held the paper. She held the names.

And she waited.

The city went on outside the window.

And she watched.

---

That evening, Kara went back to her apartment.

Non was at the kitchen table. He looked up when she came in.

"You found the leak," he said.

"We found the person who opened the door," Kara said. "A guard. Marcus. He was in the corridor the night Astra left. He is the only person who was in the corridor. He is the one who rewired the door and let Astra out."

"And Marcus works for Lord."

"Marcus works for Lord. Or he was paid by Lord. Or he was told by someone who works for Lord. We don't know who's behind Marcus. We know Marcus is the one who did it."

"And you're watching him."

"We're watching him. Without the cameras. With our eyes. And we're writing it on paper."

"Good. The paper is the tool that Marcus can't control. Marcus controls the cameras. Marcus controls the corridor. Marcus controls the door. Marcus can't control a piece of paper in your hand."

Kara sat down across from Non. "And we're waiting."

"You are waiting. You are holding the pattern. You are holding the paper. You are holding Marcus. And you're waiting for Lord to make his move."

"And when he makes his move?"

"When he makes his move, you use it. You use the pattern. You use the paper. You use Marcus. You use all of it. You don't use part of it. You use all of it."

"And then?"

"And then you stop him."

Kara leaned back in her chair. "I don't know when he'll make his move."

"You will know. Men like Lord don't hide their moves. They build the story, and then they act. They act in the open. They act in front of cameras. They act so everyone can see. You will know when it happens."

"And we'll be ready."

"And you'll be ready. You will have the pattern. You will have the paper. You will have the names. You will have the marks and the prints and the places. You will have everything. And you'll use it."

Kara looked at Non. The lamp was on. The city lights were on outside the window. The apartment was quiet.

"I'm going to keep writing the pattern," she said. "I'm going to keep watching Marcus. I'm going to keep holding the paper. And I'm going to wait."

"I'll stay here," Non said. "I'll wait with you."

Kara went to the sofa and sat down.

The television was off. The city lights moved across the ceiling.

She sat there. She thought about Marcus, walking the corridor, not knowing his name was on paper. She thought about Astra, walking the city with a dagger, not knowing someone was looking for her. She thought about Lord, building his story, not knowing someone was holding the pattern.

She thought about the paper. The names. The times. The marks. The prints. The pattern.

She didn't know when Lord would make his move. She didn't know what it would look like. She didn't know what would happen.

She knew one thing. She had the pattern. She had the paper. She had the people. And she would be ready.
