# Chapter 13: Human for a Day

*Episode: S01E07 - Human for a Day*

A small piece of Kryptonite sat in a sealed case on the table outside the cell. Green light pulsed faintly beneath the glass, humming at a frequency no human ear could catch.

The light fell across Kara's face as she looked at it from across the room.

---

She felt it before she named it. A weakness that started behind her sternum and spread outward, down her arms, into her legs. Her knees went first. She dropped to them, hands bracing on the floor, breathing hard. Her throat went raw. Her vision blurred at the edges.

Alex stood beside the table, tablet in hand, reading the vitals.

"We need to know how long you can stay near it," Alex said. "Your baseline is higher after the sun. Your cells remember, and they overcompensate. But we need the number."

Kara looked up from the floor. "Longer than last time?"

"Longer." Alex watched the readout. "After sun, your hearing sharpened. You still weaken. How long?"

Kara tried to answer. The words came out thin. She looked at the case — the small piece, not a dagger, not the weapon, just a fragment — and forced her breathing under control.

Adrian stood further back. The band rested beneath his left cuff. He was human-level again, wearing ordinary clothes, an ordinary man in an extraordinary room. He could hear the pen scratching in the bullpen two floors away, but he didn't say anything. He stayed back.

Hank watched Kara from the doorway. "You are sealed?"

Adrian nodded once. The band didn't move.

Kara pushed herself up from the floor. She stood, wobbling, then steadied. "You did something bad to a city," she said to Adrian. "You told me. Genuine responsibility. You were caught because you were exhausted. You built the device yourself because you wanted ordinary." She paused, catching her breath. "Is that why you can be near this longer? Is that why you can be near Kryptonite longer than I can?"

Adrian looked at her. "I built the device myself because I wanted ordinary. Some Daxamites murdered my parents over a mixed union. My father was from an influential Kryptonian house. My mother was from a collateral branch of the Daxamite royal family. I did something bad to a city. Genuine responsibility."

Kara stared at him from across the room, weakened but listening.

"And you chose ordinary after?" she asked.

"Yes. I wanted ordinary. Now I'm choosing when not to be ordinary."

Alex set the tablet down. "We should focus. Kara, can you stand?"

"I'm standing."

"You're swaying."

Kara stopped swaying. "I'm standing."

Hank looked from one of them to the other, then back at Kara. "That's enough for today. We have the baseline."

---

Cat's mother was still in town.

Katherine Grant had been in National City for three days. She had opinions about Cat's office decor, Cat's staffing choices, Cat's taste in restaurants, and Cat's decision to hire a cousin of a Kryptonian. She hadn't said anything that wasn't, technically, an observation. Every observation landed like a cut.

Kara found Cat in her office with a glass of water and a bottle of Advil on the desk. The glass had been knocked over. Water had run across the surface and dripped onto the carpet.

"You should clean that up," Kara said.

"I know." Cat's voice was sharper than usual. "I'll get to it."

Kara picked up the glass. It had cracked on the edge when it fell. She carried it to the trash can and came back with a towel.

Cat watched her. "Don't cut yourself."

"I won't."

Kara did cut herself. A sliver of glass sliced the tip of her finger as she pressed the towel to the wet surface. Blood welled up — a thin red line, surprising, bleeding in a way she had never bled before. Not a cut that healed in seconds. A cut that bled the way an ordinary human bleeds.

She stood there with her finger in the towel, looking at it.

Cat looked up. "When did you last have a tetanus shot? Go get a bandage. Don't bleed on the Persian rug."

Kara went to the bathroom. She pressed tissue to the wound and watched the blood spot spread through the white paper. It hurt. A small, ordinary human way of hurting. Different from taking a missile on her shoulder. Different from everything she had ever felt before.

She wrapped the finger and went back to Cat's office.

---

James found her in the garage later, punching the bag.

Her form was methodical. Left, right, left. She wasn't training. She was hitting something that wouldn't break, and she was hitting it because she needed to hit something that wouldn't break.

Sweat had darkened the front of her shirt.

"You broke your wrist?" James asked, leaning against the wall.

"Red Tornado." Kara kept punching. "I kept punching after it'd done."

James waited. He didn't interrupt.

Kara stopped. She stood with her hands on the bag, breathing hard. She looked at the car in the corner.

The totaled car from the DEO. Dented steel. Pulled and held overhead. Flight and counter-rotation. Setting it down gently instead of destroying it. She had felt the weight. She hadn't broken it. But naming that had upset her more than it should have.

"You want to hit something that won't break," James said, "or someone who won't flinch?"

Kara looked at him. "Both."

He held up his hands. "I'll take my chances."

She punched his palm once, gently, then stepped back. "I'm not going to break you."

"I know. That's not what I meant."

Adrian stood in the doorway. He was wearing ordinary clothes. The band was at his wrist. He was human-level, and he had been standing there for some time.

"You shouldn't lift cars alone," he said quietly.

"I know." Kara picked up her water bottle and drank. "I know."

---

The earthquake came in the late afternoon.

It started as a low grinding in the ground, a tremor that built into a jolt. Monitors slid off desks. Winn's chair went backward into the filing cabinet. The lights flickered. The walls groaned.

Kara was at her apartment. She had a bowl of soup in her hands when the ground shook. The bowl cracked. She dropped it and went to the window. The street below was swaying. Cars were stacking at intersections where the lights had gone out. A building two blocks over lost a chunk of its facade.

She called Alex. No answer. She called the DEO. No answer.

She flew. Her reserves weren't fully back after the Kryptonite test. Flight felt sluggish, like swimming through water instead of air, the sun-recovered energy not completely restored.

CatCo first. The building was groaning. The staff was inside.

Kara went through the lobby. The elevator shaft had buckled. The elevator car had dropped, stopped by a safety brake holding by threads. The doors were jammed.

She could hear people on the other side, yelling. One voice rose above the others, telling them to stay back from the doors.

Kara set her hands against the doors. The metal was hot, warped. She pushed — not with brute force, but with the controlled weight Alex had taught her. Absorb the momentum. Counter-rotate. Let the structure tell her what it could take.

The doors came apart.

The elevator car was half-filled. People pressed together, faces white. James was at the front, one arm braced against the wall, the other holding the hand of a woman who had been clutching her purse like a lifeline.

Kara got them out, one at a time. Her hands were steady. Her voice was calm.

When the last person was clear, she looked at James. He was shaking. His hand was still pressed to the wall.

"You could have waited for the elevator," he said, trying to make it a joke. His voice wasn't steady.

"I didn't have a lot of time to think about it."

Then the tremor intensified. A second wave, harder than the first. The floor gave.

Kara grabbed James's wrist and the edge of the shaft in the same motion. She pulled him out of the way as the elevator car dropped the last six feet and slammed into the bottom of the shaft. The sound was a ringing crash that echoed up through the building.

James was on the ground, breathing hard. Kara's hand was on his wrist. She had held on.

He looked up at her. For a second, she saw it. Not hero worship. Not awe. Not the thing CatCo wrote about. Just a man who had been afraid and was now trying not to show it.

"I'm okay," he said.

"I know. But I need to check the rest of the building."

She went to the roof, intending to fly. Her knees buckled. The sun had helped, but the Kryptonite test had taken its toll, and the earthquake had spent her. She got to her knees. Then to her feet.

And she flew anyway. Slow. Labored. Not the Supergirl who had taken a missile on her shoulder.

A woman who had decided that being seen weak was better than being seen dead.

She checked the rest of CatCo. Then the DEO.

The DEO was worse. Power was out. Backup generators stuttered. She found Alex in the holding area, yelling into a dead radio, trying to reach Hank.

Hank was missing.

The cell that had held Jemm was empty. The doors were torn from their frames.

"Jemm is out," Alex said. "Hank went after him. I can't reach either of them."

Kara went.

She moved through the dark corridors, listening. The DEO was a maze of metal corridors and blast doors, and in the dark, every shadow looked like a shape. She found Hank in the sub-level, standing over a body.

Jemm's body. The alien dead on the floor. A smear of green-black blood on the wall behind him.

Hank was breathing hard. His hands were on his knees. He looked up at Kara, and for the first time she saw what he was: not the man who ran the DEO, not the mentor, but a being who had been carrying a secret for a very long time.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Kara stood there. The emergency lights flickered. The body was on the floor. Hank was looking up at her. The earthquake was still rolling through the city outside, but in this room, the only sound was Hank's breathing and the hum of the emergency generators.

"What are you?" she asked.

Hank straightened. He looked at her — at Kara Danvers, the assistant, the sister of Alex, the woman who had saved the building — and he made his choice.

The shape he wore changed. The features softened, shifted, melted into something older, greener, alien.

J'onn J'onzz. Martian Manhunter.

"I'm J'onn J'onzz," he said. "I've been pretending to be a man who died because of me for a very long time."

Kara stared at him. The earthquake was still rolling. The body on the floor was Jemm, who had been Astra's prisoner, who had escaped during the quake, who had fought Hank and lost. Hank — J'onn — had killed him. Not with the DEO's authority. With his own hands.

"The real Hank Henshaw?" Kara asked.

"Died. After he killed Jeremiah Danvers. Jeremiah turned against him to protect me. Your father knew about me. He died protecting a Martian."

Kara's legs went out. She sat down on the floor. Not because she was weak — she was — but because the world had tilted. Her father had died protecting a Martian. Her sister had been working for a Martian. Her whole life — the DEO, the rescue, the training, the secret — had been run by a Martian who had been watching her since before she knew she was Kryptonian.

"Why didn't you tell me?" she asked.

"I didn't know how," J'onn said. "And I didn't know if I could trust you with it. I still don't know."

Kara looked at the body on the floor. Then she looked at J'onn.

"I need to go," she said. She stood, her legs unsteady. "I need to get back to CatCo. The building isn't stable."

She flew out. Behind her, J'onn J'onzz stood in the flickering emergency light, a Martian in a dead man's building, carrying a secret that was older than the DEO.

---

When Kara got back to CatCo, James was being checked by paramedics. He was okay. The earthquake was passing. The city was still standing, dented and dirty and stubborn.

Kara went to her desk.

Adrian was there. Ordinary clothes. Band at his wrist. He looked at her the way he looked at her now — not as a coworker, not as a friend, but as someone who had seen her at the edge of something and was waiting to see which way she would fall.

"I need soup," she said. "And I need you to not ask me any questions."

He nodded. He went to the kitchen. He came back with soup. It was light. It wasn't complicated. It was soup.

She drank it. She looked at the desk. She looked at the mug beside the theater mask magnet.

"Did you know?" she asked, without looking up.

Adrian paused. He stood in the doorway, holding the empty bowl.

"I knew Hank wasn't who he said he was," he said. "I didn't know who he was."

Kara nodded. She put the mug down. She went back to work.

Cat's intercom came on. "Kara, where is my coffee?"

Kara brought it with both hands and didn't spill.

Cat took the cup without looking up. She read the first paragraph of the copy Kara had left on the desk, marked it with a single line in the margin, and pushed it back. "The lead is too soft."

"I can tighten it."

"Do it. And send the layouts to art before they leave."

"They're already on their way."

Cat looked up then. She studied Kara's face for a moment — the bandage on the finger, the tiredness around the eyes, the fact that she hadn't sat down since she came in. "Sit down, Kara."

"I've the layouts—"

"Sit down."

Kara sat.

Cat leaned back in her chair. The coffee steamed beside her. "My mother thinks I built this on luck. She says it every time she visits. She has said it three times in three days."

Kara waited.

"She isn't wrong about the luck," Cat continued. "She is wrong about what I did with it. I worked. I built it. The luck was that nobody burned the building down before I finished." She took a drink. "She doesn't see the difference."

"You don't have to explain it to her."

"I don't explain things to her. I listen. Then I come to work and work." Cat set the cup down. "How is your finger?"

"It's fine."

"It bled on the carpet. You cleaned it."

"I did."

"Good." Cat opened the next file. "The layouts, Kara. Now."

Kara stood and went to collect them.

---

The next morning, CatCo was quiet. The earthquake had passed. The building was repaired. The staff was back at their desks, and the conversations in the bullpen were about the weather and the delayed flights and the fact that the coffee machine had been moved to a different wall.

Kara arrived early. She had coffee, folders, and a list from Cat that included three things that had nothing to do with each other.

She brought Cat her coffee first. Cat took it without looking up and said, "My mother is still in town. She thinks I built this on luck. She visited. I stayed. She is difficult. But she's my mother."

Kara stood by the desk. "You don't have to keep her around."

"She is my mother."

"That isn't an answer."

Cat looked up. "It is the only answer I've. Go get me water. And tell Wraith I want an interview."

"I don't know Wraith."

"Everyone knows someone who knows Wraith. Find him."

Kara took the empty glass and went to the water cooler. She filled it, set it on Cat's desk, and went back to her own.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was ordinary — band at his wrist, human-level, typing with the same steady rhythm he always used. He had heard Cat's question. He had heard Kara's answer. He didn't say anything. He kept typing.

Kara looked at him across the room. He didn't look up. He kept typing.

She turned to her screen and started the search for Wraith.

---

Later, at the DEO, Alex found Kara in the hallway outside the holding area.

"You fought Astra," Alex said. "You brought her in. She is in custody. She isn't okay. She is angry. She is also a person. She is a person who made bad choices. She was also a person who was trying to save Krypton. She failed."

Kara leaned against the wall. "She killed people."

"She killed people who were trying to kill her. It isn't the same thing, and it's also not the same thing." Alex crossed her arms. "She is in a cell. She will be tried. She won't be executed, because that isn't what we do."

"And if she escapes again?"

"Then we catch her again."

Kara pushed off the wall. "That isn't a plan."

"It is the plan we've."

Kara walked down the hallway. Alex followed.

"What about Hank?" Kara asked, without turning around.

Alex stopped.

Kara turned. "What about Hank?"

Alex's expression was careful. "Hank is fine. He is dealing with the fallout."

"Dealing with it how?"

"He is a man who has been carrying a lot for a long time. He will carry it."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the answer I've."

Kara looked at Alex for a long moment. Then she nodded. "Okay."

She went back to her desk at CatCo.

---

At night, the apartment was quiet.

Kara sat on the sofa with her feet pulled up under her. Adrian sat at the other end, a book in his lap that he hadn't opened.

The television was off. The city lights came through the window and moved across the ceiling.

"Did you know?" Kara asked. She didn't look at him.

"About Hank?" Adrian set the book down. "I knew he wasn't who he said he was. I didn't know who he was."

"How long have you known?"

"A while."

"Why didn't you say anything?"

Adrian was quiet for a moment. "Because it wasn't my secret to tell. And because I didn't know what it meant for you."

Kara looked at her hands. The bandage on her finger was gone — the cut had healed during the night, as all cuts did — but she remembered the feeling of it bleeding. The ordinary, human way it had hurt.

"I sat on the floor of the DEO today," she said. "I sat down because my legs went out. And I sat there and listened to a Martian tell me that my father died protecting him."

Adrian didn't say anything.

"I've been Kryptonian for twenty-six years," Kara said. "I've been Supergirl for a year. And today I sat on a floor and felt like a human who had just found out that everything she thought was true wasn't."

Adrian reached across the space between them and put his hand on her wrist. The band was beneath his cuff. He was human-level. He was ordinary.

"You are both," he said.

Kara looked at him.

"You are Kryptonian and you're human," Adrian said. "Not one or the other. Both. The Kryptonite made you weak. It didn't make you less. It made you feel what a human feels when they're weak. And you kept standing."

"I fell."

"You stood back up."

Kara leaned her head back against the sofa. "I'm so tired."

"I know."

"Not the Kryptonite tired. The other tired."

Adrian didn't say anything. He sat there with his hand on her wrist.

After a while, Kara said, "Do you think we'll always stay together?"

"If you want," he said. "I want if you want."

"I want."

She closed her eyes. She fell asleep there, on the sofa, with his hand still on her wrist.

He stayed awake a little longer, listening to the city without using his hearing, choosing ordinary every day.

Then he turned off the lamp and went to bed.

---

The next morning, Kara woke up on the sofa.

The sun came through the window and fell across the floor. She sat up. She looked at the kitchen table. She looked at the kitchen. She looked at the door.

She was alone.

She stood up and went to the kitchen. She made coffee. She drank it and looked at the window.

The city went on outside. The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved. The people walked the same streets. The buildings stood the same way.

She finished her coffee. She went to the shower. She got dressed. She picked up her bag.

She was going to CatCo. She was going to do her work. She was going to wait for the next message. And when it came, she would tell Cat. And they would decide together what to do.

She walked out of the apartment. She locked the door behind her. She went down the stairs and out into the street.

The morning was warm. The city was busy. The delivery trucks unloaded at the same doors. The street vendors set up their carts. The people moved in the same direction they always moved.

She walked to the car. She got in. She drove to CatCo.

---

At CatCo, the bullpen was quiet.

Winn was at his desk. James was at his desk. The receptionist was at the front desk. Adrian was at his desk.

Kara walked to her desk and sat down. She opened her email. She started working.

She didn't think about the box. She didn't think about the man. She didn't think about the message. She thought about her work. She thought about the files on her screen. She thought about the emails she needed to answer.

She did her work.

At ten, Cat called her into the office.

"The chemical plant fire is still in the news," Cat said. "The workers are telling their story. They're saying a woman flew them out. The story is building. And Lord is building with it."

"He is using it," Kara said.

"He is using it. He is calling it a miracle. He is calling it a tragedy that was averted. He is calling it a reason to build something. He is calling it a story that proves the city needs him. And the story is getting bigger."

"And we're holding the pattern."

"We're holding the pattern. We hold the paper. We hold the names. We watch the tech room. We watch Marcus. We watch the ghost code. And we wait."

"I know," Kara said. "I'm holding. I'm waiting. And I'll tell you when the next message comes."

"Good. Do your work. Go home. Live your life. Wait for the next message. And when it comes, you tell me. And we decide together what to do."

"I'll."

Kara left Cat's office and went back to her desk.

---

At lunch, Kara was still at her desk when Adrian came over with a sandwich.

He set it beside her keyboard. "You didn't eat this morning."

"I made coffee. That is eating."

"Coffee isn't eating."

"Coffee is enough for now."

He sat in the chair beside her. "You are tired."

"I'm tired."

"You went into a burning building yesterday. You got three people out. You went back in. You came out through a vent. And you're tired."

"I'm tired of all of it. The fires. The files. The leak. The ghost code. The waiting. I'm tired of waiting."

"I know. But the waiting is the work. The waiting is the part that matters. Because when Lord makes his move, he'll act in the open. He will act in front of people. He will act because he wants everyone to see. And when he acts, he'll leave a trail. And the trail will lead to him. And you'll have the pattern. You will have the paper. You will have the names. And you'll use all of it. And you'll stop him."

"And if I can't stop him?"

"You can stop him. Not alone. Not with your power alone. You stop him with the pattern. You stop him with the paper. You stop him with the people you trust. You stop him by knowing what he did and how he did it and who helped him. And you stop him by using all of it at the right moment."

Kara picked up the sandwich. She took a bite. "And until then?"

"And until then, you hold. You hold the pattern. You hold the paper. You hold the names. You watch the tech room. You watch Marcus. You watch the ghost code. You watch Lord. You watch without the cameras. You watch with your eyes. And you wait."

She took another bite. "I'll hold."

"I know you'll."

Adrian stood up. He went back to his desk.

Kara finished the sandwich. She looked at the screen. She went back to work.

---

That evening, Kara went back to her apartment.

She opened the door. She went in. She looked at the kitchen table. Non was there, as he had been every evening since he came. His hands were on the surface. He was looking at the window.

"You are back," he said.

"I'm back," Kara said. She sat down across from him.

"You are holding," Non said.

"I'm holding. I'm doing my work. I'm waiting for the next message. And when it comes, I'll tell Cat. And we'll decide together what to do."

"And you're tired."

"I'm tired. I went into a burning building yesterday. I got three people out. I went back in. I came out through a vent. And I'm tired of all of it. The fires. The files. The leak. The ghost code. The waiting."

"I know. But the waiting is the work. The waiting is the part that matters. Because when Lord makes his move, he'll act in the open. He will act in front of people. He will act because he wants everyone to see. And when he acts, he'll leave a trail. And the trail will lead to him. And you'll have the pattern. You will have the paper. You will have the names. And you'll use all of it. And you'll stop him."

"And I'm holding. And I'm waiting. And I'll stop him."

"You will stop him," Non said. "Not alone. Not with your power alone. You stop him with the pattern. You stop him with the paper. You stop him with the people you trust. You stop him by knowing what he did and how he did it and who helped him. And you stop him by using all of it at the right moment."

"And until then?"

"And until then, you hold. You hold the pattern. You hold the paper. You hold the names. You watch the tech room. You watch Marcus. You watch the ghost code. You watch Lord. You watch without the cameras. You watch with your eyes. And you wait."

Kara went to the sofa and sat down.

The television was off. The city lights moved across the ceiling.

She sat there. She thought about the fire. She thought about the catwalk. She thought about the workers. She thought about the smoke and the flames and the vent on the north side.

She thought about the files. She thought about the names. She thought about the leak. She thought about the ghost code. She thought about Lord, building his story, waiting for the right moment.

She didn't know when he would make his move. She didn't know what it would look like. She didn't know what would happen.

She knew one thing. She had the pattern. She had the paper. She had the names. And she would be ready.

She sat there. The night went on. The city went on outside the window.

The chipped mug sat on the desk beside the magnet, holding its corner the way it always did.
