# Chapter 17: Childish Things Part 2 R1 - The Convention 4000w+ Full Not Rushing

The Schott Toys factory was abandoned in the way only a toy factory can be abandoned. Not empty, but full of things that had been left mid-motion. Assembly lines still, dust thick, windows broken, giant building blocks six feet tall stacked like a child's playroom but covered in cobwebs and bird droppings. The air smelled like old plastic and oil and something sweet that had gone bad.

Winslow Schott Sr. was there, sitting on a block, holding another doll, this one a little girl with yarn hair. He looked tired now, not calm, tired like a man who had walked too far.

"Supergirl," he said, almost delighted, as if she were a toy he had been waiting to unwrap. "You are even more beautiful in person. My son has good taste in friends."

"Where is Winn?" Kara asked, cape snapping in the draft from broken windows, boots soft on concrete that had once been polished.

"Safe. For now. He is my best creation, you know. Better than any toy. I wanted to give him the world, but Dunholtz stole my designs. He stole his childhood. He stole my chance to watch him grow up. I went to prison because of him. Winn, I almost lost you because of him."

Kara stepped forward. "That does not give you the right to kill. Six people died because you mailed a bomb to the wrong desk."

"Right? Right is for children," Schott said, and pressed another button in his pocket, the same pocket that had held the yo-yo.

The floor under Kara opened. Not a trapdoor, a vat. Thermite quicksand, a mixture that burned at four thousand degrees, hidden in a giant building block that had been hollowed out. It was not quicksand that drowned, it was quicksand that burned and sucked down, and it was rigged to detonate if she used heat vision to free herself. The chemical reaction would use the salt water in a standard IV—NaCl and H2O—to produce hydrochloric acid, lethal to humans, but for her it would be a thermal charge that would burn through boots and skin and keep her stuck while it burned. A proper trap, not a joke, built by a man who understood chemistry and hate in equal measure.

She sank to her knees, then waist. The heat was immediate, even through the suit, even with cells that had been to the sun and remembered to overcompensate, baseline higher temporarily then settles higher than before cells remember overcompensate. She could feel her boots soften.

Above her, on a track on the ceiling that had once moved toys from painting to packaging, a small wooden steamer trunk moved toward an industrial shredder, and inside it a child's voice screamed, "Help! Help me! Help!"

Kara looked up, looked down, and made a choice.

She did not use heat vision. She had learned from Red Tornado that using the obvious power is sometimes the trap. She used freeze breath.

She exhaled, not a gasp but a controlled, superpowered, proper, no-joke stream of cold that dropped the temperature in the vat from four thousand degrees to minus fifty in three seconds. The thermite froze, cracked, became brittle, the chemical reaction stopped mid-reaction because cold stops chemistry. She planted her hands on the edge, concrete and rebar, and pulled, muscles in shoulders and back and legs, absorbing the momentum of her own weight, flight counter-rotation to keep from spinning, proper Kryptonian strength not a joke, and ripped herself free. The frozen thermite shattered like glass, shards tinkling.

She shot up, caught the steamer trunk an inch before the shredder's teeth, tore it open with fingers that did not ache—

Inside was another doll, talking, laughing, a little girl doll with yarn hair.

Schott was gone. A side door open, footprints in dust leading out, quick, small steps.

Kara stood in the factory, holding a doll that laughed, covered in frozen thermite dust, breathing hard, and she thought about how some traps are not meant to kill you but to make you feel stupid, and how Winn must have felt growing up with a father who made toys that were also traps.

She left the doll on the block, not breaking it, because breaking a child's toy, even a creepy one, felt wrong.

Winn was at Kara's apartment that night. He had not been there since before Red Tornado, since before the sun, since before Kara had kissed Adrian on a roof in November cold and learned what it meant to choose. He sat on her couch, holding a beer he did not drink, looking at the blanket Alex had left, at the TV that was off, at the chipped mug on Kara's counter that was not the same chipped mug as CatCo's but similar, because Kara had bought one like it after Evelyn left because she liked the weight.

"I worry I will snap one day," he said, quiet, not looking at Kara, looking at his hands that were good with computers and bad with people. "Like him. I am so like him. Same cowardice, same anger. I bottle things up. I... I see the same genes as time bombs. My dad was a coward who bottled things up until he mailed a bomb. I bottle things up until I... I don't know."

Kara sat next to him, not too close, because she had learned from James and from Adrian that closeness is a choice and that giving space is also a choice.

"Winn, your dad killing those people is similar to me losing all of Krypton," she said, voice soft, not a speech, just a truth. "Both of our lives ended that day. When I was thirteen and my parents put me in a ship and I watched my planet die, when you were eleven and your dad mailed a bomb and your mom left and you moved. Both of our lives ended. But rather than giving in to rage and hate, we both strive to give back. You make things that help people. You make my suit better. You make my life better. I... I try to."

Winn looked at her, eyes wet, the kind of wet that men are told not to show. "You are better than me."

"No," Kara said. "I am just... I have a sister who tells me when I am being an idiot. And a friend who places a chipped mug back on the desk next to a theater mask magnet and goes back to work ordinary human-level as if not helped. And a friend who hears my pen across the room and texts hallway when I need help."

Winn laughed, a little, broken.

Then he leaned in and kissed her.

It was not a long kiss. It was a scared kiss, a bottled-up-for-years kiss, a kiss that had been waiting since before she was Supergirl, since CatCo, since she smiled at him when he fixed her computer and she did not know how to turn it on and he did not know how to tell her she was beautiful without sounding creepy. His lips were chapped, his breath smelled like beer he had not drunk.

Kara pulled away immediately, gentle but firm, hand on his shoulder, not pushing, just holding.

"Winn," she said.

"I know," he said, standing, embarrassed, sad, both, the worst combination. "I know. I just... I am done being a coward who hides his feelings and bottles everything up like my father did. I have loved you since before you were Supergirl. Since CatCo. Since you."

Kara stood too, because sitting while someone stands feels like power and she did not want power. "I love you, Winn. As a friend. As my best friend. As family. But I—"

"I know," he said again, and left, not slamming door, just closing it soft, because Winn does not slam doors.

Out in the hallway, a hand covered his mouth. Ether, sweet, chemical, on a rag that had once been used to clean toys. Winslow Sr., who had been waiting in the stairwell because fathers know where their sons go when they are sad, because he had followed Winn from CatCo and he knew Kara's address because he had been watching.

When Winn woke, he was in the back of a van that smelled like old toys and gasoline, hands tied with zip ties that were actually toy handcuffs, big and plastic and humiliating and a little loose because Schott Sr. had never been good at tying real knots. His father sat across from him, holding a plastic gun that looked like a toy but had a real barrel, and a phone with ten red dots.

"Chester Dunholtz will be at the National City Toy Convention tomorrow," Schott Sr. said, calm, as if discussing a shopping list, as if this were normal father-son activity. "Receiving a lifetime achievement award for designs he stole. My designs. The jumping jack. The talking bear. All of them. He stole your childhood, Winn. He stole my chance to watch you grow up. I went to prison because of him. Winn, I almost lost you because of him."

"I will not kill him," Winn said, voice hoarse from ether.

"Then I will detonate ten bombs I have hidden in various toys throughout the convention floor," Schott said, showing him the phone, ten red dots blinking, each one a toy, a doll, a robot, a jack-in-the-box. "Hundreds will die. Children. Parents. Collectors. Including you. Including your friend Kara, if she tries to save them. It is up to you. This murder by you will bring us closer together. You are the best thing I ever made, Winn. Not the toys. You. The family that kills together..."

Winn stared at the gun. It was light, plastic, toy-like, but the barrel was real, and it would not set off metal detectors because it was mostly plastic, a special polymer Schott had invented for this exact purpose.

He thought about texting Kara. He thought about calling Agent Chase. He thought about being brave, and then he thought about being his father's son, and he put the gun in his jacket.

The National City Toy Convention was bright, loud, full of children and parents and collectors and a giant display of Schott Toys through the years, including the jumping jack Dunholtz had stolen and the talking bear that had killed six people. There was a Superman rocket ride outside, smaller than the arcade's, for kids.

Chester Dunholtz was on the dais, old, smug, accepting a lifetime achievement award for designs he stole, smiling with teeth that were too white.

Winn walked up the aisle, gun in his jacket, heart hammering so loud he was sure everyone could hear it, so loud Adrian could have heard it from CatCo if he had been listening, which he was not, because ordinary was a choice and he was choosing ordinary today. Winn had not texted Kara. He had not called the FBI. He thought, stupidly, that he could handle it alone, because he was his father's son and he wanted to prove he was not, and because he was embarrassed about the kiss and did not want to see Kara.

He reached the dais, pulled the gun, aimed at Dunholtz's face, finger on trigger, hand shaking—

And could not.

He had fixed Kara's computer. He had made her suit. He had placed a chipped mug back on a desk. He was not his father.

He pointed it at the ceiling and fired. The plastic gun cracked, a small pop, not loud, but loud enough.

The crowd screamed. Children cried. Parents pulled them close.

Supergirl was already there. She had been following Winn since he left Kara's building, because Adrian had heard Winn's heartbeat go irregular in the hallway and had texted Kara a single word: hallway. And Kara had followed, quiet, no ticker, no WRAITH SEEN NEAR DEO TEST SITE, just a friend following a friend, because she had learned from Alex that sometimes you follow without asking.

She saw Toyman hiding beneath the stage, phone in hand, thumb over detonate, smiling.

"Winn, bombs?" she asked, fast, landing next to him, not grabbing, just standing.

"Ten, in toys, throughout floor," Winn gasped, gun empty, hands up.

Countdown: five seconds. Schott had started it when Winn did not shoot.

Kara did not try to collect them all. She could not, not in five seconds, not with hundreds of people panicking, not without someone dying. She had learned from Red Tornado that sometimes you do not overpower, you absorb momentum, you redirect.

She ripped the sprinkler pipe from the ceiling with one hand, absorbing the momentum of water pressure so it did not whip and hit a child, proper superpowered, no joke, and activated the system. Water poured, cold, from a hundred heads.

Then she used freeze breath, wide, controlled, proper superpowered, no jokes, no cartoon, and froze the water into an ice dome, thick, clear, sealing the crowd on the stage, sealing herself outside with the bombs, sealing Winn inside with the crowd because he was safer inside.

The bombs went off. Ten small explosions, toy-sized, but together enough to kill, enough to shred flesh and plastic and dreams. The sound was muffled by ice, then loud, then muffled again.

The ice shattered, taking the entirety of the blowback, shards flying outward, melting into steam before they could cut anyone, because Kara held the field for three seconds longer than physics said it should, because her cells remembered sun and overcompensated and baseline higher. The dome held, then fell, water and ice and steam, and everyone inside was wet and alive and staring, and a little girl in the front row laughed because she thought it was part of the show.

Toyman was on the floor, caught by his own blast, leg broken, yo-yo gone, phone dead.

Winn was on his knees, gun empty, crying now, not hiding, because he was done bottling things up.

Supergirl landed next to him, quiet, you okay? Same quiet as after train, after gas.

Winn nodded, crying.

Kara approached, holding two coffees, one in the chipped mug that was Evelyn's, one in a new mug that said WORLD'S BEST ASSISTANT that Cat had given her as a joke.

"Winn?"

He looked up. His eyes were red, but less red than before.

"I am in love with you," he said, all at once, as if saying it fast would make it hurt less, as if not bottling would save him. "I have been since before you were Supergirl. Since you smiled at me and did not know how to turn on your computer. Since you asked me where the bathroom was and I told you wrong because I was nervous. I cannot be your friend when it hurts to bottle up my feelings. It was my father bottling up his feelings that turned him into the Toyman. I am done being a coward who hides."

Kara sat, put the mug down next to the theater mask magnet, next to the chipped mug.

"I do not want things to change," she said, because she is Kara and she wants everyone to be happy and she does not know how to want less.
