# Chapter 14: Hostile Takeover

*Episode: S01E08 - Hostile Takeover*

Astra sat inside the cell, her wrists bound with cuffs that glowed with faint Kryptonian script. The light caught the edges and pulsed with each breath.

On the table outside, in a sealed case, was a dagger. It glowed green.

---

The DEO was quiet that morning. Quiet for a place that kept Kryptonians in cells.

Alex walked the corridor with a tablet in her hand. She checked the locks on the holding area, checked the camera feeds, checked the status board. All green. She stopped at Astra's cell and looked through the glass.

Astra sat with her back against the wall, her knees up, her wrists in the cuffs. She looked up at Alex without surprise.

"You are checking on me," Astra said.

"I'm checking on the locks."

"The locks are fine."

"I'm checking on the locks," Alex said again.

Astra let her head rest against the wall. "You don't have to keep me in the dark, Alex."

"I'm not keeping you in the dark. You are in a cell."

Astra smiled. It wasn't a warm smile. "You are a Danvers. Your father was a stubborn man. You are a stubborn woman."

"I'll take that as a compliment."

"It isn't a compliment."

Alex turned away and walked down the corridor. She didn't look back.

---

In the bullpen, Winn was arguing with a printer.

The machine had jammed for the fourth time that morning. He had opened the panel, pulled out a strip of paper, put it back, and the machine had refused to print again. He stood over it with his hands on his hips, muttering.

"It does this when you argue with it," Kara said, walking past.

"I'm not arguing with it. I'm diagnosing it."

"You are mumbling at it. That is arguing."

Winn looked up. "It is a malfunctioning piece of equipment that has decided to make my morning difficult."

"Turn it off. Wait ten seconds. Turn it back on."

"I already tried that."

"Try it again."

Winn stared at the printer, then at Kara. He turned it off. He waited. He turned it back on. The machine hummed, whirred, and started printing.

"See?" Kara said.

"I'm not sure I want to reward it for this."

"You don't have to reward it. You just have to let it work."

Winn took the pages as they came out. "I won't tell anyone you gave me machine advice."

"Good. Because I won't tell anyone you cried at it."

He went back to his desk with the pages. Kara went to hers.

Adrian was already at his desk. He had been there since seven. The band was at his wrist. He typed at the same steady pace he always used, and he didn't look up when she came in.

"Tom is coming tomorrow," Kara said.

"Cat told you."

"She told me this morning. She didn't ask me anything. She told me."

"That is how Cat communicates. She tells you what's happening. She doesn't ask for permission."

Kara sat down. "She said he'll stay for an indefinite period."

"That means he doesn't have a return date."

"That means he's staying until he gets what he came for."

Adrian stopped typing and looked at her. "That is what it means."

---

The next morning, Kara arrived at CatCo at eight. She went to her desk, set down her bag, and looked at the door.

Tom hadn't arrived yet. The receptionist hadn't seen him. The lobby was quiet.

She checked her email. Nothing urgent. She checked Cat's calendar. Cat had a meeting at nine. She had blocked out the rest of the day.

"He isn't here yet," Adrian said from across the bullpen.

"I didn't say he was."

"You were looking at the door."

"I was looking at the door because the door is where people come in."

Adrian went back to his work.

At ten, Tom walked through the lobby. He had a travel bag over his shoulder and the ease of a man who had once had a key to the building. He was tall, clean-cut, and he smiled at the receptionist like he knew her. He asked for Cat's office.

Kara watched him through the glass.

Tom walked across the bullpen. He looked at the desks, at the people, at the photographs on the wall. He stopped at Kara's desk.

"Kara Danvers," he said. "Cat's assistant."

"Yes."

"Cat mentioned you." He looked her over. "She said you're good."

"She said that?"

"She said you handle things." He smiled. "I'm sure you do."

He walked on. He went to Cat's office and knocked.

Cat opened the door. She didn't smile.

"Tom," she said.

"Catherine." He used her full name. "You look well."

"You are in my office."

"I'm in the building you own. There is a difference."

He stepped past her and went inside. Cat closed the door behind him.

---

Tom stayed for the rest of the morning.

He came out of Cat's office at eleven and walked through the bullpen again. He looked at the layout. He looked at the stairs. He looked at the elevator. He looked at the door to the stairwell. He looked at the fire exit.

He stopped at Winn's desk.

"You are the IT guy," he said.

"I'm the IT guy," Winn said.

"How many people work on this floor?"

"About fifteen."

"And the building?"

"Three floors. About fifty total."

Tom nodded. "Cat built this from nothing. That isn't easy."

"It isn't easy," Winn said.

Tom moved on. He stopped at James's desk. He looked at the photographs on the wall above it.

"You take these?" he asked.

"Some of them," James said.

"The one with the bridge. That is the one Cat has in her office. You took that?"

"I did."

"It is a good picture."

"Thank you."

Tom moved on. He stopped at Adrian's desk. He looked at the band beneath the cuff.

"A watch?" he asked.

"A watch," Adrian said.

Tom looked at it for a moment. "Expensive-looking."

"It keeps time."

Tom smiled. "I'm sure it does more than that."

"It keeps time," Adrian said again.

Tom moved on. He walked back to Cat's office and knocked.

---

Kara went to the water cooler after Tom went back into Cat's office.

She filled a glass and drank. She set the glass down and looked at the bullpen.

Adrian was at his desk. He was typing. He didn't look up.

Winn was at his desk. He was looking at the printer like it had personally offended him.

James was at his desk. He was sorting contact sheets.

The receptionist was at the front desk. She was reading a book.

Everything looked normal. Everything was normal. But Tom was in Cat's office, and Tom was Cat's ex-husband, and Tom was looking at the building like he was measuring it.

Kara went back to her desk.

"He looked at the stairs," she said to Adrian.

"He looked at everything," Adrian said.

"He looked at the fire exit."

"He looked at the fire exit."

"He looked at you."

"He looked at the watch," Adrian said. "He didn't look at me."

Kara sat down. She opened her email and started working. She didn't look at the door again.

---

The next three days passed in the same pattern.

Tom came to CatCo every morning. He had dinner with Cat at a restaurant down the street. He walked through the bullpen and looked at the desks. He asked the receptionist about the staff. He asked Winn about the IT setup. He asked James about the photographs. He asked Kara about her work.

He was polite. He was pleasant. He was the kind of man who knew how to ask questions without sounding like he was asking questions.

Kara answered what she could. She didn't answer what she couldn't. She kept her desk and her work and her face neutral.

Adrian watched from across the bullpen. With his band at his wrist and his hearing restricted to ordinary range, he noticed the things Tom noticed. The locks on the doors. The cameras in the corners. The layout of the floor. The location of Cat's office. The stairs. The elevator. The fire exit. The load-bearing walls.

He said nothing. He kept typing.

---

On the fourth day, Tom asked Kara to lunch.

"I want to understand the operation," he said. He sat across from her at a small table in a restaurant two blocks from CatCo. "Cat built something here. She built it from nothing. I want to know how."

"She hired people. She worked hard. She made good choices."

"That isn't an explanation."

"It is the explanation."

Tom leaned back. "You are very loyal to her."

"I work for her."

"You do more than work for her. You protect her."

Kara didn't say anything.

"Cat always had people who protected her," Tom said. "She didn't always appreciate it."

"Then she's lucky to have it now."

Tom smiled. "You are a very direct person, Kara."

"I'm an assistant. I get things done."

"I think you get more done than an assistant."

Kara stood up. "I've to get back to work."

She left the restaurant and walked back to CatCo. She didn't look back.

---

That afternoon, Kara went to the DEO.

She didn't have a reason. She told herself she was checking on something for Cat, a story about Lord Technologies that Cat had asked her to look into. But the DEO was in the same building as Lord Technologies. And she hadn't been to the DEO in three days.

She wanted to walk through the corridors. See the cells. See that Astra was still in her cell. See that the locks were still green.

Alex met her in the hallway.

"You are here," Alex said.

"I'm here for a story."

"You are here to check the locks."

Kara didn't say anything.

Alex looked at her for a moment. Then she nodded. "The locks are green. The cells are full. Astra is in her cell. Jemm is dead. Hank is dealing with it."

"How is he dealing with it?"

"He is a man who has been carrying a lot for a long time. He will carry it."

Kara looked down the hallway. The holding area was quiet. The camera feeds were green. The status board was green.

"Everything is fine," she said.

"Everything is as it's," Alex said.

Kara nodded. She turned and walked back to the elevator.

---

That evening, Kara went to Cat's office.

Cat was at her desk, reading a document. She looked up when Kara came in.

"He asked me to lunch," Kara said.

Cat set the document down. "I know."

"You knew he was going to ask me."

"I knew he would try to talk to everyone." Cat leaned back. "I didn't know he would choose you first."

"He asked about you. He asked about the building. He asked about how you built it."

"And what did you tell him?"

"I told him you hired people and worked hard."

Cat looked at her for a long moment. "That is the truth."

"It isn't the whole truth."

"It is the truth he's allowed to have." Cat picked up the document again. "Tom left me because he wanted a different life. He is coming back because he wants a different life again. He will look around. He will ask questions. He will leave when he's what he came for."

"What did he come for?"

Cat didn't answer. She turned the page.

Kara stood there. Then she turned and walked out.

---

She didn't go straight home. She went to the garage at CatCo and stood by the punching bag.

She hit it. Left, right, left. She hit it because she didn't want to think about Cat's ex-husband walking through the building and looking at the locks and asking questions and smiling.

She hit it until her arms were tired. Then she stopped. She stood with her hands on the bag, breathing hard.

"You are hitting that harder than you need to," a voice said.

She turned. Adrian was in the doorway. He had come down from the bullpen. He was wearing his coat. He was going home.

"I'm hitting it because I want to hit something," she said.

"You can hit me instead."

"I don't want to hit you."

"Then hit the bag less hard. You will need your hands tomorrow."

Kara let go of the bag. It swung and settled.

"He asked me to lunch," she said.

"I know."

"He asked about Cat. He asked about the building. He asked about how she built it."

"And you told him the truth he's allowed to have."

"I told him she hired people and worked hard."

"That is the truth."

"It isn't the whole truth."

Adrian stood in the doorway. He didn't come in. He watched her.

"It is the truth that matters to him," he said.

Kara looked at the bag. It had stopped swinging.

"I don't like being watched," she said.

"I know."

"I don't like being watched by people who want something."

Adrian was quiet. Then he came in. He stood in front of her. He put his hands on her shoulders.

"Then don't let him watch you," he said. "Let him watch the desk. Let him watch the building. Let him watch Cat. Don't let him watch you."

Kara looked up at him.

"You are harder to watch than the building," Adrian said.

She stood there with his hands on her shoulders. Then she leaned forward and put her head on his chest.

He stood there. He didn't move. He let her.

After a while, she stepped back. "I need to go home."

"I'll walk with you."

They walked out of the garage together. The city was dark. The streetlights came on. The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved.

They didn't talk. They walked.

---

Two days later, Tom left.

He came to CatCo in the morning, packed his bag, and walked through the bullpen on his way out. He stopped at Kara's desk.

"It was good talking to you," he said.

"It was okay."

He smiled. "Cat is lucky to have you."

"She is."

He walked out. The receptionist watched him go. Winn watched him go. James watched him go. Adrian watched him go from across the bullpen, and then he turned back to his work.

The lobby went quiet after the door closed.

Winn let out a breath. "I'm glad he's gone."

"He wasn't here that long," Kara said.

"Four days is long enough for someone who asks questions about the locks."

Kara looked at Winn. "You noticed the locks."

"I notice everything. I'm the IT guy. That is my job." He went back to his desk. "Also, I don't like being watched."

Kara went to her desk. Adrian was already at his. He was typing. He didn't look up.

"He is gone," she said.

"He is gone," Adrian said.

"He didn't find what he came for."

"He didn't find what he came for," Adrian said. "But he'll try again."

"How do you know?"

"Because he came here once. Men who come back once will come back again."

Kara sat down. She looked at the door. The door was closed. The lobby was quiet. The receptionist was reading her book.

"He looked at the fire exit," she said.

"He looked at the fire exit," Adrian said. "He will remember it. He will remember the stairs. He will remember the cameras. He will remember the layout of the floor."

"And then?"

"And then he'll come back with a different question."

Kara opened her email. She started working. She didn't look at the door again.

---

That evening, Kara and Adrian sat on the sofa in her apartment.

The television was on, but nobody was watching it.

"He was looking for something," Kara said.

"Yes," Adrian said.

"He was looking for something in CatCo. He was looking at the locks, the cameras, the stairs. He was looking at the building."

"He was looking at the woman who owns the building," Adrian said. "He wanted to know what she had built. He wanted to know if he could take it."

Kara leaned back against the sofa. "Can he?"

"Not if she doesn't let him."

"She let him in."

"She let him in because she wanted to see what he would do. She has done that before. She watches people to find out who they're." Adrian turned his head toward her. "She watched you, at lunch. She watched me, from across the bullpen. She watches everyone."

"She is good at it."

"She is a journalist. It is what she does."

Kara looked at the ceiling. "I don't like being watched."

"I know."

"I don't like being watched by people who want something."

Adrian was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "Neither do I."

He reached across the space between them and put his hand on her wrist. The band was beneath his cuff. He was human-level. He was ordinary.

Kara closed her eyes.

The television played. The city went on outside the window.

After a while, she said, "Do you think he'll come back?"

"I think he'll try."

"And then?"

"And then we'll see what he tries."

Kara nodded. She didn't say anything else. She sat there with his hand on her wrist and the television playing and the city going on outside the window.

The chipped mug sat on the desk beside the magnet, holding its corner the way it always did, holding it steady through the morning and through whatever the morning brought.

---

The next morning, Kara arrived at CatCo at eight.

The lobby was quiet. The receptionist was reading her book. The elevator hummed. The bullpen was empty.

She walked to her desk and set down her bag. She looked at the door. The door was closed. Nobody had come through it.

Tom was gone. The building was quiet. The locks were green. The cameras were green. The status board was green.

She sat down. She opened her email. She started working.

Adrian arrived at eight-fifteen. He walked through the lobby with his coat on his arm. He went to his desk. He sat down. He started typing.

He didn't look at the door. He didn't look at the locks. He didn't look at the cameras.

He typed.

Kara typed.

The building went on. The city went on. The morning went on.

It was ordinary. It was what it'd supposed to be.

Outside, the sun came up over National City.

The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved. The delivery trucks unloaded at the same doors. The street vendors set up their carts. The city went on without Tom, and it would go on after him. That was the way of it.

Kara finished her coffee. Adrian finished his work. The morning went on, the way mornings do.

Nobody looked at the door. Nobody needed to.

The building was quiet, and the quiet was the point. A quiet that had been earned.

Cat called Kara into her office at nine.

"My ex-husband is coming to town," Cat said. She didn't look up from her desk. "Tom. He will be here tomorrow. He is staying for an indefinite period."

Kara stood by the door. "That isn't a question."

"It isn't." Cat turned her chair toward the window. "He left two years ago. He sent a letter. He is coming back."

"Why?"

"Because he thinks he's business here." Cat picked up a pen and put it down. "Because he thinks National City is a place he can walk back into."

Kara waited.

"He is a journalist," Cat said. "He was at the Daily Planet. He will want to look around. He will want to talk to people. He will want to know what I'm doing and who I'm working with."

"You can tell him to stay away."

"I can. He won't." Cat looked at Kara. "I don't want him in this building. I don't want him in the bullpen. I don't want him asking questions about my staff."

"I'll keep him out."

"I'm not asking you to keep him out. I'm telling you that he'll come, and that he'll be unpleasant, and that you shouldn't feel obliged to be pleasant to him."

"I don't feel obliged to be pleasant to anyone, Cat."

Cat looked at her for a moment, then nodded. "Good."
