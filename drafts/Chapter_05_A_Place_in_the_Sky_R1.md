# Season 1, Episode 1 — Pilot

## Chapter 5 — A Place in the Sky

By the time Henshaw returned, someone had replaced the coffee in the briefing room and Adrian had left the second cup untouched.

The questions had become narrower as the night went on. Where he lived. Where he worked. Which names he used. Whether another prisoner had contacted him on Earth.

He had answered those.

Other questions required distinctions the people asking them did not like. Knowing Astra in prison was not knowing where she slept now. Surviving Krypton was not possessing a complete archive of its courts. Building the band was not an invitation to let someone dismantle it.

Henshaw put a sheet of paper on the table.

“A return time. A contact number. You tell us if anyone connected to Fort Rozz approaches you, and you do not contact Vartox or involve yourself in his questioning.”

Adrian read it.

“This afternoon?”

“Yes.”

“And until then?”

“You can go home.”

Adrian looked up.

Henshaw remained standing.

“You came here voluntarily. You’ve identified yourself and given statements we can check. I’m not turning a preliminary interview into a confrontation while half the questions about that record are still unanswered.”

“The conviction isn’t one of them.”

“No. Neither is your cooperation an acquittal.”

Henshaw pulled out the chair opposite him.

“The inquiry continues. We will assess the record, your account and what we can independently establish about your time here. Any longer-term decision comes after that work, not because you brought us a prisoner.”

Adrian looked again at the return time.

“I’ll be here.”

“Keep your phone on.”

“I will.”

Henshaw watched him fold the paper.

“You understand that people may recognize you now.”

“They could recognize me before.”

“Before, you weren’t standing beside the woman on every front page in the city.”

Adrian stopped folding.

“No,” he said. “I wasn’t.”

***

His apartment looked exactly as he had left it.

A glass stood beside the sink. One chair was pulled a little too far from the kitchen table. A message about a routine building inspection had been pushed under the door.

Adrian picked it up and set it beside the DEO’s return instructions.

Through the walls, he could hear a television, a restless child, running water. Beyond the building came traffic and voices, distinct enough that he had to choose which sounds to leave alone.

He drew back his cuff.

The controls were familiar beneath his fingers. He had built them for this decision, not for someone else to make it on his behalf.

He re-engaged the restriction.

The distant voices disappeared. The apartment became a room with a refrigerator humming in it and a pipe knocking somewhere in the wall.

Adrian put his phone on the table, checked its volume and took off his coat.

For a while, he stood without doing anything else.

Then he washed the dried blood from his hands.

***

Kara arrived at CatCo with a fresh dressing beneath her sleeve and a list of things Alex had told her not to do with that arm.

She had agreed to the list. She was finding the ordinary parts of it more irritating than she had expected.

Her bag kept sliding off the wrong shoulder. Opening the heavy glass door was awkward until she remembered to use her other hand. The pain had eased overnight, but it still objected when she moved carelessly.

Adrian was already at his desk.

She stopped for half a step before continuing.

He looked up.

“Morning.”

“Hi.”

On his screen was the guest schedule she had seen him working on yesterday. The interview rooms had not stopped needing people in them because their lives had changed.

“They let you leave?” she asked quietly.

“For now. I’m going back this afternoon.”

“Did they—”

“No decision about the rest.”

She nodded.

His cuff had shifted when he reached for a folder. Kara saw the band beneath it.

“You’re using it again?”

“Yes.”

She looked toward Cat’s office, then back at him.

“All the way?”

“All the way.”

It made him seem different for a moment, although nothing about his face had changed. Yesterday, he had crossed the city before she could get away from an axe. Now the telephone on his desk was the only way to reach him from another room without walking there.

He noticed her looking at it.

“The number still works,” he said.

“I know.”

A bakery bag sat beside his keyboard. There was another beside it, folded over at the top.

“There's a glazed donut in that one,” he said. “For you, if you want it.”

Kara looked at the bag.

She remembered the pleasure of finding out that he had noticed what she liked. She still liked the same things. That seemed oddly unfair.

“Not right now.”

“All right.”

He put the folder he had been holding on the edge of her desk.

“Cat moved the eleven o’clock meeting. These are the changes.”

Kara took it.

He did not ask whether she had thought about yesterday. He did not tell her that she looked tired. When she went to put away her bag, he let her go.

That helped more than another explanation would have.

***

Cat summoned them both before Kara had finished reading the revised schedule.

She looked at Kara’s sleeve, then at Adrian.

“Are we expecting a repeat of yesterday?”

“I have an appointment this afternoon,” Adrian said. “I've arranged cover for the guest floor.”

“And you were going to inform me when?”

“Now.”

“An excellent improvement on vanishing first.”

Cat turned to Kara.

“Are you well enough to work?”

“Yes, Ms. Grant.”

“Then stop holding that folder as if it contains a live animal.”

Kara changed hands.

Cat let the silence last just long enough for both of them to know she had noticed.

“The emergency is private,” she said. “The work you leave behind is not. If you have to leave, someone takes over before the telephone rings unanswered.”

“Yes, Ms. Grant,” Kara said.

Adrian nodded.

Cat turned her screen toward them. Supergirl looked back from James’s photograph.

“The city has had time to become interested. I would now like it to become interested in something we can tell it that no one else can.”

“The interview,” Kara said.

“The interview. Not another panel of people discussing what she might say if someone managed to interview her.”

Her eyes remained on Kara for a moment, then moved to Adrian.

“And keep the original passenger material in the archive. The original, not a compressed copy someone has improved beyond usefulness.”

“The shared material and source copy will stay intact,” he said.

“Good. I prefer evidence that hasn't been made more convenient.”

Kara looked down at the folder.

Cat tapped a finger against the photograph.

“We know what she can lift. Someone should find out what she intends to do.”

***

Winn caught up with Kara beside the break room.

He looked first at her arm, then at her face.

“You’re really back at work.”

“So are you.”

“Yes, but the most dangerous thing that happened to me yesterday was a call from accounting.”

She smiled before she could stop herself.

He looked relieved.

“How bad is it?”

“Better than it was. Alex wants to check it again later.”

“Good.” He glanced past her toward Adrian’s desk. “And that?”

Kara followed his glance.

“I asked him for some time.”

“Does he understand that?”

“Yes.”

Winn lowered his voice.

“I heard what the guy with the axe called him. I heard the prison part.”

“I know.”

“Am I supposed to pretend I didn’t?”

“No.”

Kara leaned back against the counter, careful of her arm.

“There’s more to it than we heard at the plant. He told me about it last night. I’m still trying to work out what I think.”

“Okay.” Winn waited, then added, “But you gave me his number.”

“I did.”

“And I called it.”

“You did exactly what I asked. And he came.”

Winn nodded slowly.

“If you’re in trouble again?”

“Call Alex. If I’m in immediate danger and you can reach Adrian, call him too.”

He studied her.

“I’m not going to pretend he didn’t save me because I’m upset with him,” she said. “That doesn’t mean everything else is settled.”

“All right.”

He stayed with her for another moment, then let her return to her desk.

Later, Adrian found him checking a damaged seam in the cape Kara had brought back.

“Is that repairable?” Adrian asked.

“The fabric, yes.”

Winn did not look up immediately.

Adrian waited.

“What does Kael-Varr mean?”

“My name.”

“The part he was scared of.”

Adrian pulled the empty chair out just far enough to sit. Their voices would not carry to the neighboring desks over the printers and conversation.

“I destroyed a city on Krypton,” he said. “I was convicted of mass killing. That was why I was in Fort Rozz.”

Winn’s hands stopped moving.

For several seconds, he looked at the seam rather than the man beside him.

“You’re saying you did it.”

“Yes.”

“Not that they blamed you.”

“No.”

Winn finally looked up.

“Does Kara know?”

“She does now. So do the people Alex works for.”

Adrian let the answer stand. He did not follow it with his parents’ deaths or ask Winn to weigh one piece of suffering against another.

Winn put the cape down.

“I’m glad you answered the phone,” he said at last.

“So am I.”

“I don’t have anything else to say yet.”

“All right.”

Adrian stood and left him the room to decide when he did.

***

At the DEO, Henshaw returned to Vartox without the aircraft photograph.

He brought the duty orders instead.

Vartox watched him set the page where he could read it through the barrier.

“What is that?”

“Confirmation that Kael-Varr is not participating in your interrogation. He has no access to your cell.”

Vartox read the name, then looked sharply at him.

Henshaw waited while the prisoner read the rest of the page.

“You could change this.”

“I could. I’m telling you what the arrangement is. I’m not offering you release.”

Vartox remained silent.

Henshaw took the chair outside the barrier.

“You asked to be kept away from him. That was already the policy. Now you’ve seen it. What can you tell me that I can check?”

After a while, Vartox said, “The truck.”

“What truck?”

He gave the location of a parking area near the diner, then described the tanker he had been using.

“There’s a communications unit in the cab.”

“Connected to the people who gave you the orders?”

“Yes.”

Henshaw passed the location to an agent outside the interview area. He did not leave to supervise the search himself.

“Who will we find on it?”

Vartox looked at the duty orders again.

“The man who passes them on.”

“His name?”

Vartox did not answer.

Henshaw waited.

“He serves Astra,” the prisoner said finally.

The director kept his expression still.

“Did she speak to you?”

“Not for this.”

“Did you hear her order the aircraft attack?”

“No.”

“Or the attack on Kara?”

“The orders came through her man.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

Vartox’s jaw tightened.

“No.”

Henshaw took a note.

“Then we keep that distinction.”

Vartox leaned forward as far as his restraints permitted.

“If they had known Kael was beside the girl, they wouldn’t have sent me with an axe.”

“Did you know he was here?”

“You saw what happened when I found out.”

Henshaw stood.

The fear was useful information. It was not a guarantee that everything offered beside it was true.

“We’ll look for the truck,” he said.

***

Alex listened to the interview before she called Kara.

Henshaw stood at the end of the console, looking over the recovery request.

“You’re telling her,” he said.

“Yes.”

“It’s one prisoner’s account.”

“I’ll tell her that too.”

He looked toward the screen, where the recording had paused on Vartox’s face.

“Her aunt is a potential subject of an investigation. That does not make this a family visit.”

Alex took off the headset.

“Then give her a way to work with us instead of leaving her to find out from whoever calls her next.”

Henshaw said nothing.

“She’s going to help people,” Alex continued. “She’s made that decision. We can insist she stays outside until the next emergency, or we can make sure she has information and someone to call.”

“This isn't a recruitment decision.”

“Then start with a phone number.”

After a moment, Henshaw pushed the desk handset toward her.

“Direct operations contact. We share what she needs for a specific response. If she follows a lead, she tells us where she’s going.”

“And we answer when she calls.”

“Yes.”

Alex picked up the phone.

***

Kara found James near the elevators with a flat package under his arm.

“I was going to ask if you wanted lunch,” she said.

His smile made the sentence feel less casual after she had said it.

“Can we make one stop first?”

“Where?”

“The roof.”

She looked at the package.

“Is this a photography thing?”

“Not entirely.”

The wind caught her hair as she stepped outside. The city spread beyond the edge of the building, enormous and busy and unconcerned that she had not yet worked out her place in it.

James set the package on a low wall, away from the drop.

Kara stayed beside the door for a moment.

“You know,” she said.

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Before I came here.”

She looked down, then back at him.

“Did my cousin send you?”

“I was ready to leave Metropolis. That part was mine. He encouraged National City.” James paused. “And he wanted you to have a friend here who understood some things without making you explain them.”

Kara crossed her arms, then immediately uncrossed them when the movement pulled at her injury.

James noticed but did not reach for her.

“Someone to watch me?”

“Someone you could talk to. Those aren't the same thing.”

“Were you going to tell me?”

“Yes.” He glanced at the package. “I was hoping to do it with a little less mystery than this.”

That earned a small, reluctant smile.

He opened the wrapping.

The red fabric inside caught the light differently from the material Winn had bought. Kara knew it before she touched it. Something about the weave brought back clothes folded in a room that no longer existed.

She put her hand against it.

“Krypton.”

James nodded.

“Your cousin sent it. It came from the blanket he arrived in.”

Kara drew the cape carefully out of the package.

For a moment, she could not speak.

Her mother’s recording had given her a voice from home. This was something she could hold.

“He kept this.”

“And now he wants you to have it.”

She ran her thumb along an edge.

“I spent so long thinking I was supposed to take care of him.”

James waited.

“Then I got here, and he didn’t need me to.”

“Maybe he doesn’t need you to have his life.”

Kara looked up.

“He’s proud of you,” James said. “Not because you’re doing exactly what he did.”

The wind moved the loose corner of the cape against her wrist.

Yesterday, someone stronger had saved her. Today, someone stronger had sent her a gift. Neither had told her that she should stop.

She had been supplying that argument herself.

“Thank you,” she said.

“You can tell him too.”

“I will. There’s something else I need to talk to him about.”

James did not ask her to pass it through him.

“He'll want to hear from you.”

Her phone rang before she could answer.

Kara glanced at the screen.

“It's Alex.”

James nodded and stepped toward the far side of the roof, giving her room.

Kara answered with the cape still resting over her arm.

“Hi.”

“I have something from Vartox,” Alex said. “It isn't independently confirmed yet.”

Kara’s hand closed on the fabric.

“What?”

“He says the man who gave him his orders serves Astra.”

For a moment, Kara heard only the wind.

“He spoke to her?”

“No. He says he didn't. He has given us a location for a vehicle and a communications unit. A team is checking it.”

Kara looked out over the city.

Yesterday, she had learned that her aunt might have survived. She had gone to sleep trying to imagine what it would be like to hear her voice again.

Now that voice might be waiting at the far end of a chain that led to an aircraft full of people.

Or a prisoner might have given them a familiar name because it was useful to him.

“I want to hear what he actually said,” she told Alex.

“You can.”

“Does Adrian know?”

“Not from us. This just happened.”

Kara looked toward the rooftop door. Several floors below, Adrian was working with the band engaged. He could not hear her making this decision.

She did not have to wait for him to make it with her.

“I’m coming in.”

“Henshaw has agreed to a direct operations contact,” Alex said. “If you're responding to something, tell us where. We’ll give you what we have.”

Kara took a breath.

“And when the information concerns my family?”

“We tell you what we know. And what we don’t.”

“All right.”

She ended the call.

James came back only after she looked toward him.

“Lunch?” he asked gently.

“Can I owe you one?”

“Sure.”

Kara folded the cape over both hands.

“I have somewhere I need to be.”

***

She did not disappear without telling anyone this time.

There was a message for Cat, a handoff to the other assistant covering the desk, and a brief stop beside Winn.

He looked from the package to her face.

“Do I want to know?”

“My cousin sent something.”

Winn saw a corner of red fabric and immediately looked interested.

“Later,” she said.

“Later,” he agreed. “Where are you going?”

“The DEO. Alex knows I’m coming.”

He nodded.

Adrian was on the telephone when she passed his desk. He saw the package, then her expression, and finished the sentence he was speaking rather than abandoning the call to question her.

Kara stopped.

When he put down the receiver, she said, “There’s a lead about my aunt. I don’t know how much of it is true yet.”

He became still.

“Alex?”

“She called me. I’m going to hear it.”

“All right.”

“You’re going in later?”

“This afternoon.”

Kara nodded.

She could have told him to come with her. Yesterday she might have wanted the strongest person she knew between her and every uncertainty.

What she wanted now was to hear the evidence without anyone deciding how she ought to feel about it.

“I’ll see you there,” she said.

“If you want to.”

She let that remain an offer rather than answering it as a promise.

Then she went to change.

On the roof, the new cape settled against her shoulders. It did not remove the ache in her arm or the things she was afraid of learning.

Below, the city kept moving.

Kara checked that the route ahead was clear, rose above the building and turned toward the people waiting to give her answers.

This time, they knew she was coming.
