# Chapter 28: Better Angels

*Episode: S01E20 - Better Angels*

The sword came down.

Kara did not move. She did not raise her hands. She did not block. She stood there with the Kryptonite blade aimed at her chest and the person holding it was her sister and was not her sister, and she let the sword come, because she would not fight her sister, and because if she had to choose between dying and hurting Alex, she would choose dying.

The blade stopped an inch from her chest.

Not because Kara stopped it. Because something else did.

J'onn was there. He had recovered enough to stand. He had dragged himself to the broadcasting station, his chest bleeding, his shape-shifting broken, and he had put himself between Kara and Alex. His hands were on the blade. His fingers were wrapped around the Kryptonite. And he was holding.

And Eliza was there.

Alex's mother stood behind her daughter. She did not touch her. She did not grab her. She just stood there and looked at the face that was not her daughter's face, and she spoke.

"Alex," she said. "Alex Danvers. Look at me. Look at me and tell me who you are."

Alex's eyes did not focus. The Kryptonite suit hummed. The sword was still aimed at Kara. And then, slowly, the eyes shifted. The blankness broke. The fog cleared. And Alex was there.

"Mom," she said.

The sword dropped.

Eliza put her arms around her daughter. Alex leaned into her. She was shaking. She was crying. She was back.

***

The broadcast went out at six in the morning.

Max had set up the equipment. Cat was at the controls. Kara stood at the microphone. She looked at the camera. She looked at the city through the window. She looked at the people — the drones, the walking, the typing, the gone — and she did not know if it would work. She did not know if hope was a signal. She did not know if the thing Alura had told her was real.

But she had no bomb. And she had no plan. And she had her sister back. And she had Cat. And she had Max. And she had to try.

"My name is Kara Danvers," she said. "And I am Supergirl. And I am standing here, in this city, with the people I love, and I am telling you something. I am telling you that you are not alone. I am telling you that there is hope. I am telling you that fear is what they want. And I am telling you that hope is stronger. I lost everything when I was young. I was sad. I was alone. And then I found love. And love is not a signal. Love is not a device. Love is the thing that is already in you. And it is the thing that will wake you up. And I am sending it to you now."

The House of El symbol appeared.

It appeared on the screen behind her. It appeared on every screen in the city. On every phone. On every monitor. On every device that had received the Myriad signal. The S-shield, glowing, bright, the symbol of hope, broadcasting on the same frequency as the control, louder than the control, stronger than the control.

It was not just a symbol. It was a thing that had been in Kara's family for generations. It was the thing that had been on her cape. It was the thing that had been on the pod that brought her to Earth. It was the thing that her mother had given her, and her aunt had given her, and her cousin had given her, and now she was giving it to the city.

And the city took it.

Winn stopped typing.

He looked at his screen. He blinked. He looked around. He saw James. He saw the bullpen. He saw the city. And he was himself.

James stopped typing. He looked at his screen. He blinked. He was himself.

The receptionist — a different receptionist, because the one from the day before was dead — stopped typing. She blinked. She was herself.

The people in the streets stopped walking. They looked around. They blinked. They were themselves.

The city came back.

***

Non was not there.

He was at his hideout, and Indigo was with him, and the Myriad signal was dead, and Non was sitting with the failure, and Indigo was standing, and her eyes were not calm.

"You have failed Astra," Indigo said.

"I failed her," Non said. "The plan failed. The city is free. And the mission is over."

"The mission is not over," Indigo said. "You did not think big enough. You wanted to unite the people. You wanted to give them purpose. And you failed. And now the only thing left is to destroy them. Kill the humans. All of them. And leave Kara behind on a dead Earth. And then we move on. To other worlds. Without the Kryptonians who would protect them. Without the Kryptonians who would stop us. And we build a new order. Without anyone to stop us."

Non looked at her. "That is genocide."

"That is survival," Indigo said. "And you are weak. And you have been weak since Astra died. And if you will not do it, I will. And I will do it without you. And you will sit in this fortress and watch the world die and you will have failed again. And this time, you will not have Astra to blame. You will only have yourself."

Non did not answer. He looked at the empty space where Astra should have been. He looked at the place where her voice should have been, where her presence should have filled the room, where the thing that had kept him going for years should have been. And it was not there. And it was never going to be there again.

And he made a choice.

***

The next morning, National City was back to normal.

And then it was not.

Max arrived at the DEO with his hands in his pockets and his face tight. He went straight to Kara and Alex, who were in the tech room with the guard log open and the ghost code log pulled up. The pattern was still there. The leak was still there. And the city was back. And the devices were still out there. And now there was something new.

"Myriad has been amplified," Max said. "The signal is back. Not the control signal. A different signal. A kill signal. It is designed to overload human brains. To cause headaches that will get worse and worse until — until they kill everyone. We have four hours. Four hours until every person in this city, and every person within a hundred miles, is dead."

Kara looked at him. "Can you find the source?"

"I have found the source," Max said. "It is at Fort Rozz. The crash site. In Nevada. The US government has been hiding it. Cloaking it. It has been there the whole time. And it is where Non and Indigo are. And it is where the Omegahedron is."

"The Omegahedron," Alex said.

"An unlimited Kryptonian power source," Max said. "It was used to power Myriad. And it is still there. And Non and Indigo are still there. And the signal is still running. And we have four hours."

"I am going," Kara said.

"You cannot go alone," Max said. "You need backup. You need Superman. You need J'onn. You need the DEO at full strength. And you have none of those. J'onn is wounded. Superman is unconscious. The DEO is depleted. And if you go alone, you will die. And if you die, the signal does not stop. And the city does not survive."

"I am going," Kara said.

"You will die," Max said.

"I will try not to," Kara said.

***

She went around the city saying goodbye.

She did not call it a goodbye. She called it a thank-you. She called it a visit. She called it seizing the day. But she went to Winn first, and she told him that his friendship had meant more to her than anything, that he had been the one who had believed in her when no one else did, that he had been the one who had held the pattern with her, and that she was glad he was her friend.

Winn looked at her. He did not know what was happening. He could guess. He did not say it. He just hugged her.

She went to Cat. She brought Cat her coffee. She stood in the office and told Cat that working at CatCo had been the honor of her life, that Cat had taught her more than anyone, that Cat had believed in her when she did not believe in herself.

Cat looked at her over the coffee. "What is this?"

"Seizing the day," Kara said.

Cat studied her. She did not push. She accepted the answer. But she looked at Kara the way a person looks at someone they care about who is saying something that sounds like a goodbye.

She went to James. She stood in his office and told him that they had missed their chance. That they could have been happy together. That she wanted him to find someone who deserved him, who appreciated him, who would not always be running toward a crisis.

James looked at her. "Why are you saying this?"

"Because I care about you," Kara said. "And I have to go."

She did not say what she was really saying. She did not say that she might not come back. She did not say that she was going to Fort Rozz to fight Non and Indigo and that the signal was a kill signal and that she had four hours and that she did not know if she would survive. She did not say it because she did not want him to know. She did not want anyone to know. She wanted them to have their ordinary lives, their ordinary days, without the weight of what she was about to do. She wanted them to eat their dinner and watch their television and go to their work and live the way people live when the crisis is not on their doorstep. And she wanted that for them, even if it meant she would not be there to have it for herself.

***

J'onn was at the DEO.

He was in the med bay. He was wounded but he was standing. He had recovered enough. And when Kara came in, he looked at her and he knew. He did not ask. He did not say it. He just looked at her and then he stood up.

"I am going with you," he said.

"You are wounded," Kara said.

"I am going with you," J'onn said. "Because you do not have anyone else. And because this is my fight too. And because I will not let you go alone."

General Lane was there. He tried to stop him. He said J'onn was still under arrest, still a national security risk, still the thing he had been pretending to be. And Lucy was there. And Lucy stood up to her father.

"He saved people," Lucy said. "He saved the city. He saved me. And you want to put him back in a cell. And that is why you drove Lois away. And it is the reason you are driving me away. And I am not going to let you do it."

General Lane looked at his daughter. And for the first time, he did not override her. He did not give the order. He stood there, and Lucy overrode him, and J'onn was free, and J'onn and Kara went to Fort Rozz.

***

Adrian was at the DEO back gate.

He had been running since the night before. He had gone to the broadcasting station. He had not made it in time for the broadcast. He had arrived after, when the city was waking up, when the drones were becoming people again, and he had stood in the alley and watched the city come back and he had felt the relief and the weight and the thing that was still running.

And then he had gone to the DEO. And he had heard what Max had said. And he had heard what Kara had said. And he had made a choice.

He went to his apartment. He opened the black case. He took out the band.

He looked at it. He held it in his hands. He thought about the seal. He thought about the ordinary. He thought about the choice he had made years ago to be human-level, to work at CatCo, to live an ordinary life.

And he thought about Kara, going to Fort Rozz alone, with a wounded Martian and a four-hour countdown and a signal that would kill every human in the city.

He unfastened the band.

The world came back. The speed, the strength, the hearing, the everything. It was the thing he had been holding back for years. The thing he had built to be ordinary. The thing that had let him work at CatCo and live in National City and be the man he wanted to be. And it was his. And he was choosing to use it.

Not because he wanted to. Not because he had been waiting for a moment like this. Because the moment had come. And Kara was going alone. And the city was going to die. And he was a Kryptonian-Daxamite hybrid with the strength to fight and the speed to move and the body that could survive what no human could. And he had been ordinary for years. And the world had needed him to be extraordinary. And he was going to be.

He went to the DEO. He walked into the tech room. He stood in front of Kara and J'onn.

"I am going with you," he said.

Kara looked at him. "You do not have to."

"I am going with you," Adrian said. "Because you are going to die if you go alone. And because I can help. And because I have been holding back for years, and this is not the moment to hold back."

J'onn looked at him. J'onn could sense the thing that Adrian was — the Kryptonian-Daxamite hybrid, the powers, the strength, the thing that had been sealed and was now unsealed. J'onn did not say anything. He just nodded.

***

Fort Rozz was in the desert.

The crash site. The thing the US government had been hiding for years. The thing that had been there the whole time. The fortress-prison that had come from Krypton, that had crashed in the desert, that had been the source of every alien threat National City had faced.

Kara, J'onn, and Adrian landed in the desert. The thing was ahead. The prison. The source. The thing that had the Omegahedron and the signal and Non and Indigo.

Non and Indigo were there. And the rest of the prisoners were in their sleep pods, preparing for the journey into space, the journey to conquer other worlds, the journey that would begin when the signal killed everyone on Earth.

"You are late," Indigo said. She was standing on the wreckage. She was smooth. She was liquid. She was the thing that could not be killed by fire. "The signal is running. The humans are dying. And you cannot stop it. And you cannot stop us."

Kara did not answer. She moved.

The fight was four against two. Kara against Non. J'onn against Indigo. And Adrian — Adrian was the third. The one who had not been in the canon plan. The one who was not supposed to be there. The butterfly. The thing that changed the outcome.

Kara and Non traded heat vision. The beams hit, reflected, intensified. Kara held. Non pushed. And then Kara found the angle, the opening, the moment, and she pushed back. Her heat vision overwhelmed his. The beam went into Non's eyes, and he collapsed. He was dead.

J'onn and Indigo fought. J'onn was wounded. J'onn was weakened. And Indigo was strong. She was about to win. She was about to kill him. And then she was distracted — she saw Non fall, and the distraction was the opening, and J'onn grabbed her, and he ripped her in two.

And Adrian was there. He had been the third. He had been the one who had held the center, who had kept the prisoners in their pods, who had watched the fight and kept the signal from being re-routed, who had done the thing that the canon plan did not account for. He had made the difference. He had been the butterfly.

And then Indigo — what was left of her — spoke. Her voice was fading. Her form was dissolving.

"You cannot stop the signal," she said. "It is not coming from the Omegahedron anymore. It is running on its own. You cannot shut it down. You cannot stop it. The only thing left is to watch the world die."

And she deactivated.

The signal kept running. The clock was at four minutes. The humans in National City were in pain. The DEO was in pain. And the signal was going to kill everyone.

"We cannot stop it," J'onn said. He was bleeding. He was leaning on the wreckage. "We cannot shut it down. We cannot —"

"We can move it," Kara said.

She looked at Fort Rozz. The prison. The thing that had crashed. The thing that was the source. If she moved it — if she lifted it out of the atmosphere, if she took it into space, where the signal could not reach Earth — the signal would stop. The humans would live.

But she would die. She would fly it into space. She would let go. And she would not be able to breathe. And she would not be able to fly back. And she would be alone in the cold, and she would die.

"I cannot let you do this," Alex said. She was on the comm. She had been listening. She had been at the DEO, watching the countdown, watching the pain, watching her sister. "I cannot let you die."

"I have to," Kara said. "It is the only way. And I am the only one who can. And I want you to promise me something. I want you to promise me that when you find Dad — when you and J'onn go to Cadmus and you find him — I want you to tell him that the Danvers gave me a good life. I want you to tell him that I never took off his glasses. I want you to tell him that I was his daughter. And I want you to live. I want you to have the life you should have had. Not as my sister. Not as the sister of a superhero. The life you should have had."

"I will not promise you that," Alex said. "Because you are coming back. You are coming back, and we are going to find Dad together, and we are going to have dinner, and you are going to be there. You are coming back."

"I do not know if I am," Kara said. "But I am going to try. And I love you. And I am lucky to be your sister."

She flew to Fort Rozz. She positioned herself beneath it. She lifted.

The thing was massive. The prison. The fortress. The thing that had crashed. She lifted it, inch by inch, and her muscles burned, and her heat vision flared, and she held, and she lifted, and the thing came off the ground, and she carried it upward, into the sky, past the clouds, past the atmosphere.

And then she let go.

Fort Rozz drifted into space. The signal died. The signal was gone. The humans in National City stopped hurting. The DEO stopped hurting. And Kara was in the cold, and she was losing consciousness, and she was floating, and she was going to die.

And then a ship came.

Kara's ship. The pod that had brought her to Earth. And Alex was at the controls.

The pod reached her. The door opened. Kara was pulled inside. The pod turned. The pod flew back, down through the atmosphere, down to National City, down to the DEO, down to the med bay, where Kara was laid on the bed and she was alive and she was breathing and she was home.

***

The next day, the DEO was whole again.

General Lane came to the med bay. He stood in front of J'onn. And he told J'onn that the President had agreed to pardon him. That he was reinstated as head of the DEO. And J'onn stood up, and he said that he would not do it alone. That he and Lucy would run it together. Co-directors.

Lucy was there. She looked at her father. And for the first time, something was different. The wall between them had a crack. And the crack was the thing that had been missing — the thing that had been driving her away, the thing that had been driving Lois away, the thing that had been General Lane's way of seeing the world as black and white, right and wrong, without the gray where people actually lived.

***

Cat called Kara into her office.

She was at her desk. She was not packing her things. She was not collecting her belongings. She was sitting behind the desk the way she always sat behind the desk. And she looked at Kara the way she had looked at her every day for a year.

"Sit down," she said.

Kara sat down.

"You are the best assistant I have ever had," Cat said. "Which is why I cannot keep you as an assistant. And which is why I am promoting you. To a position of your own. To a job that you will design. Because you have earned it. And because I have faith that you can change the world. Not as Supergirl. As Kara. And I want you to come back to CatCo, and I want you to tell me what you want to do. And I will build it around you."

Kara looked at her. "What is the job?"

"You tell me," Cat said. "That is what the job is. You tell me what you want to do, and I will give you the resources to do it. Because you are the best thing that ever happened to CatCo. And I am not going to let you walk away."

"Thank you," Kara said.

"Do not make me regret it," Cat said. And then, for the first time in a year, she said the name correctly. "Kara."

***

That evening, Kara had a dinner party.

It was at her apartment. Eliza was there, with a place setting for Jeremiah — empty, waiting, the thing they were going to fill when they brought him back from Cadmus. Alex was there. J'onn was there. Winn was there. James was there. And Adrian was there, in ordinary clothes, with the band at his wrist, the band back on, the seal back on, the choice made and the moment past.

They ate. They talked. Eliza told Alex that she had been raised by wolves, because Alex did not know table etiquette. J'onn worked the stove. Winn talked too much. James listened.

And then James pulled Kara aside. He had something in his hand. A framed photo. The photo he had taken of her — not as Supergirl. As Kara. In her glasses. At her desk. The ordinary version. The real one.

"I wanted you to have this," he said.

"Thank you," Kara said.

He leaned in. He kissed her.

And it was interrupted, not by a crisis, not by a signal, not by a device, but by Alex, who was standing in the doorway with a champagne bottle and a grin.

"Do the trick," Alex said.

Kara took the bottle. She held it in her hand. She squeezed. The cork popped. The champagne sprayed. The room laughed.

Kara raised her glass.

"To family," she said. "Because love binds us all. And because we are not alone. And because we made it. And because we will keep going."

They drank. The ordinary went on. The city went on outside the window. The lights moved across the dark. The traffic moved in the direction it always moved. And the night went on the way nights go on when the crisis is past and the people are safe and the work is not done but the work is on hold, and the people who do the work are together, and they are ordinary, and they are extraordinary, and they are alive.

Adrian sat at the table with the band at his wrist, the band back on, the seal back on, the choice made and the moment past. He had unsealed. He had gone to Fort Rozz. He had been the third. He had been the butterfly. And the outcome had changed. And Kara had come back. And the city had lived. And he was here, at the table, with the people he cared about, and the thing he had chosen to be — ordinary, extraordinary, human, Kryptonian, both, neither — was his. And he was okay.

***

And then something fell from the sky.

It was a fireball. A streak of light across the night, coming down fast, toward the city, toward the apartment, toward the thing that no one had expected.

Kara and J'onn were at the window in seconds. They saw it. They recognized it.

A Kryptonian pod. Exactly like the one that had brought Kara to Earth.

It landed. It was intact. The door was closed. And it was steaming. And it had come from the sky. And it had landed in National City. And it was the same as Kara's pod. And it was new.

Kara walked to it. She reached for the door. She did not stop. She did not hesitate. She did not think about what could be inside. She just reached for the door, and she pulled it open.

And the episode ended.
