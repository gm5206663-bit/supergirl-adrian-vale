# Chapter 11: The Sun

*Original Butterfly Chapter - The Sun*

By Tuesday, Kara couldn't lift a bus.

She tried at the DEO range first.

The target was a steel plate, the same one she had cut clean through the week before with a focused heat beam.

She focused. The beam that came out was wider than she meant it to be — ten feet across instead of two.

It didn't just cut the plate. It melted through it and the wall behind it. It left a scorched circle the size of a swimming pool in the reinforced concrete.

She wasn't out of breath after.

That was the part that scared her.

The next day she couldn't lift a bus.

She stood on the roof of CatCo and tried anyway, just to see.

The bus was one of those new ones, composite body. She put her hands under it, set her feet, and the bus didn't move.

Not even a little.

Not because it was heavy — it wasn't, not for her — but because the power that had always been there, the thing that had been a constant behind her ribs since she was nine, was quiet.

Not gone. Quiet. Like a lightbulb that was still in the socket but the circuit had been switched off somewhere upstream.

Hank had her on a Kryptonite exposure test the day after that. Small piece in a sealed case on a table outside the holding cell, green glow, light pulsing faintly, humming, green light reflecting off the glass. Kara looked at it from across the room and felt her knees go. She fell to them, breathing hard, vision blurring, throat raw.

Alex was at her side in three steps, tablet in hand. "We need to know how long you can stay near it. After the sun — you said the baseline came up. Cells remember, overcompensate. How long?"

Kara breathed hard, hands on knees, cape pooling. "Longer than last time?"

"Longer," Alex said, reading the tablet. "But still weak. How long can you stand it?"

Kara looked up at the small glowing piece on the table. It wasn't a dagger. It was a shard, the kind of thing you might carry in a pocket if you were stupid or careless. She had been both, once, and it had cost her.

Adrian stood further back. He was wearing ordinary clothes, band at wrist, human-level. He had been watching from the doorway for ten minutes without saying anything. He didn't say anything now. He just watched her breathe.

"You are sealed?" Alex asked him.

Adrian nodded once.

Kara pushed herself up off the floor, wobbling, and stood. She could stand. She couldn't fly. She couldn't lift. But she could stand.

"Adrian," she said, not looking at him. "When you built the device — when you sealed yourself — did you go to the sun?"

Adrian looked at her. He was a Kryptonian who had chosen to be ordinary. A man who had built a device with his own hands because he was tired of being extraordinary.

The question took him back. Not to the star. To the night he had built the thing. Alone, in a room with no windows, testing it against the light he could no longer safely reach.

"Once," he said. "To know if the seal would hold. Private departure, nonpublic return, route in light, no civilian disclosure."

"Can you take me?"

Adrian looked at Alex, then at Kara, then at Alex again. "Yes. Private departure, nonpublic return, route in light, no civilian disclosure. No Wraith. Just Adrian and Kara."

Alex said, "Did anyone see you? Did anyone see you take Supergirl to the sun?"

"No. Private departure, nonpublic return, route in light, no civilian disclosure. No Wraith. Just Adrian and Kara."

Alex looked at Kara. Kara looked at Adrian. There was a beat where Alex could have said no.

She could have insisted on DEO protocol. On an escort. On an entire investigation into the physiology of a Kryptonian going fully solar after a depletion event. She didn't.

"Okay," Alex said. "You went to the upper atmosphere to recharge after Red Tornado. True. Just upper atmosphere. A little higher than usual."

---

They left at night, from the CatCo roof, November cold, wind finding the gap between collar and skin. The city below was lights, no ticker, just lights.

Adrian resealed at the edge of the atmosphere. The band went back to his wrist. The human-level seal came back over him like a curtain dropping.

Then they were in the dark above the clouds. The only light was the city, reflected off the underside of the clouds and the thin cold air above them.

Kara felt the air thin. Then nothing. Then sun.

It wasn't like flying to the upper atmosphere, where the air thinned and the sky went from blue to black and you could see the curve of the Earth.

It wasn't like any flight she had ever taken.

It was falling into light. Falling into a star.

The sun wasn't a distant point above them. It was everywhere, filling the space around them. And as Kara's cells reached for it, the power that had gone quiet behind her ribs came roaring back.

Not like a switch being flipped. Like a tidal wave finding a shore it had been drawn to.

She gasped.

Her skin felt like it was burning. Her eyes stung. Her chest was the center of a fire.

She was laughing. Not because anything was funny. Because the power was back. It was *stronger* than it had ever been.

The only thing she could compare it to was the first time she had ever bled. The surprise of it. The reminder that a body that could take a missile on its shoulder could also be cut by a piece of broken glass.

Adrian held her forearm. Not pulling, not holding her back. Just guiding, the way you guide someone through a door you've been through before and they haven't. He flew supersonic beside her, counter-rotating, absorbing his own momentum so that the windows of the world below didn't shudder.

"Breathe," he said. "Cells remember. Overcompensate. Breathe."

Kara laughed again, brighter than before, and came down through the open roof hatch of CatCo, landing lightly, no wind, no sonic boom, no heat vision, no wide beam melting asphalt. Just a woman on a roof, in November cold, with a power that was humming higher than it had ever hummed and a finger that had healed and left no scar.

She looked at Adrian. He had resealed on the way down. The band was back at his wrist, human-level again, wearing ordinary clothes. The mug was waiting inside, the magnet on the fridge.

"Thank you," she said.

"You don't need to thank me," he said. "You needed sun. I know what it feels like to burn out."

---

The next morning, CatCo was the same as always. Coffee. Folders. Cat's voice on the intercom asking where her coffee was. Kara brought it with both hands and didn't spill.

But the DEO range was different.

She stood in front of the steel plate again and focused, and this time the beam came out two feet wide, clean, cutting the plate in one pass without touching the wall behind it. Alex whistled. The baseline had come up higher than before. Higher than it had been before the Red Tornado fight. Higher than it had been before she had cut her finger on the broken glass and bled for the first time.

"Cells remember," Alex said. "Overcompensate. The sun filled them and they remembered, and they overcompensated."

Kara stood there, breathing normally, and felt the power hum behind her ribs like a quiet engine. She could lift a bus now. She could probably lift two.

She went back to her desk. Adrian was at his desk — ordinary, band at wrist, human-level — as if he hadn't just flown to a star and back before Alex's shift ended. As if the thing between them — the sun, the flight, the quiet guidance, the breath-held moment in the dark above the clouds — hadn't happened.

Kara typed. Her fingers didn't ache. Her vision, even with glasses off, saw individual dust motes in Cat's office from the bullpen. She could hear Adrian's pen across the room.

She looked at him across the bullpen. He looked up. Quiet. You okay? From across the room, just a mouth movement, no words.

She nodded.

---

Winn was the first to notice.

He came in with bagels the morning after — glazed, half for Kara, half for the drawer labeled "Winn, don't eat before noon" — and stopped at her desk, bagel in hand, and just stared at her.

"What?" Kara said.

"You're... glowing," Winn said. Not a joke. He meant it literally, the way he meant it when he said her hair was different or her shirt was backward. "Like, actually glowing. Your skin. Is that sun stuff?"

Kara looked down at her hands. She wasn't glowing. She knew she wasn't glowing. She knew because she could feel the light inside her, the way you feel your own heartbeat — not visible, not obvious, just there, behind everything.

"Sun stuff," she said. "Sort of."

Winn put the bagel down. "Sort of? You went to the sun? With who?"

"With Adrian," Kara said, and Winn's face did a thing. It was the thing it did when he was trying not to have a face do a thing. "He has been to the sun before. He knew how to do it safely."

"Safely," Winn said. "The sun is a giant ball of nuclear fire and you went to it safely."

"Yes."

"Okay. Fine. Great. Super. Glad you're back and glowing and safe." He picked up the bagel again. "Don't tell Cat. She will write a headline about it."

Kara didn't tell Cat. Cat noticed anyway.

Cat came in at 9:30. Coffee order unchanged — black, no sugar, the kind of order that suggested she had been born fully caffeinated.

She looked at Kara the way she looked at everyone. A combination of appraisal and impatience.

"You look different," Cat said.

"Different how?"

"Different like you slept for forty-eight hours and woke up taller." Cat took her coffee. "Did you sleep?"

"I did," Kara said. "Not forty-eight. Maybe... twelve."

"Twelve hours," Cat said. "Lucky." She went into her office and closed the door.

James was the last. He came in with a contact sheet — the harbor rescue, the freeway, the train — and put it on Kara's desk without looking at her. Then he looked at her.

"You're okay," he said. It wasn't a question.

"I'm okay," Kara said.

James nodded. He went back to his desk. He didn't ask about the sun. He didn't ask about Adrian. He just looked at her the way he looked at her when she had first started at CatCo — carefully, patiently, like a man who was waiting for the right moment to ask the right question.

---

At the DEO, the test was more formal.

Alex had her in the kinetics lab, the same room where they had tested her strength the week she had arrived. The equipment was the same — force plates, strain gauges, a wall of sensors that read her output like a heartbeat — but this time Alex had a baseline to compare against.

"Before Red Tornado, you were at a 9.2 on the kinetic scale," Alex said, reading off the tablet. "After Red Tornado, you were at 4.1. I didn't tell you that because I didn't want to scare you."

Kara stood on the force plate. "And now?"

Alex tapped the tablet. "We will see. Try a controlled heat beam. Focused. Two feet wide. On the target."

Kara focused. The beam came out clean, two feet wide, cutting the steel plate in one pass. The sensors behind the plate registered the output.

"9.8," Alex said. "Higher than before. Cells remember. Overcompensate."

Kara breathed out. "So I'm stronger than before the fight?"

"Yes," Alex said. "You went to the sun, your cells absorbed more than they had lost, and they overcompensated. That is what happens. It isn't magic. It is biology. Your biology."

Kara looked at the force plate. She could lift a bus now. She could probably lift two. She could fly supersonic without absorbing her own momentum. She could hear a heartbeat from across a roof.

"Does Adrian know?" she asked.

"Adrian was there when you flew to the sun," Alex said. "Adrian probably knows more about your biology right now than I do."

Kara didn't answer. She went back to her desk. Adrian was at his desk — ordinary, band at wrist, human-level — as if he hadn't just flown to a star and back before Alex's shift ended. As if the thing between them — the sun, the flight, the quiet guidance, the breath-held moment in the dark above the clouds — hadn't happened.

Kara typed. Her fingers didn't ache. Her vision, even with glasses off, saw individual dust motes in Cat's office from the bullpen. She could hear Adrian's pen across the room.

She looked at him across the bullpen. He looked up. Quiet. You okay? From across the room, just a mouth movement, no words.

She nodded.

"I'm okay," Kara said.

Cat looked at her. Then Cat said, "My mother is still in town. She thinks I built this on luck. She visited. I stayed. She is difficult. But she's my mother."

Kara listened. Cat went into her office and closed the door.

"He is ordinary," Kara said. "He chooses to be."

---

That night, Kara went to the DEO roof. Adrian was there. He didn't always follow her to this roof. He didn't always say why. He just did.

They stood there. After a while, Kara said, "I kept thinking about the sun. About the light. About how it filled me. I kept thinking that I've never felt anything like that. Not even flying. Not even the first time I used heat vision. The sun was... it'd everything."

Adrian said, "I know. I've been to the sun. Once. To test the device. I went to see if the seal would hold. I didn't go because I wanted to feel the light. I went because I needed to know."

Kara said, "And did the seal hold?"

Adrian said, "Yes. The seal held. But I also felt the light. And I thought... I thought I could go back. That I could unseal, just for a minute, and feel it again. And then I thought... no. I chose ordinary. I chose the device. I chose the seal. I chose the life I've. And I'm not going back."

Kara looked at him. He looked at her. The city below. The lights. The cold.

She said, "I'm glad you chose to take me. Even though it wasn't your choice to make. Even though you could have said no. Even though you could have said it wasn't safe."

Adrian said, "It wasn't safe. You were depleted. You were weakened. You had just fought Red Tornado. You had just been tested on Kryptonite. You weren't... you weren't in a state where you should have gone to the sun. But you needed to. And I knew what it felt like to need it. So I took you."

Kara said, "Thank you."

Adrian said, "You don't need to thank me. You needed sun. I know what it feels like to burn out."

She took his hand. Not pulling. Not asking. Just taking.

They stood there. The city. The lights. The cold. The hand.

Adrian said, "You can be careful. You can also be honest. You can also choose. You can also choose to be careful and honest and also choose to kiss me. Those aren't the same thing. Those aren't opposite things. You can have both."

Kara said, "I want both."

Adrian said, "Then have both. Be careful. Be honest. Kiss me. Or don't. I won't move closer. I won't move away. I'll let you decide. That is what I've been doing. That is what I'll keep doing."

Kara looked at him. He looked at her. The city below. The lights. The cold.

She said, "I'm going to kiss you again."

He said, "I'm going to let you."

She did.

The next morning, the dock was quiet.

The water held the light the way it always held it. The boats sat in the water. The wind moved across the surface in small waves.

Kara stood on the dock. She looked at the water. She looked at the boats. She looked at the sun.

The morning went on. The city went on behind her. The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved.

She turned. She walked back to the car. She drove to CatCo. She went to her desk. She opened her email. She started working.

The chipped mug sat beside the theater mask magnet. It had been there every morning. It would be there tomorrow.

The next morning, Kara woke up early.

The sun was already coming through the window. She lay there and looked at it. The light fell across the floor and moved up the wall.

She had been out in it the day before. She had absorbed it. She had felt it fill her cells, bring her back, bring her back to herself.

She sat up. She went to the kitchen and made coffee. She drank it and looked at the window.

The sun went on outside. The city went on. The traffic moved in the same direction it always moved.

She finished her coffee. She got dressed. She picked up her bag.

She walked out of the apartment. She locked the door behind her. She went down the stairs and out into the street.

The morning was warm. The sun was warm on her face.

She walked to the car. She got in. She drove to CatCo.

At CatCo, the bullpen was quiet. Winn was at his desk. James was at his desk. The receptionist was at the front desk. Adrian was at his desk.

Kara walked to her desk and sat down. She opened her email. She started working.

She didn't think about the sun. She didn't think about the power. She thought about her work. She thought about the files on her screen. She thought about the emails she needed to answer.

She did her work.

At ten, Cat called her into the office.

"The convention center is still in the news," Cat said. "The exhibit is opening next week. Lord is building attention. And the story is getting bigger."

"He is using it," Kara said.

"He is using it," Cat said. "He is building a story. He is building public demand. He is building the narrative that the city needs him. And the story is working."

"And we're holding the pattern."

"We're holding the pattern," Cat said. "We hold the paper. We hold the names. We watch the tech room. We watch the ghost code. And we wait."

"I know," Kara said. "I'm holding. I'm waiting."

"Good. Do your work. Go home. Live your life. Wait. And when the moment comes, you tell me. And we decide together what to do."

"I'll."

Kara left Cat's office and went back to her desk.

The morning went on. The sun went on outside the window. The city went on.

The chipped mug sat beside the theater mask magnet. It had been there every morning. It would be there tomorrow.
