# Season 1, Episode 7 — Human for a Day (with sun recovery)

## Chapter 11 — The Sun

Kara kept touching the bandage.

It was a small cartoon bandage, a little dog with a cape that Winn had given her, and it covered a thin line across her fingertip that had bled for the first time in her life. The blood had stopped, but the fact of it had not.

She sat in the DEO med bay after Red Tornado, Alex scanning her hand with a device that beeped too loudly.

"Cells are still Kryptonian," Alex said, frowning at the readout. "But you burned a lot. Heat vision like that — wide, sustained, hot enough to melt asphalt ten feet wide — it takes it out of you. You were out of breath, eyes watering, throat raw. That's not normal for you."

"I know," Kara said. "I felt— empty after. Like I used everything."

"Your solar reserves are low," Alex said. "Yellow sun radiation is what powers you. You used a lot in the road rage stops, the test fight, the tornado street, then the final blast. And the cut—" She gestured. "First time bleeding means your invulnerability was down because your reserves were down. You need sun. Real sun. Not National City in November."

Kara looked at the bandage. "I can fly up. Above the clouds."

"Above the clouds is not enough. You need close. But not too close. And you are tired. Flying to the upper atmosphere while tired is not safe."

From the doorway, Adrian said, "I can take her."

Both of them looked up. He stood in the doorway with Evelyn's chipped mug in his hand, now washed again, and a folder that said *CAT GRANT EXCLUSIVE: WHO IS WRAITH?* that he had not opened.

Alex's hand went to her sidearm instinctively, then relaxed. "Adrian."

"I heard the readout," he said. "From outside. Your reserves are low. Yellow sun radiation is what recharges Kryptonian cells. National City in November gives you maybe sixty percent of what you need on a good day. After what you did—" He looked at Kara's hand. "You need more."

Kara stared at him. "You can—"

"I can take you to the sun," he said simply. Not as a boast. As a fact. "Not into it. To it. Close enough that your cells get full spectrum, far enough that you do not burn. I can get you there faster than you can get yourself right now, and I can shield you on the way."

Alex stepped between them. "You are sealed."

"I am," Adrian said. "I built it myself. Voluntary. I can release it."

The med bay went quiet. The device beeped.

Alex looked at the readout, then at him. "Your file is mostly redacted by Henshaw. What I could read— It says you were strong before. Stronger than Kara, even. That you did something on Krypton, something bad, a city? That you were caught because you were exhausted? That you built the device yourself because you wanted to be ordinary?"

"Something like that," Adrian said quietly.

"How far can you go?" Alex asked.

"Far enough to reach the sun and back before your shift ends," Adrian said. "If you let me."

Kara looked at her bandaged finger, at the small dog with a cape.

"Will it hurt?" she asked.

"No," Adrian said. "Your cells will drink it. Like water after being thirsty."

"And after— will my powers increase?"

"Yes," Adrian said. "Temporarily at first, then your baseline will be higher. Your cells remember. First time you go close after depletion, they overcompensate. You will be stronger, faster, heat vision hotter, for a while. Then it settles, but higher than before. Growing up under a red sun is harder, but you learn how to take sun without burning out."

Alex looked at Kara, then at Adrian.

"This is not DEO approved," Alex said.

"DEO thinks Wraith is outside jurisdiction," Adrian said. "This is not Wraith. This is Adrian offering to help Kara recover. No mask. No coat. Just someone who knows what it feels like to burn out and need sun."

Kara stood. The bandage crinkled.

"Okay," she said. "Take me to the sun."

Alex grabbed her arm. "Kara—"

"I trust him," Kara said. "He taught me to call for help. Now I need help."

Adrian nodded once. "We go from the roof. Private. No one sees. You wear your suit under your clothes. I will unseal there."

On the roof, National City wind cold, November sky grey, Kara in her Supergirl suit under a long coat, Adrian in ordinary clothes, no mask, no coat, just the band at his wrist.

He looked at her. "Ready?"

"Ready."

He pressed the band. It clicked, then released with a soft hiss, not a dramatic explosion, but a quiet unsealing that made the air around him shiver. For a second, nothing looked different. Then the wind stopped touching him.

His posture changed, not bigger, not bulkier — lean, harmonious, naturally defined, exceptionally handsome, balanced strength/agility/speed/flexibility/control — but the way he stood changed, as if gravity had become a suggestion.

"Hold on," he said.

Kara grabbed his forearms.

He flew.

Not like Kara flew, with effort and joy and a little wobble when she was tired. He flew like falling up. One moment roof, next moment clouds below, next moment blue thinning to black, next moment stars.

Kara gasped. The air thinned. Her lungs, Kryptonian, adapted. Her cells tingled.

They rose, Earth shrinking below, National City a light smudge, then continent, then planet. The sun grew from a coin to a plate to a furnace.

He stopped at a distance that would have melted satellites. Close enough that the sun filled half the sky, a wall of fire and light, not yellow from here but white, roaring silent.

Kara's suit, designed for Earth, would have burned. Adrian's field, not a visible shield but a manipulation of his own bio-electric aura, extended around her, filtering, letting through the yellow spectrum her cells craved, blocking the hard radiation that would kill.

"Close your eyes for a second," he said. "Let them adjust."

Kara closed her eyes. Her cells drank.

It was like water after being thirsty, but also like being plugged in after being unplugged. Her fingertip throbbed, then stopped. She peeled off the cartoon bandage. The thin line was gone. No scar.

"Oh," she whispered.

"Check your reserves," Adrian said.

Kara opened her eyes. The sun was still there, huge, and she was not squinting. Her eyes had adjusted. She could see the prominences, the flares, the granulation.

"I can see—"

"Your vision is already increasing," Adrian said. "Heat vision will be hotter. Strength higher. Speed faster. For a few days, you will be more. Then it settles, but higher than before."

Kara looked at her hands, at the light on them.

"Why are you not burning?" she asked.

"I am," Adrian said. "A little. My cells are also drinking. But I have had more practice. Growing up under a red sun teaches you how to take sun without burning out, even if it is harder in other ways."

They stayed for what felt like minutes but was maybe twenty seconds.

Kara felt her powers return, then exceed.

"Okay," Adrian said. "We should go back. Before Alex sends a search party."

He turned, Earth now a blue marble behind them, and flew back, faster than he had flown out, because Kara was now adding her own flight, not just being carried. Two Kryptonians, one hybrid, falling down toward Earth together, sun at their backs.

They re-entered atmosphere over the Pacific, a streak of light that no radar caught because Adrian's field bent it, then slowed over National City, clouds parting around them, then roof, cold wind again, November grey.

Alex was on the roof, gun not drawn but hand near it, Winn behind her with a laptop.

Kara landed first, steady, no wobble. Her eyes were brighter. The bandage was gone, finger unmarked.

Alex stared. "Your—"

"My powers increased," Kara said, and to prove it, she lifted the water tower on the next roof over with one hand, something that would have taken two hands and effort before. Now it was easy. She set it down gently, not a drop spilled. "Baseline higher. Temporarily more, then settles higher than before."

She looked at Adrian, who had resealed on the way down, the band back at his wrist, human-level again, ordinary clothes, mug waiting inside.

"You okay?" she asked him.

"Yes," he said. "Tired. Not depleted. Just— it has been a while since I went that close."

Alex looked between them, at Kara's unbandaged finger, at Adrian's ordinary posture that now she knew was a choice.

"Did anyone see you?" Alex asked.

"No," Adrian said. "Private departure, nonpublic return route, field bent light. No civilian disclosure. No Wraith. Just Adrian and Kara."

Winn whispered, "Did you just take Supergirl to the sun to recharge?"

"Yes," Adrian said.

Kara looked at her hands again, at the light.

"I can hear—" She tilted her head. "I can hear the city better. And—" She looked at Adrian. "I can hear your heart. From here. Before I had to be close."

Alex lowered her hand from her gun. "Okay. You went to the sun. You came back. Powers increased. No one saw. What do we tell DEO?"

"Tell them Supergirl went to the upper atmosphere to recharge after Red Tornado," Kara said. "Which is true. Just— upper atmosphere was a little higher than usual."

"And Wraith?" Alex asked.

"Wraith was not there," Adrian said. "Wraith is a name Cat gave a masked figure who does freeway and train. This was Adrian helping Kara recover. No mask, no coat, no reward."

From inside, Cat's voice over intercom: "Keira! Where is my coffee?"

Kara laughed, a real laugh that was brighter than before, and flew down through the open roof hatch, landing lightly.

Adrian picked up Evelyn's mug, placed it back on his desk next to the theater mask magnet, and went back to work, ordinary, ordinary, human-level, as if he had not just flown to the sun and back before Alex's shift ended.

But Kara, at her desk, typing with fingers that did not ache, with vision that could see the individual dust motes in Cat's office from the bullpen, with hearing that could hear Adrian's pen from across the room, knew that ordinary was a choice he made, and that he had chosen to share the sun with her.

Later, when she tested her heat vision in the DEO range, the beam was wider, hotter, melted the target faster, left a scorched circle fifteen feet wide instead of ten, and she was not out of breath after.

Alex whistled. "Baseline higher indeed."

Kara looked at the scorched circle, at her hands, at the place where the cut had been.

"Thank you," she said to Adrian over private channel.

"You do not need to thank me," he said. "You needed sun. I know what it feels like to burn out."

Kara watched the city through the window, powers humming higher than ever, finger healed, and thought that maybe being able to bleed and being able to go to the sun were two sides of the same thing: vulnerability and strength, and choosing when to share either.

There would be another headline tomorrow. Tonight, there was sun still in her cells, and a mug on a desk, and a friend who had taken her to a star and back and asked for nothing.
