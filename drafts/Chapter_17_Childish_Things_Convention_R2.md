# Chapter 17: The Convention

*Episode: S01E10 - Childish Things*

The convention center opened its doors at nine.

A long hall stretched from the entrance to the main floor. Booths lined the walls. Banners hung from the ceiling. A crowd moved through the space in a slow, steady stream, looking at what was on display and moving on.

Kara stood at the entrance and watched the crowd come in.

---

"This is a mess," she said to Adrian.

"It is a convention," Adrian said. He was standing beside her, his coat folded over his arm. "Conventions are always a mess. People are always a mess when they're in a room full of things they want."

"I didn't know there was a convention today."

"There is always a convention. There is always a room full of people who want something. Today it's tech. Next week it will be something else. The room changes. The people change. The wanting doesn't."

Kara looked at the crowd. "Why are we here?"

"Because Cat sent us. Because she wants to know who's at this convention. Because she wants to know who's buying what. Because she wants to know who's talking to whom."

"That isn't a reason."

"It is the reason she gave."

Kara watched the crowd. "This is going to take all day."

"It is going to take as long as it takes," Adrian said. "We're here. We do the work. We go home."

---

Cat had sent them to the convention center with a list of names and a list of booth numbers. The names were people she wanted to see. The booth numbers were places she wanted checked.

"Walk the floor," Cat had said. "Look at the booths. Look at the people. Look at who's there and who isn't. Look at who's talking to whom. Come back and tell me who was there and who wasn't."

"That is a lot of looking," Kara had said.

"It is a lot of looking. That is why I sent two people. One can look. Two can look and cross-check."

So Kara and Adrian walked the floor.

They started at the entrance and worked their way down the left wall. They looked at the booths. They looked at the people behind the booths. They looked at the people in front of the booths.

Kara checked the list. "Booth twelve. Tech hardware. The name isn't on the list."

"Booth thirteen. Software. The name isn't on the list either."

"Booth fourteen. Networking. Not on the list."

They moved on.

Adrian checked the list. "Booth twenty. Security systems. The name is on the list."

Kara looked at booth twenty. A man stood behind a display of cameras and sensors. He was talking to a woman in a business suit. He was pointing at a camera and explaining how it worked.

"That is him," Adrian said.

"That is him," Kara said. She looked at the man. He was ordinary. He was middle-aged. He had the look of a man who sold things and didn't mind selling things.

"What does Cat want to know about him?"

"She wants to know if he's talking to anyone she doesn't want him to talk to."

"Then we watch who he talks to."

They stood at the edge of the booth and watched.

The man finished with the woman in the business suit. She walked away. He turned and adjusted a camera on the display. He looked around the hall. His eyes passed over Kara and Adrian without stopping.

"He doesn't look nervous," Kara said.

"He doesn't have anything to be nervous about. He is at a convention selling cameras. That is what he does."

"Cat thinks he's doing something else."

"Cat thinks everyone is doing something else. That is how Cat works. She assumes everyone has a second job they aren't telling her about."

Kara watched the man. He picked up a brochure and set it down. He looked at the crowd. He looked at the entrance. He looked at the back of the booth.

"He is looking at the exits," she said.

"Everyone looks at the exits. It is a convention. People look at exits. People look at entrances. People look at the crowd. It is called being aware."

"He is looking at the exits more than the crowd."

Adrian watched the man for a moment. "He is. He is checking the exits and then checking the crowd and then checking the exits again."

"That isn't normal."

"That isn't normal for a man selling cameras at a convention. That is normal for a man who's waiting for something to happen and wants to know where to go when it does."

Kara looked at the man again. He had turned back to his display. He was adjusting the camera again. He looked normal. He looked like he was selling cameras.

"We need to watch him," she said.

"We're watching him," Adrian said. "We will watch him until he does something that needs watching. Or until the convention ends. Whichever comes first."

---

They moved through the crowd. They checked the rest of the booths on Cat's list. Most of the names were there. A few weren't.

"Booth thirty-one. The name is on the list. The booth is empty," Kara said.

"The booth is empty," Adrian said. He looked at the booth. It had a banner with the company name. It had a table. It had two chairs. It had nothing on the table. No display. No products. No brochures. No people.

"An empty booth at a convention isn't normal," Kara said.

"An empty booth means the company didn't show up. Or it means they showed up and left. Or it means they showed up and are somewhere else."

"Or it means they're here and they aren't at their booth."

"That is the one that matters."

Kara looked at the booth again. The banner was new. The table was clean. The chairs were empty. Nobody had been at this booth today.

"Check the name," she said.

Adrian looked at the list. "The name is on the list. Cat wants to know if he's here."

"He isn't at his booth."

"He may not be at his booth. He may be somewhere else on the floor. He may be in the back. He may be in the parking lot."

"Or he may not be here at all."

"Or he may not be here at all." Adrian looked at the empty booth. "But someone put up the banner. Someone set up the table. Someone brought the chairs. Someone set this booth up and then didn't stay at it."

"Someone set it up for someone else."

"Someone set it up so it would look like the company was here. So anyone walking past would see the banner and think the company was here. So anyone looking for the company would see the booth and move on."

Kara looked at the booth. "It is a front."

"It is a front. It is a booth that looks like a booth but isn't a booth. It is a place for a person who doesn't want to be seen to have a reason to be in the building."

"So the person is here. They're in the building. They aren't at the booth. They're somewhere else."

"They're somewhere else. And they set up the booth so nobody would look for them."

Kara looked at the crowd. The crowd moved in a slow, steady stream. It went past the empty booth without stopping.

"We need to find him," she said.

"We need to find him without him knowing we're looking for him," Adrian said. "He set up a booth that looks like a booth. He is using the convention as cover. If we walk the floor looking for him, he'll see us looking. He will know we're looking. He will leave."

"Then how do we find him?"

"We don't look for him. We let him be. We let him think he's hidden. We watch the booth. We watch who comes to the booth. We watch who looks at the booth. We watch who doesn't look at the booth but is near it."

"We watch without looking like we're watching."

"We watch without looking like we're watching."

---

They found a bench near the food court that had a view of booth thirty-one.

Kara sat on the bench and took out a brochure she had picked up at the entrance. She spread it on her lap and looked at it. She didn't look at booth thirty-one.

Adrian sat beside her and looked at his phone. He didn't look at booth thirty-one either.

They sat there. They looked at their brochure and their phone. They looked like they were waiting for something.

The convention went on around them. The crowd moved. The noise rose and fell. A food cart sold pretzels. A man in a costume walked past. A group of teenagers laughed near the entrance.

Kara's eyes moved without moving her head. She watched booth thirty-one from the corner of her vision.

The booth was empty. The banner was up. The table was clean. The chairs were empty.

Nobody came to the booth. Nobody looked at the booth. Nobody stopped near the booth.

They sat for an hour. Nothing happened.

They sat for another hour. Nothing happened.

"Maybe he isn't here," Kara said, without looking up from her brochure.

"Maybe he isn't here," Adrian said, without looking up from his phone.

"Maybe the booth is just a booth."

"Maybe the booth is just a booth."

Kara kept watching.

---

At three, a man walked past booth thirty-one.

He didn't stop. He didn't look at the booth. He walked past it at the same pace as the rest of the crowd. He had a bag over his shoulder. The bag was rectangular. It was the same shape as the bag in the footage from the fence-line camera at Lord Technologies.

He walked past the booth and disappeared into the crowd.

Kara's hand tightened on the brochure. She didn't move. She didn't look up. She kept her eyes on the brochure.

"He just walked past," she said, without moving her mouth much.

"I saw him," Adrian said.

"He didn't stop. He didn't look at the booth. He walked past it like it wasn't there."

"He walked past it because it isn't there. Not to him. It is a booth that isn't a booth. He knows that. He doesn't need to look at it."

"Where did he go?"

"He went into the crowd. He is moving through the crowd. He is going somewhere."

"We need to follow him."

"We need to follow him without him knowing we're following him." Adrian stood up. "We will follow him at a distance. We won't run. We won't hurry. We will walk at the pace of the crowd."

Kara stood up. She put the brochure in her pocket. She walked into the crowd.

Adrian followed.

They moved through the convention floor at the pace of the crowd. They didn't look ahead. They didn't look around. They kept their heads down and their pace steady.

The man with the bag was ahead of them. He was moving through the crowd with the ease of a man who knew where he was going. He didn't look back. He didn't slow down. He didn't change his pace.

He reached the far end of the hall and turned into a corridor that led to the back of the building.

Kara kept walking. She reached the corridor and turned into it. The corridor was narrower than the main hall. It had doors along the walls. It was quieter.

The man was ahead of her. He walked down the corridor and stopped at a door. He looked at the door. He looked at the corridor. He took out a key and unlocked the door.

He went in. The door closed behind him.

Kara stopped. She stood in the corridor and looked at the door.

Adrian stopped beside her. "He went in there."

"I saw him go in," Kara said.

"We need to see what's in there."

"We need to see what's in there without him seeing us."

Kara looked at the door. It was a plain door. It had a number on it. It had a lock. It had a keypad beside it.

"It is a back room," she said.

"It is a back room. It is a room that isn't on the convention floor. It is a room that people don't walk past. It is a room that someone set up to use."

"We need to get in."

"We need to get in without him knowing we got in." Adrian looked at the corridor. It was empty. "Wait here. Watch the corridor. I'll try the door."

"You can't try the door. The door is locked. The man has a key."

"The man has a key. The key is on a ring. He took it out of his pocket. He didn't hide it. He didn't check the corridor before he took it out. He isn't expecting anyone to be watching."

"He wasn't expecting us. That doesn't mean we can get in."

"We don't need to get in. We need to see who goes in and who comes out. We need to see what they carry. We need to see how long they stay."

"We need to see what's happening behind that door."

"We need to see what's happening behind that door from the outside. Because the inside is his space. He knows the inside. He doesn't know the outside. We watch the outside. We let him think he's safe inside. We see who comes and who goes."

Kara looked at the door. "We will watch."

"We will watch."

---

They watched the door for an hour.

Nobody came out. Nobody went in. The corridor was empty. The door stayed closed.

"He is inside," Kara said.

"He is inside. He is doing something inside. And he's staying inside until he's done."

"What is he doing?"

"I don't know. But I know this. He set up a booth that looks like a booth. He walked past it without looking at it. He came to a back room and locked the door behind him. He is doing something in that room that he doesn't want anyone to see."

"Like what?"

"Like meeting someone. Like receiving something. Like sending something. Like counting something. Like planning something."

Kara looked at the door. "We need to know what it's."

"We need to know what it's. But we can't go in. We can't break the door. We can't pick the lock. We can't do anything that will let him know we're here."

"Then we wait."

"We wait. We let him finish. We let him come out. We let him go back into the crowd. We follow him. We see where he goes. We see who he meets. We see what he does next."

They waited.

At four-thirty, the door opened.

The man came out. He had the bag over his shoulder. He didn't look left or right. He walked down the corridor and turned into the main hall.

Kara stood up. "He is leaving."

"He is leaving. We follow."

They walked into the main hall and followed the man at the pace of the crowd. He walked to the entrance. He went out the door. He walked to the parking lot.

Kara and Adrian followed.

The man walked to a car. He unlocked it with a key fob. He got in. He started the engine. He drove away.

Kara stopped at the edge of the parking lot. She watched the car drive away.

"We didn't get the plate," she said.

"We got the car," Adrian said. "We got the direction. We got the time. We got the place. We know he was here. We know he was in a back room. We know he came out with the bag. We know he drove away."

"We don't know what was in the back room."

"We don't know what was in the back room. But we know the back room exists. We know the booth is a front. We know the man is connected to Lord."

"We need to tell Cat."

"We need to tell Cat. We go back. We tell her what we saw. We tell her the booth number. We tell her the door number. We tell her the description of the man and the car and the direction."

"And then?"

"And then we wait. We let Cat do what Cat does. We let her make the calls. We let her pull the records. We let her find out who owns the booth and who has the key to the back room."

Kara turned away from the parking lot. "We go back."

"We go back."

They walked to their car and drove back to CatCo.

---

That evening, Kara and Adrian sat on the sofa in her apartment.

The television was on. The convention center was behind them, the parking lot behind that, the man in the car behind that.

"We found the booth," Kara said.

"We found the booth. We found the back room. We found the man."

"We didn't find what he was doing in the back room."

"We didn't find what he was doing in the back room. But we know he was doing something. And we know he didn't want anyone to see it."

"We need to go back to the convention tomorrow."

"We need to go back tomorrow. The booth will still be there. The back room will still be there. The man may come back. Or someone else may come. We watch. We see who comes and who goes."

"We watch without looking like we're watching."

"We watch without looking like we're watching."

Adrian reached across the space between them and put his hand on her wrist. The band was beneath his cuff. He was human-level. He was ordinary.

Kara closed her eyes.

The television played. The city went on outside the window.

After a while, she said, "Do you think he'll come back?"

"I think he'll," Adrian said. "He set up the booth. He used the back room. He didn't clean it up. He didn't take the banner down. He didn't remove the table. He left it all there. He will come back to use it again. Or he'll send someone else to use it again."

"And we'll be watching."

"And we'll be watching. We will watch the booth. We will watch the back room. We will watch who comes and who goes. We will watch without looking like we're watching. And we'll tell Cat what we see."

Kara nodded. She didn't say anything else. She sat there with his hand on her wrist and the television playing and the city going on outside the window.

The chipped mug sat on the desk beside the magnet, holding its corner the way it always did.

---

The next morning, Kara and Adrian went back to the convention center.

They arrived at eight-thirty, before the doors opened. They stood at the entrance with the other people who were waiting. They looked like they belonged there.

The doors opened at nine. The crowd came in. Kara and Adrian went in with it.

They walked the floor. They went past the booths. They looked at the displays. They picked up brochures. They looked like they were at a convention.

They reached booth thirty-one.

The banner was still up. The table was still clean. The chairs were still empty. The booth looked exactly the same as it had the day before.

"Nothing has changed," Kara said.

"Nothing has changed," Adrian said.

They walked past the booth and kept going. They walked the rest of the floor. They looked at the other booths. They looked at the people. They didn't look back at booth thirty-one.

They reached the food court and sat on the bench that had the view of the booth.

Kara took out a brochure. She spread it on her lap. She looked at it.

Adrian took out his phone. He looked at it.

They sat there. They watched without looking like they were watching.

The convention went on around them. The crowd moved. The noise rose and fell. A food cart sold pretzels. A man in a costume walked past. A group of teenagers laughed near the entrance.

They sat for two hours. The booth stayed empty. Nobody came to it. Nobody looked at it. Nobody stopped near it.

---

At eleven, a different man walked past booth thirty-one.

He was younger than the man from the day before. He was shorter. He had a different bag over his shoulder. The bag was rectangular. It was the same shape as the bag in the footage from the fence-line camera at Lord Technologies.

He walked past the booth without stopping. He walked past it at the same pace as the rest of the crowd. He disappeared into the crowd.

"Another one," Kara said, without looking up from her brochure.

"Another one," Adrian said, without looking up from his phone.

"Different man. Same bag."

"Different man. Same bag. Same booth. Same pattern."

"We need to follow him."

"We follow him the same way we followed the other one. We walk at the pace of the crowd. We don't run. We don't hurry. We let him think he's alone."

Kara stood up. She put the brochure in her pocket. She walked into the crowd.

Adrian followed.

They moved through the convention floor at the pace of the crowd. The younger man was ahead of them. He moved through the crowd with the same ease as the man from the day before. He didn't look back. He didn't slow down.

He reached the far end of the hall and turned into the corridor.

Kara and Adrian followed. They reached the corridor and turned into it. The corridor was narrower than the main hall. It had doors along the walls. It was quieter.

The younger man walked down the corridor and stopped at the same door as the day before. He looked at the door. He looked at the corridor. He took out a key and unlocked the door.

He went in. The door closed behind him.

Kara stopped. She stood in the corridor and looked at the door.

Adrian stopped beside her. "Same door."

"Same door," Kara said.

"Same room. Same key. Same setup. Different man."

"They're using the same room."

"They're using the same room. They're using the same booth. They're using the same pattern. They aren't hiding. They're operating in the open. They have a booth at a public convention and a back room in a public building and they walk in and out in front of everyone."

"Because nobody expects it."

"Because nobody expects it. A convention is a place where people walk around and look at things. It isn't a place where people expect to find a back room where someone is doing something they don't want seen. That is why they use it."

Kara looked at the door. "We watch the door again."

"We watch the door again. We watch who comes and who goes. We watch how long they stay. We watch what they carry."

They found the same bench near the food court. They sat down. They watched the door from the corner of their vision.

---

They watched for an hour. Nobody came out.

They watched for another hour. Nobody came out.

"He is inside," Kara said.

"He is inside. He is doing whatever the other man was doing the day before. And he's staying inside until it's done."

"We need to know what's in that room."

"We need to know what's in that room. But we can't go in. We can't break the door. We can't pick the lock. If we do any of those things, he'll know. He will tell the other man. They will change the room. They will change the booth. They will change the pattern. And we'll have nothing."

"Then we wait."

"We wait. We let him finish. We let him come out. We let him go back into the crowd. We follow him. We see where he goes. We see who he meets. We see what he does next."

They waited.

At three-thirty, the door opened.

The younger man came out. He had the bag over his shoulder. He didn't look left or right. He walked down the corridor and turned into the main hall.

Kara stood up. "He is leaving."

"He is leaving. We follow."

They walked into the main hall and followed the younger man at the pace of the crowd. He walked to the entrance. He went out the door. He walked to the parking lot.

Kara and Adrian followed.

The younger man walked to a different car from the day before. A different make. A different color. He unlocked it with a key fob. He got in. He started the engine. He drove away.

Kara stopped at the edge of the parking lot. She watched the car drive away.

"Different car," she said.

"Different car. Different man. Same pattern. They rotate. They don't use the same car twice. They don't use the same man twice. They change the things that can be changed. They keep the things that can't be changed."

"The booth. The back room. The convention."

"The booth. The back room. The convention. Those are the constants. The man and the car are the variables. They change the variables so you can't track them. They keep the constants so they can operate."

"We need to tell Cat."

"We need to tell Cat. We tell her what we saw. Two men. Two cars. One booth. One back room. One convention. We tell her the descriptions and the times and the directions."

"And then?"

"And then we wait for her to do what she does. She will pull the records. She will find out who booked the booth. She will find out who rented the building. She will find out who has the key to the back room."

"And then we'll know who they're."

"And then we'll know who they're. Or we'll know who they work for. Or we'll know the company that's paying them. And that will lead us to Lord."

They walked back to their car and drove back to CatCo.

---

Kara went to Cat's office with what they had.

Cat listened. She didn't interrupt. She sat with her hands on the desk and let Kara finish.

"We found the booth," Kara said. "We found the back room. We found two men. Two cars. One convention. They walk in, they go to the back room, they stay for hours, they come out, they drive away."

"And you followed them both times," Cat said.

"We followed them both times. We didn't get close. We didn't get the plates. We got the descriptions and the directions and the times."

"You did the right thing. You didn't get close. You didn't get the plates. You watched from a distance. You let them think they were alone. That is how you watch."

Cat leaned back. "The booth is registered to a company. The company is a shell. The shell is registered to another shell. The shells go up and up and up until they reach a name that doesn't exist. The building is rented by a company that doesn't exist. The back room is part of the rental. The key is issued to a name that doesn't exist."

"So we can't trace it."

"We can't trace it through the paperwork. The paperwork is a wall. It is a wall built to stop anyone who tries to follow the paper trail. Lord builds walls like that. He builds them everywhere."

"Then how do we find him?"

"We don't find him through the booth. We don't find him through the back room. We find him through what he's doing. The booth is a place. The back room is a place. The convention is a place. Places can be changed. Places can be moved. But the work doesn't change. The pattern doesn't change. The man who sent the yo-yo to your desk is the same man who set up the booth. The man who stood in the DEO lobby with a magazine is the same man who walks into the back room. They all work for the same person. They all follow the same pattern. We find the pattern. We find the person."

Kara nodded. "We have the pattern. We have the booth, the back room, the convention, the two men, the two cars. We have the times. We have the directions."

"And you've the yo-yos. And you've the man in the DEO lobby. And you've the footage from Lord Technologies. And you've the fire. And you've the warehouse. All of it's connected. All of it's the same pattern."

"What do we do with it?"

"We hold it. We don't go to Lord. We don't go to the booth. We don't go to the back room. We don't do anything that will let him know we're holding it. We hold it. We wait. And when the moment comes, we use it."

"When is the moment?"

"When Lord makes his move. Men like Lord don't wait forever. They build their story, and then they act. When he acts, we'll be ready. We will have the pattern. We will have the pieces. We will know what he did and how he did it and who helped him. And we'll use it."

Kara stood by the desk. "I'll hold it."

"You will hold it. You won't act. You won't move. You will hold it and wait."

Kara nodded. "I'll hold it."

She left Cat's office.

---

That evening, Kara and Adrian sat on the sofa in her apartment.

The television was on. The convention center was behind them, the parking lot behind that, the two men in two cars behind that.

"We're holding it," Kara said.

"We're holding it," Adrian said.

"We have the booth, the back room, the two men, the two cars, the times, the directions. We have the yo-yos. We have the man in the DEO lobby. We have the footage from Lord Technologies. We have the fire. We have the warehouse."

"We have the pattern."

"We have the pattern. And we aren't doing anything with it. We're holding it. We're waiting."

"We're waiting for Lord to make his move."

"And when he makes his move, we use it."

"And when we use it, we use all of it. We don't use part of it. We use all of it. The booth, the back room, the men, the cars, the yo-yos, the lobby, the footage, the fire, the warehouse. We use the whole pattern. We don't leave a piece out."

Kara leaned back against the sofa. "When will he make his move?"

"I don't know. Men like Lord take their time. They build the story. They build the pressure. They build the attention. They wait until the city is asking for them. And then they act."

"And we wait."

"And we wait. We hold the pattern. We don't act. We don't move. We wait for him to move. And when he does, we'll be ready."

Adrian reached across the space between them and put his hand on her wrist. The band was beneath his cuff. He was human-level. He was ordinary.

Kara closed her eyes.

The television played. The city went on outside the window.

After a while, she said, "Do you think he'll make his move soon?"

"I think he'll," Adrian said. "He has built the story. He has built the attention. He has built the pressure. He is waiting for the right moment. And when it comes, he'll act."

"And we'll be ready."

"And we'll be ready. We will have the pattern. We will have the pieces. We will know what he did. And we'll use it."

Kara nodded. She didn't say anything else. She sat there with his hand on her wrist and the television playing and the city going on outside the window.
