# Season 1, Episode 1 — Pilot

## Chapter 4 — What Remains

Winn was still talking into Kara’s ear.

She had lost track of his voice somewhere between the helicopter landing and the red light touching her arm. Now, with Alex securing the dressing, she heard her name again.

“Kara? Someone needs to tell me what’s happening.”

She lifted her uninjured hand to the earpiece.

“I’m here.”

“Are you okay?”

“The bleeding’s stopped. Alex is with me.”

“And Adrian?”

She looked at him.

He was standing a few feet away, watching the agents prepare Vartox for transport. When one of them changed the position of the restraints, the prisoner glanced toward him before doing exactly what he was told.

“He’s here too.”

“What was that name?”

Kara closed her eyes for a moment.

“I need to ask him some questions.”

“Right.” Winn’s voice had gone quieter. “Just call me when you can.”

“I will.”

She ended the connection before taking out the earpiece.

Alex watched her put it away.

“You told him where you were going?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

The approval was so immediate that Kara looked up.

Alex tightened the fastening on the medical kit.

“You shouldn’t have gone alone. But I’m glad someone knew where you were.”

Beyond her shoulder, Adrian spoke briefly to the agent handling the axe. He did not touch it again. The weapon went into a rigid case, separate from the prisoner.

Kara followed it with her eyes.

That blade had gone through her skin. Adrian’s hand had stopped it as easily as she might have caught a door before it closed.

She could still feel the heat he had used afterward, although the sting itself was fading.

Alex’s radio sounded. She stepped aside to listen.

When she returned, her expression had changed.

“Henshaw found a record,” she told Adrian.

He waited.

“He wants to speak to you.”

“Is Kara going back to your facility?”

“She needs a proper examination.”

“Then I’ll come.”

Alex did not move aside immediately.

“This won’t just be about what happened here.”

“I know.”

***

They transported Vartox separately.

The additional team brought equipment designed for the prisoners the DEO had spent years hunting: a reinforced transport cradle, restraints fixed to the vehicle rather than held by a pair of agents, and a containment enclosure that closed before anyone withdrew.

Adrian remained in sight until it was secured.

Vartox said nothing while they moved him. His eyes stayed on Adrian through the narrowing space at the door.

Then the enclosure shut.

Alex checked the transport team’s clearance before taking Kara toward the helicopter. Adrian followed without being escorted between armed men. That distinction did not escape any of them.

Inside, Kara found a message from Cat.

It contained her name, a question mark, and nothing else.

Adrian looked down at his own phone when it vibrated.

For one absurd second, Kara nearly laughed.

There were armed agents on either side of the landing zone. Her costume was torn. A man had tried to kill her because of her mother, and another man had saved her while revealing a name she remembered from a dead world.

Cat still expected someone to answer the telephone.

“Tell her there’s a medical emergency,” Alex said. “She can call me if she needs confirmation.”

Kara typed with one hand.

Across from her, Adrian sent a message to the person covering the guest rooms at CatCo, then wrote another to Cat.

“What did you tell her?” Kara asked.

“That I had to leave with you. And where the revised schedule is.”

Her phone vibrated again.

*I expect both of you to understand the difference between an emergency and an absence without notice.*

Below it came a second message.

*Keep me informed.*

Kara turned the screen off.

Neither of them tried to pretend it was an ordinary afternoon after that.

***

The fragment in Kara’s arm was smaller than she had expected.

Alex placed it in a tray under the examination light. Without the rest of the axe around it, it looked almost harmless.

Kara flexed her fingers, then stopped when Alex touched her wrist.

“Not yet.”

“You got it out.”

“Yes. Give me a minute to check the rest.”

The bleeding had not resumed. Adrian had done exactly what he said he would do: stopped it without closing the entire injury over whatever lay beneath.

Removing the fragment relieved a deeper pressure Kara had not realized she was feeling. The ache remained, but it no longer seemed to pull with every movement.

Alex checked the new images, examined the wound again and replaced the dressing.

“Better?”

“Yes.”

Outside the glass, Adrian stood with Henshaw.

He had not touched the band on his wrist. He was listening to the director, hands loose at his sides, while an agent held a tablet where both men could see it.

Kara could have listened too.

She looked back at the fragment instead.

“Vartox said he brought down the plane.”

Alex became very still.

“He said someone ordered it. Then they ordered him to kill me.”

“Did he name them?”

“No. He said ‘the commander.’ Adrian heard him. Winn probably did too.”

Alex reached for a recorder.

“Tell me exactly what you remember.”

Kara did.

When she finished, Alex played back the last part, asked one question about the order of the words, and sent the statement to Henshaw.

Only then did she let her hand rest against the edge of the table.

“I was on that plane,” she said quietly.

“I know.”

For a moment they were both back above the water, in different parts of the same aircraft.

Then Kara looked toward the glass.

“You knew about my mother.”

Alex followed her gaze.

“I knew she had sentenced prisoners in Fort Rozz. When you appeared on the news—”

“You knew they might come after me.”

“Yes.”

“And you told me to stop. You didn’t tell me why.”

“I wanted to keep you away from all of this.”

Kara looked down at the emblem on her chest.

“They already knew who I was.”

Alex did not have an answer that made her earlier choice harmless.

“No,” she said. “Keeping you uninformed didn’t keep you safe.”

The admission took some of the force out of Kara’s next breath, but not the hurt.

“Then don’t do it again.”

“I’ll do better.”

Kara studied her sister’s face.

She wanted something stronger than a promise. She also knew Alex could not go back and give her the warning yesterday.

“Start with that record.”

***

The recovered entry did not contain Adrian’s whole life.

It contained a name, an image, a prisoner designation and a summary of the judgment that had sent him to Fort Rozz. Parts of the accompanying material had not survived in usable form.

The important words were legible.

Destruction of an inhabited city. Mass fatalities.

Alura Zor-El’s name appeared in the judicial record.

Henshaw turned the tablet so Adrian could read it without reaching across the table.

“Is this you?”

“Yes.”

“Is the conviction accurate?”

Adrian looked at the image. He remembered the room where it had been taken, although the record did not show that room.

“Yes.”

Henshaw had brought him into a briefing room rather than a cell. The door remained open. That did not make the people outside it casual about where he stood.

“How did you leave custody?”

“The prison reached Earth. I left it.”

“You weren’t released.”

“No.”

Henshaw set the tablet down.

“Saving Kara doesn’t dispose of this.”

“I haven’t asked you to dispose of it.”

“What are you asking?”

“To know what you intend to do before you try doing it.”

The director regarded him for a few seconds.

“We’re going to establish what we can verify, what authority applies and what danger we’re dealing with. You’re not leaving here with an assurance that a false name and a job make a prison record irrelevant.”

“I didn’t expect one.”

Henshaw’s eyes moved to the band.

“Agent Danvers says Kara already knew about that.”

“I told her it restricts my abilities. I built it.”

“Is it restricting them now?”

“No.”

The agent outside shifted his weight. Henshaw did not look away.

“Will you submit it for examination?”

“I’ll answer questions about what it does. I’m not handing it over to be dismantled.”

“That isn’t your decision about every object you bring into this facility.”

“Then tell me where you want this conversation to take place.”

There was no threat in Adrian’s voice, but no suggestion that he had mistaken the conversation for permission to pretend he was harmless.

Henshaw tapped the table once.

“For now, here. You stay out of the secure areas, you don’t approach the prisoner, and someone stays with you while we check your account.”

“For this preliminary inquiry, yes.”

“Not a resolution.”

“No.”

An alert appeared on Henshaw’s tablet. He read Alex’s statement without speaking.

Then he looked up.

“Tell me what Vartox said about the aircraft.”

***

Kara found Adrian in the same room afterward.

He stood when she came in.

“Alex removed a piece of the blade,” she said.

“How does your arm feel?”

“Better.”

He nodded.

She looked at the chair across from him, then at the small light beside the room’s microphone.

“Is that on?”

“Yes,” Alex said from the doorway. “I can give you space, but this room is monitored.”

Kara considered it.

She had spent too much of the last two days learning what people knew without telling her. She did not want another conversation whose terms she had to discover afterward.

“All right.”

Alex stepped outside. Through the glass, Kara could see her take a place at the nearby console.

Adrian waited until Kara sat down.

“I remember your name,” she told him. “Not everything. I was a child.”

“You don’t have to explain what you don’t remember.”

“The record says a city.”

“Yes.”

“Tell me what happened.”

He rested his hands on the table.

For the first time since the power plant, Kara saw him hesitate over something that was not an attempt to choose the most useful answer.

“My parents were murdered.”

She remembered asking about them in the meeting room at CatCo. The short answer. The way he had looked down at his hands.

“Because they were together?”

“Yes. Some Daxamites decided the marriage was something they had a right to punish.”

His voice remained level, but the stillness around the words was effort now.

“My father came from an influential house on Krypton. My mother belonged to a collateral branch of the royal family on Daxam. Neither of those things protected them.”

Kara looked at him without interrupting.

“I went after the people who killed them,” he said. “And I destroyed a city.”

“The people there—”

“Most of them had nothing to do with it.”

She had not realized how much she wanted a different answer until he gave her that one.

He saw it. He did not try to soften what he had said.

“Did you lose control?”

“Yes. That doesn’t mean everything I did happened without choices.” He drew a breath. “I could have left. I didn’t.”

Kara remembered the axe stopping in his hand.

On Earth, she had spent years learning how little force ordinary things could bear. A door handle. A glass. Someone’s fingers closing around hers.

A city was not an ordinary thing to imagine breaking.

“You had powers on Krypton?”

“Yes.”

“Under the red sun?”

“My body developed differently. The strength came before the prison. So did damage to how I handled anger and stress.” He paused. “That explains part of what happened. It doesn’t turn everyone who died into someone who deserved it.”

Kara looked away.

Beyond the glass, Alex was listening. Her expression had become very careful.

“My mother sentenced you.”

“She heard the case. She sent me to Fort Rozz.”

“Did you hate her?”

“For a while.”

The answer hurt even though Kara had asked for it.

Adrian did not pretend otherwise.

“She was the person who could decide what happened to me next. I resented that. It was easier than thinking about what I had already decided for other people.”

“You don’t think she was wrong?”

“Not for holding me responsible.”

Kara put her uninjured hand flat against her knee.

“I’m sorry about your parents.”

“Thank you.”

“I’m not saying—”

“I know.”

He let her stop there.

For a little while, neither spoke.

The man across the table had remembered what she liked to eat. He had listened when she talked about something as small as an ice-cream shop. He had come when Winn called, and the blood beneath her dressing had stopped because he took care over where to put the heat.

None of those things had become false.

They had also stopped being enough to tell her who he was.

“Why CatCo?” she asked.

He looked surprised by the question.

“Because it was work I could do.”

“That isn’t what I mean.”

“I was tired, Kara.” His voice had lost some of its careful distance. “I wanted things I could finish. A schedule. A problem that ended when the right person received the right information. I wanted to go home without wondering what would happen if someone said my name.”

“Were you ever going to tell me?”

“Before yesterday, I didn’t know whose daughter you were.”

“After yesterday.”

He looked at her.

“I hadn’t decided how much I was willing to lose.”

Kara swallowed.

“You let me think I was getting to know you.”

“You were.”

“Only the parts you chose.”

“Yes.”

There was no answer after it to make the omission hers to forgive on his schedule.

***

Kara stood, then stopped beside the chair.

“Is there anything else about my family I should hear from you before someone else says it?”

Adrian’s gaze lifted to hers.

“Astra was in Fort Rozz.”

For a moment the room seemed to lose its edges.

“My aunt?”

“Yes. Non too.”

She sat down again without looking for the chair.

“Did you see them?”

“I spoke with both of them. In prison.”

“Are they here?”

“I don’t know where they are. I haven’t spoken to them on Earth.”

“Then they could be alive.”

“They could.”

It was not enough. It was much more than she had possessed a moment earlier.

Kara could remember Astra’s voice without effort. That made the possibility of hearing it again almost painful.

“What were they like with you?”

Adrian considered the question before answering.

“Your aunt wanted me to agree with her. Sometimes I did. Not always about the things she cared about most.”

“Were you friends?”

“I wouldn’t give you that simple an answer.”

“Was she afraid of you?”

“Yes.”

The word landed between them.

“That didn’t stop her arguing with me,” he added.

Despite everything, Kara could picture it.

“And Non?”

“He was less interested in the argument than in whether I would be useful.”

“What did you tell him?”

“No, often enough that he stopped enjoying the question.”

Through the glass, Alex reached for a fresh page on the console.

Kara noticed.

“The commander Vartox mentioned,” she said. “Could it be one of them?”

“I don’t know.”

“But you knew them.”

“In prison. That is not the same as knowing what they’re doing now.”

She held his gaze for a moment, searching for the hesitation that had preceded his earlier omissions.

He did not look away.

Kara stood again.

“I need some time.”

“All right.”

“I’m glad you came when Winn called.”

“So am I.”

She nodded once, then went to the door.

This time, he did not follow her.

***

Vartox refused to speak when Henshaw first entered the interview area.

He had been searched, separated from the transport restraints and secured inside a cell. No part of the axe had come with him.

Henshaw remained outside the barrier.

“You made statements at the power plant about an aircraft.”

Vartox watched the door behind him.

“Is he here?”

“You’re speaking to me.”

“That isn’t what I asked.”

Henshaw set a photograph where Vartox could see it through the barrier. Flight 237 rested in the water, emergency crews gathered around it.

“Who ordered you to bring this down?”

Vartox glanced at the photograph, then returned his attention to the door.

“I want to know where you’re keeping me.”

“You’re already in a cell.”

“Away from him.”

Henshaw let the request stand for a moment.

Fear had stopped Vartox from continuing his attack. It had not made him incapable of bargaining.

“You can give me information,” he said. “That doesn't buy a promise I haven't made.”

Vartox leaned back as far as the restraints allowed.

“Then you can wait.”

Henshaw picked up the photograph.

Outside, he instructed the duty team to keep the prisoner’s access and communications logged. Nobody was to bring Adrian down to frighten answers out of him.

Then he sent the aircraft statement and the recovered weapon to the appropriate teams.

There was no reason to let one prisoner decide which evidence they could work on.

***

Kara was home before she understood how tired she was.

Alex had stayed until she was satisfied with the dressing, then left to finish at the DEO. Adrian had remained there for the inquiry he had agreed to. Kara had not asked him when he would go home.

She called Winn as promised.

He asked about her arm, then listened while she told him that the man who had attacked her was in custody.

“And Adrian?”

“He’s answering questions.”

“Are you okay with him?”

Kara looked at the red fabric draped over the back of a chair.

“I don’t know yet.”

Winn did not make her turn that into an answer she could give him more comfortably.

“Okay,” he said. “I’m here.”

After the call, she sat on the couch with the room quiet around her.

She had wanted to help people. She still did.

But the world had become much larger than the space between a falling plane and the water. There were weapons that could hurt her, prisoners who remembered her mother, and a colleague whose careful kindness had existed beside a history she could barely begin to hold in her mind.

There might be family left.

She did not know whether that thought should comfort her.

When Alex knocked later, Kara let her in.

Her sister carried something wrapped in a plain cloth.

“This was recovered from your pod,” she said. “We got it working.”

Kara stood very still while Alex placed it on the table.

Light rose between them.

Her mother’s face appeared in it.

“Mom?”

The image could not hear her.

Alura spoke to the daughter she had sent away: about the life ahead of her, the strength she carried, the need to use it wisely. Her expression belonged to a moment before Krypton’s end. She did not know about National City, Cat Grant or the blood on Kara’s costume.

She could not answer a question about Astra.

Kara listened anyway.

By the time the recording ended, Alex had moved beside her.

“I used to think I knew what she would say,” Kara whispered.

“About what?”

“Everything.”

Alex waited.

“Now I want to ask her about someone she put in prison. And about her sister. And I can’t.”

“No.”

Kara wiped her face with her uninjured hand.

“Adrian told me what he did.”

“I heard.”

“He also saved my life.”

“I know.”

“I don't know what to do with both of those things.”

“You don't have to decide everything about him tonight.”

The projector’s light had faded. Kara kept looking at the place where her mother had stood.

“What if there’s always someone stronger?” she asked.

Alex sat beside her.

“Would that have made you leave the plane?”

Kara shook her head before she had thought about it.

“Then that part hasn’t changed.”

For a while, they sat without speaking.

Finally Kara looked down at her arm.

“I need to learn how to fight someone who can hurt me. Not just how to stand in front of a gun.”

Alex nodded.

“We can work on that.”

“And if you know something that changes what I’m walking into, you tell me.”

“Yes.”

Kara leaned back against the couch. Beside her, Alex stayed.

***

Far from the apartment, a man reported that Vartox had missed his check-in.

He had waited until he had more than silence to bring to his commander. An observer had passed the power plant and seen human teams removing damaged equipment under guard. No one had obtained a clear view of the prisoners or the people who had fought there.

Astra listened without interrupting.

“The girl?” she asked.

“Not confirmed.”

“Vartox?”

“No contact.”

The man waited, then offered, “We could send someone else.”

“To do what?”

“Finish the assignment.”

Astra turned toward him.

“We don't know why he failed.”

The man lowered his eyes.

“Find out who took him,” she said. “Before we give them another prisoner.”

After he left, Astra remained beside the table.

The image on its screen showed the woman who had brought the aircraft down safely, the emblem of her house visible on her chest.

Alura’s daughter.

For now, that was the only familiar face Astra knew had entered the fight.
