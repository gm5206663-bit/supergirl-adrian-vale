# Chapter 28: Better Angels

*Episode: S01E20 - Better Angels*

The sword was coming.

Kara did not move. She did not raise her hands. She did not block. She stood there with the Kryptonite blade aimed at her chest and the person holding it was her sister and was not her sister, and she let the sword come, because she would not fight her sister, and because if she had to choose between dying and hurting Alex, she would choose dying every time.

The blade stopped.

Not because Kara stopped it. Because something else did.

J'onn was there. He had dragged himself to the broadcasting station, his chest bleeding, his shape-shifting broken, and he had put himself between Kara and Alex. His hands were on the blade. His fingers were wrapped around the Kryptonite. And he was holding.

And Eliza was there.

Alex's mother stood behind her daughter. She did not grab her. She did not pull her. She stood there and looked at the face that was not her daughter's face, and she spoke.

"Alex," she said. "Alex Danvers. Look at me. And tell me who you are."

Alex's eyes did not focus. The Kryptonite suit hummed. The sword was still aimed at Kara. And then, slowly, the blankness broke. The fog cleared. And Alex was there.

"Mom," she said.

The sword dropped.

Eliza put her arms around her daughter. Alex leaned into her. She was shaking. She was crying. She was back.

***

The broadcast went out in the early morning, when the city was still dark and the people were still walking in the rhythm, and Kara stood at the microphone with Cat at the controls and Max behind the equipment, and she did not know if it would work.

She did not know if hope was a signal. She did not know if the thing Alura had told her was real. She did not know if the people on the other side of the screen could hear her or if they were too far gone to hear anything.

But she had no bomb. She had no plan. She had her sister back. And she had to try.

"My name is Kara Danvers," she said. "And I am standing here, in this city, with the people I love, and I am telling you something. I am telling you that you are not alone. I am telling you that there is hope. I am telling you that fear is what they want, and that hope is the thing that is already in you, and that it is stronger than any device."

She did not give a speech. She did not make a plan. She did not try to be eloquent or clever or strategic. She just said the thing she knew, the thing she had known since the day she arrived on Earth, the thing that had been the reason she had become Supergirl in the first place, and she said it the way she would say it to anyone, the way she would say it to Winn or to James or to Alex, the way a person says the thing they believe when they do not have anything else left to say.

The House of El symbol appeared.

It appeared on the screen behind her. It appeared on every screen in the city. On every phone. On every monitor. On every device that had received the Myriad signal. The S-shield, glowing, bright, the thing that had been on her cape, the thing that had been on the pod that brought her to Earth, the thing her mother had given her and her aunt had given her and her cousin had given her.

It was not a pattern. It was not a signal. It was a choice. And it was the one thing that a control device could not replicate, because a control device could force a mind to think as one, but it could not force a mind to choose.

And the city chose.

Winn stopped moving. He blinked. He looked around. He saw the bullpen. He saw James. He saw the city. And he was himself.

James stopped moving. He blinked. He was himself.

The people in the streets stopped walking. They looked around. They blinked. They were themselves.

The city came back.

***

Non was at the hideout, and the signal was dead, and the city was free, and he was sitting with the failure, and Indigo was standing, and her eyes were not calm.

"You failed Astra," she said.

"I failed," Non said. "The plan failed. The city is free. And the mission is over."

"The mission is not over," Indigo said. "You did not think big enough. You wanted to unite the people. You wanted to give them purpose. And you failed. And now the only thing left is to destroy them. Kill the humans. All of them. And leave Kara behind on a dead Earth. And then we move on to other worlds. Without the Kryptonians who would protect them. Without the Kryptonians who would stop us. And we build a new order, and no one is left to stop us."

Non looked at her. "That is genocide."

"That is survival," Indigo said. "And you have been weak since Astra died. And if you will not do it, I will. And you will sit in this fortress and watch the world die, and you will have failed again."

Non did not answer. He looked at the empty space where Astra should have been. The place where her voice should have been. The thing that had been keeping him going for years, and it was not there, and it was never going to be there again.

And he made a choice.

***

The next morning, the city was back to normal. And then it was not.

Max arrived at the DEO with his hands in his pockets and his face tight. He went straight to Kara and Alex. The guard log was open on the desk. The ghost code log was pulled up. The pattern was still there. The leak was still there. And the city was back. And the devices were still out there. And now there was something new.

"The signal is back," Max said. "Not the control signal. A different signal. A kill signal. It is designed to overload human brains. To cause pain that will get worse and worse until it kills everyone. We have four hours. Four hours until every person in this city, and every person within a hundred miles, is dead."

Kara looked at him. "Can you find the source?"

"I have found the source," Max said. "It is at Fort Rozz. The crash site. In Nevada. The US government has been hiding it. Cloaking it. It has been there the whole time. And it is where Non and Indigo are. And it is where the Omegahedron is."

"The Omegahedron," Alex said.

"An unlimited Kryptonian power source," Max said. "It was used to power Myriad. And it is still there. And the signal is still running. And we have four hours."

"I am going," Kara said.

"You cannot go alone," Max said. "You need backup. You need J'onn. You need the DEO at full strength. And you have none of those. J'onn is wounded. The DEO is depleted. And if you go alone, you will die. And if you die, the signal does not stop. And the city does not survive."

"I am going," Kara said.

"You will die," Max said.

"I will try not to," Kara said.

***

She went around the city before she left.

She did not call it a goodbye. She called it a thank-you. She went to Winn first, and she told him that his friendship had meant more to her than anything, that he had been the one who had believed in her when no one else did, that he had been the one who had held the pattern with her, and that she was glad he was her friend.

Winn looked at her. He could guess what was happening. He did not say it. He just hugged her.

She went to Cat. She brought Cat her coffee. She stood in the office and told Cat that working at CatCo had been the honor of her life, that Cat had taught her more than anyone, that Cat had believed in her when she did not believe in herself.

Cat looked at her over the coffee. "What is this?"

"Seizing the day," Kara said.

Cat studied her. She accepted the answer. But she looked at Kara the way a person looks at someone they care about who is saying something that sounds like a goodbye.

She went to James. She stood in his office and told him that they had missed their chance. That they could have been happy. That she wanted him to find someone who deserved him, who would not always be running toward a crisis.

James looked at her. "Why are you saying this?"

"Because I care about you," Kara said. "And I have to go."

She did not say the rest. She did not say that she was going to Fort Rozz, that the signal was a kill signal, that she had four hours, that she did not know if she would come back. She did not say it because she did not want them to carry it. She wanted them to have their ordinary lives, their ordinary days, without the weight of what she was about to do.

She went to the DEO last. She walked through the bullpen and saw the people at their desks, the guard log open, the ghost code log pulled up, the pattern still there, the leak still there. She saw the paper. She saw the names. She saw the thing she had been holding for weeks, and she knew that she might not be coming back to hold it, and she did not say that either.

She stood in the tech room and looked at the console. She looked at the screen. She looked at the thing that had been her work for weeks, the pattern, the paper, the names, the leak, and she thought about all of it — the nights in the corridor, the watches at the gate, the waiting, the holding, and she thought that the waiting had been the work, and the holding had been the work, and the breaking had been the work, and all of it had been worth it, because the city was still here, and the people were still here, and she had done her part.

***

J'onn was in the med bay at the DEO. He was wounded, but he was standing. He had recovered enough. And when Kara came in, he looked at her, and he knew. He did not ask. He stood up.

"I am going with you," he said.

"You are wounded," Kara said.

"I am going with you," J'onn said. "Because you do not have anyone else. And because this is my fight too. And because I will not let you go alone."

General Lane was there. He tried to stop him. He said J'onn was still under arrest, still a national security risk, still the thing he had been pretending to be. And Lucy was there. And Lucy stood up to her father.

"He saved people," Lucy said. "He saved the city. He saved me. And you want to put him back in a cell. And that is why you drove Lois away. And it is the reason you are driving me away. And I am not going to let you do it."

General Lane looked at his daughter. And for the first time, he did not override her. He did not give the order. Lucy overrode him, and J'onn was free.

***

Adrian was at the back gate of the DEO when he heard what Max had said. He had been running since the night before — to the broadcasting station, to the DEO, through the city while it was waking up, through the ordinary that had come back and the weight that had not.

He had not made it to the broadcasting station in time for the broadcast. He had arrived after, when the city was becoming people again, and he had stood in the alley and watched the city come back and he had felt the relief and the thing that was still running.

And then he had heard about the kill signal. And the four hours. And Kara going to Fort Rozz alone.

He went to his apartment. He opened the black case. He took out the band.

He looked at it. He held it in his hands. The thing he had built himself, on Krypton, in the rage, in the exhaustion, in the desire to be ordinary. The thing that had kept him human-level for years. The thing that had let him work at CatCo, live in National City, be the man he had chosen to be. The thing that had been his choice, every day, for years, and that he had never once regretted.

Until now.

Not because he regretted the choice. Because the choice had been made for a world that was not this world. He had chosen ordinary because ordinary had been enough. Because ordinary had been the thing he had wanted, the thing he had built, the thing that had let him be the man he was. But this world was not ordinary. This world had a signal that was going to kill every human in it in four hours, and the woman who had been carrying the pattern, carrying the paper, carrying the names, was going to Fort Rozz to fight it, and she was going to die if she went alone.

And he could go with her.

And he thought about Kara. Going to Fort Rozz. Alone. With a wounded Martian and a four-hour countdown and a signal that would kill every human in the city.

He unfastened the band.

The world came back. The speed, the strength, the hearing, the everything. Not a surge. Not a burst. The thing he had been holding back for years, and it was his, and he was choosing to use it.

He went to the DEO. He walked into the tech room. He stood in front of Kara and J'onn.

"I am going with you," he said.

Kara looked at him. "You do not have to."

"I am going with you," Adrian said. "Because you are going to die if you go alone. And because I can help. And because I have been holding back for years, and this is not the moment to hold back."

J'onn looked at him. J'onn could sense what Adrian was — the Kryptonian-Daxamite hybrid, the powers, the strength, the thing that had been sealed and was now not sealed. J'onn did not say anything. He just nodded.

***

Fort Rozz was in the desert. The crash site. The thing the US government had been hiding for years. The fortress-prison that had come from Krypton and had crashed and had been the source of every alien threat National City had faced.

Kara, J'onn, and Adrian landed in the sand. The thing was ahead. The prison. The source. The thing that had the Omegahedron and the signal and Non and Indigo.

Non and Indigo were there. And the rest of the prisoners were in their sleep pods, preparing for the journey into space, the journey that would begin when the signal killed everyone on Earth.

"You are late," Indigo said. She was standing on the wreckage. She was smooth. She was liquid. She was the thing that could not be killed by fire. "The signal is running. The humans are dying. And you cannot stop it."

Kara did not answer. She moved.

The fight was three against two. Kara against Non. J'onn against Indigo. And Adrian — Adrian was the third. The one who had not been in the plan. The one who was not supposed to be there.

Kara and Non traded heat vision. The beams hit. They reflected. They intensified. Kara held. Non pushed. And then Kara found the angle, the opening, the moment, and she pushed back. Her heat vision overwhelmed his. The beam went into Non's eyes, and he collapsed. He was dead.

J'onn and Indigo fought. J'onn was wounded. He was weakened. And Indigo was strong. She was about to win. And then she was distracted — she saw Non fall, and the distraction was the opening, and J'onn grabbed her, and he ripped her in two.

And Adrian was there. He had been holding the center. He had been watching the prisoners in their pods, making sure none of them woke, making sure none of them came out, making sure the signal was not being re-routed, making sure the thing that was running the signal was not being restarted. He had been moving through the wreckage, fast, quiet, the thing that had been sealed for years and was now not sealed, and he had been doing the thing that no one had planned for, the thing that the plan did not anticipate, the thing that changed the mechanics without changing the outcome.

He had been the difference. Not the hero. Not the one who killed the villain. The one who held the center, who kept the things that should have woken from waking, who did the work that no one saw and no one would remember, and that was the work that mattered.

And then what was left of Indigo spoke. Her voice was fading. Her form was dissolving.

"You cannot stop the signal," she said. "It is not coming from the Omegahedron anymore. It is running on its own. You cannot shut it down. You cannot stop it. The only thing left is to watch the world die."

And she deactivated.

The signal kept running. The clock was at four minutes. The humans in National City were in pain. And the signal was going to kill everyone.

"We cannot stop it," J'onn said. He was bleeding. He was leaning on the wreckage. "We cannot shut it down."

"We can move it," Kara said.

She looked at Fort Rozz. The prison. The thing that had crashed. The thing that was the source. If she moved it — if she lifted it out of the atmosphere, if she took it into space, where the signal could not reach Earth — the signal would stop. The humans would live.

But she would die. She would fly it into space. She would let go. And she would not be able to breathe. And she would not be able to fly back.

"I cannot let you do this," Alex said. She was on the comm. She had been listening. She had been at the DEO, watching the countdown, watching the pain, watching her sister. "I cannot let you die."

"I have to," Kara said. "It is the only way. And I want you to promise me something. When you find Dad — when you and J'onn go to Cadmus and you find him — I want you to tell him that the Danvers gave me a good life. I want you to tell him that I never took off his glasses. I want you to tell him that I was his daughter. And I want you to live. I want you to have the life you should have had. Not as my sister. Not as the sister of a superhero. The life you should have had."

"I will not promise you that," Alex said. "Because you are coming back. You are coming back, and we are going to find Dad together, and we are going to have dinner, and you are going to be there. You are coming back."

"I do not know if I am," Kara said. "But I am going to try. And I love you. And I am lucky to be your sister."

She flew to Fort Rozz. She positioned herself beneath it. She lifted.

The thing was massive. The prison. The fortress. The thing that had crashed. She lifted it, inch by inch, and her muscles burned, and her heat vision flared, and she held, and she lifted, and the thing came off the ground, and she carried it upward, into the sky, past the clouds, past the atmosphere.

And then she let go.

Fort Rozz drifted into space. The signal died. The humans in National City stopped hurting. And Kara was in the cold, and she was losing consciousness, and she was floating, and she was going to die.

And then a ship came.

Kara's ship. The pod that had brought her to Earth. And Alex was at the controls.

The pod reached her. The door opened. Kara was pulled inside. The pod turned. It flew back, down through the atmosphere, down to National City, down to the DEO, down to the med bay, where Kara was laid on the bed and she was alive and she was breathing and she was home.

***

The next day, the DEO was whole again.

General Lane came to the med bay. He stood in front of J'onn and told him that the President had agreed to pardon him. That he was reinstated as head of the DEO. And J'onn stood up and said that he would not do it alone. That he and Lucy would run it together.

Lucy was there. She looked at her father. And for the first time, something was different. The wall between them had a crack.

***

Cat called Kara into her office.

She was at her desk. She looked at Kara the way she had looked at her every day for a year.

"Sit down," she said.

Kara sat down.

"You are the best assistant I have ever had," Cat said. "Which is why I cannot keep you as an assistant. And which is why I am promoting you. To a position of your own. To a job that you will design. Because you have earned it. And because I have faith that you can change the world. Not as Supergirl. As Kara. And I want you to come back to CatCo, and I want you to tell me what you want to do. And I will build it around you."

"What is the job?" Kara said.

"You tell me," Cat said. "That is what the job is. You tell me what you want to do, and I will give you the resources to do it. Because you are the best thing that ever happened to CatCo. And I am not going to let you walk away."

"Thank you," Kara said.

"Do not make me regret it," Cat said. And then, for the first time in a year, she said the name correctly. "Kara."

***

That evening, Kara had dinner.

It was at her apartment. Eliza was there, with a place setting for Jeremiah — empty, waiting, the thing they would fill when they brought him back from Cadmus. Alex was there. J'onn was there. Winn was there. James was there. And Adrian was there, in ordinary clothes, the band at his wrist, the band back on, the seal back on, the choice made and the moment past.

They ate. They talked. Eliza told Alex that she had been raised by wolves, because Alex did not know table etiquette. J'onn worked the stove. Winn talked too much. James listened.

And then James pulled Kara aside. He had something in his hand. A framed photo. The photo he had taken of her — not as Supergirl. As Kara. In her glasses. At her desk. The ordinary version. The real one.

"I wanted you to have this," he said.

"Thank you," Kara said.

He leaned in. He kissed her.

And it was interrupted, not by a crisis, not by a signal, not by a device, but by Alex, who was standing in the doorway with a champagne bottle and a grin.

"Do the trick," Alex said.

Kara took the bottle. She held it in her hand. She squeezed. The cork popped. The champagne sprayed. The room laughed.

Kara raised her glass.

"To family," she said. "Because love binds us all. And because we are not alone. And because we made it. And because we will keep going."

They drank. The ordinary went on. The city went on outside the window. The lights moved across the dark.

Adrian sat at the table with the band at his wrist. He had unsealed. He had gone to Fort Rozz. He had been the third. He had held the center. He had been the difference. And the outcome had changed. And Kara had come back. And the city had lived. And he was here, at the table, with the people he cared about, and the thing he had chosen to be — ordinary, extraordinary, human, Kryptonian, both, neither — was his. And he was okay.

He looked at Kara across the table. She was laughing at something Winn had said. She was ordinary. She was extraordinary. She was the woman who had carried the pattern and the paper and the names, and who had flown a prison into space, and who had come back, and who was sitting at her own dinner table, eating her own dinner, with her own people, and she was alive.

He looked away. He picked up his glass. He drank. The ordinary went on. And he was okay.

***

And then something fell from the sky.

It was a fireball. A streak of light across the night, coming down fast, toward the city, toward the apartment, toward the thing that no one had expected.

Kara and J'onn were at the window in seconds. They saw it. They recognized it.

A Kryptonian pod. Exactly like the one that had brought Kara to Earth.

It landed. It was intact. The door was closed. And it was steaming. And it had come from the sky. And it was the same as Kara's pod. And it was new.

Kara walked to it. She reached for the door. She did not stop. She did not hesitate. She did not think about what could be inside. She just reached for the door, and she pulled it open.

And the episode ended.
