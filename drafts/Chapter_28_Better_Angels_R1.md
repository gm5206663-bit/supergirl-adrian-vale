# Chapter 28: Better Angels

*Episode: S01E20 - Better Angels*

The next morning, Kara did not go to CatCo.

She went to the DEO. She arrived at seven, before Alex, before Winn, before anyone else. She went straight to the tech room and stood in front of the guard log.

The paper was there. The stack of handwritten pages. Each one checked. Each one verified. The names. The times. The walk-throughs. The pattern.

She picked up the top page. She read it. Then she set it down and picked up the next one.

She was still there when Alex arrived at seven-thirty.

"You are early," Alex said.

"I did not sleep," Kara said.

Alex looked at her. Then at the stack of paper. Then back at Kara.

"You are going to move," Alex said.

"I am going to move," Kara said. "Today. Not tomorrow. Not next week. Today."

Alex did not argue. She sat down. She pulled the guard log toward her and opened it.

"The leak walks the corridor at one-forty in the morning," she said. "Every time. Same time. Same rounds. The person comes through the back gate. Walks to the tech room. Comes back at two-fifteen. And the cameras show nothing."

"The paper shows the walk," Kara said.

"The paper shows the walk," Alex said. "And the paper shows the name. The name is on the last page. I wrote it down. I wrote it down two weeks ago. And I have been waiting. And I am done waiting."

She turned the page. She pointed at the name.

Kara looked at it. The name she had been holding. The name she had been carrying in her pocket, folded into the paper, reading every night, reading every morning, not using it because she was waiting for the right moment.

The right moment was now.

***

They called Winn at eight.

He arrived at the DEO with a coffee in his hand and his eyes already sharp. He had been watching the traffic monitors all morning, waiting for another pattern, waiting for Lord to activate the next device. He had not seen one. But he knew it was coming.

"We are moving," Alex said.

Winn set his coffee down. "When?"

"Today," Kara said. "Tonight. At one-forty."

"The leak walks the corridor at one-forty," Winn said. "We know the pattern. We know the name. We know the time. And we are going to be there when the person walks."

"We are going to be there," Alex said. "All of us. Kara and me in the corridor. You in the tech room. Adrian at the back gate. And when the person comes through, we use the pattern. We use the paper. We use the name. And we stop them."

"And then we go to Lord," Kara said. "With the name. With the pattern. With the proof. And we stop him."

Winn nodded. He picked up his coffee. He finished it in one long sip. He set the cup down.

"I will be in the tech room," he said.

***

Adrian was at CatCo when Kara called him.

He was at his desk with his work open. He picked up his phone. He listened. He did not say anything for a moment.

"You are moving tonight," he said.

"Tonight," Kara said. "At one-forty. The leak walks the corridor. We are going to be there. And we are going to use the pattern."

"And you want me at the back gate."

"I want you at the back gate. The leak comes through the back gate. If the leak sees us in the corridor before they reach the tech room, they will run. If they run, they will go through the back gate. And if you are there, you stop them."

Adrian was quiet. Kara could hear the bullpen in the background — the typing, the phones, the ordinary work of an ordinary day.

"You know what this means," Adrian said.

"I know what this means," Kara said.

"It means the seal comes off."

"It means you make a choice," Kara said. "And the choice is yours. I am not asking you to unseal. I am asking you to be at the back gate. If the leak runs, you stop them. If you can stop them human-level, you stop them human-level. If you cannot, then you make the choice. And whatever you choose, I will not think less of you."

Adrian was quiet for a longer moment.

"I will be at the back gate," he said.

***

The plan was simple. Three points. One time.

At one-forty in the morning, the leak would walk through the back gate. Adrian would be there. If the leak stopped, Adrian held them. If the leak ran, Adrian stopped them.

The leak would walk the corridor. Kara and Alex would be in the corridor, in the dark, without the cameras, with their eyes. They would follow the leak to the tech room. They would not confront them. They would not speak. They would not move until the moment was right. They would wait until the leak opened the tech room door and went inside. Then they would close the door behind them.

Winn would be in the tech room. He would be at the console. He would have the ghost code log pulled up. He would have the footage from every night the leak had walked. He would have the paper. He would have the name. And when Kara and Alex closed the door, he would lock it. From the inside. With the leak inside. With Kara and Alex inside. With the proof on the screen.

And then they would use it. All of it. The name. The pattern. The paper. The footage. The ghost code. The walk-through log. Everything they had been holding for weeks. Everything they had been waiting for. And they would go to Lord.

"And when we go to Lord," Kara said, "we go with everything. Not just the name. Not just the pattern. All of it. The devices. The locations. The information about Marcus. The information about the grid. Everything the leak knows. Everything we have found. We go with the whole story. And we make sure he knows he cannot build another chapter."

"He will build another chapter," Winn said.

"Then we will read it," Kara said. "And we will break it. The same way we broke the traffic. The same way we broke the devices. We read it. And we break it. And we do it again. And again. And eventually he stops writing."

Alex looked at her. "You have thought about this."

"I have thought about this every day for weeks," Kara said. "I have been holding the pattern. And I have been thinking about what the pattern means. And the pattern means that we do not have to win in one day. We just have to hold. And break. And hold. And break. And keep holding. Until he stops."

***

Kara spent the rest of the day at the DEO. She went through the guard log one more time. She checked every name. She checked every time. She checked every walk-through. She verified the pattern against the footage. She verified the footage against the paper.

The pattern was solid. The name was solid. The time was solid.

She went to the roof at noon. The city went on below. The traffic moved. The ordinary went on. And somewhere in the grid, on some rooftop, in some building, another device was running. Or waiting to be turned on.

She closed her eyes. She let her hearing extend across the city. She let her energy sense extend across the grid. She looked for the other devices. She looked for the pressure that Adrian had felt on the rooftop.

She found two.

One on the west side. A building near the highway. A rooftop equipment box, the same as the one she had found the day before. She could feel it — the pressure, the hum, the thing that was running.

The second was on the east side. A different building. A different rooftop. The same pressure. The same hum. The same thing running.

She marked the locations in her mind. She would find them. She would break them. But not today. Today was for the leak. Today was for the pattern. Today was for Lord.

***

At CatCo, Cat called Kara into her office at three.

"You are not at your desk," Cat said. She was not looking up from her tablet. She was reading something. She did not sound angry. She sounded like she was noticing.

"I am at the DEO," Kara said.

"You have been at the DEO all day."

"I have."

Cat looked up. She studied Kara's face. Then she set the tablet down.

"You are going to do something," she said.

Kara did not say anything.

"You are going to do something that you have been preparing for," Cat said. "Something that has been building for weeks. And you are going to do it tonight. And you are not going to tell me what it is. Because you do not tell me the things that involve the DEO. And that is correct. That is what you should do."

Kara nodded.

"But I am going to tell you one thing," Cat said. "Whatever it is, whatever you are going to do, whatever comes of it — there will be a story. And it will be told. And I would rather it be told by the people who were there than by the people who were not. So if you need me, if you need anything from CatCo, you call. And I will provide it. And I will not ask what it is for."

"Thank you," Kara said.

Cat picked up her tablet. She went back to reading. "Do not make me regret that," she said.

Kara stood there. She looked at Cat behind the desk, the woman who had given her a job, who had given her a place, who had never asked for more than she could give. Cat was not her mother. Cat was not her sister. Cat was her boss. And Cat was standing behind the desk, reading her tablet, not asking, not pushing, just letting Kara do the thing she needed to do.

That was the kind of person Cat was. And that was the kind of person Kara was trying to be.

She left the office and went back to the DEO.

***

Adrian left CatCo at four. He went to his apartment. He opened the black case that sat in the closet.

The band was inside. The sealing device. The thing he had built himself, on Krypton, in the rage, in the exhaustion, in the desire to be ordinary. The thing that had kept him human-level for years. The thing that had let him work at CatCo, live in National City, be the man he wanted to be.

He picked up the band. He turned it over in his hands.

He did not open the case. He did not take the band out. He just held the case and looked at it.

He thought about the back gate. He thought about the leak running. He thought about the choice. He thought about Kara's voice on the phone — "I am not asking you to unseal. I am asking you to be at the back gate."

He closed the case. He set it back in the closet. He did not need to decide now. He would decide at the gate. At one-forty in the morning. When the moment came.

He went to the kitchen. He made coffee. He sat at the table. The city went on outside the window. The evening went on.

He waited.

***

At midnight, Kara arrived at the DEO.

Alex was already there. Winn was already there. They were in the tech room with the console lit up, the guard log open, the ghost code log pulled up, the footage queued.

Adrian arrived at twelve-fifty. He came through the back entrance. He did not go to the tech room. He went to the back gate.

The night was cold. The wind moved through the alley. The city lights moved across the dark. The ordinary went on — the traffic, the people, the buildings, the things that did not know what was happening in the dark behind them.

Adrian stood at the back gate. He was wearing ordinary clothes. The cuffs were down. The band was at his wrist. Human-level.

He waited.

***

At one-forty, the figure came through the gate.

The figure was a person. Not a monster. Not a shadow. A person in ordinary clothes, walking the way people walk when they are doing their job and do not want to be seen. The person crossed the alley. The person walked toward the corridor.

Adrian moved.

He did not unseal. He moved human-level. He was fast, but he was not faster than a human who was running. He stepped into the path. He raised his hands.

"Stop," he said.

The figure stopped. The figure looked at him. The figure's eyes went wide. The figure turned and ran.

Adrian moved. Human-level, he was not fast enough. He reached for the figure's arm and missed. The figure was already past him, running toward the corridor.

Adrian made the choice.

He grabbed the band. He unfastened it. He pulled it from his wrist.

The world came back.

The speed. The strength. The hearing. The everything. It was not a surge. It was not a burst. It was the thing he had been holding back for years, and it was there, and it was his, and he was choosing to use it.

He moved.

He was at the figure's side in a fraction of a second. He reached out and caught the figure's arm. He did not squeeze. He held. He stopped the run without breaking anything.

The figure struggled. The figure pulled. Adrian held.

"You are going to stop," he said. His voice was even. It did not rise. It did not fall. "You are going to walk back to the gate. And you are going to come with me. And you are going to talk to the people who are waiting for you."

The figure stopped struggling. The figure looked at him. The figure's face was pale.

"You are not going to run," Adrian said. "Because I am not going to let you. And because you know the pattern is over. You have been walking this corridor for weeks. And the paper has your name. And the paper does not lie."

The figure's eyes went wider. The figure looked toward the corridor. The figure looked toward the tech room.

"They are waiting for you," Adrian said. "In the tech room. With the paper. With the name. With the pattern. And they are going to use it. And you are going to talk. Because if you do not, they will use it without you. And you will be in the story anyway. Only you will not have a say in how it ends."

The figure was quiet. Then the figure nodded.

Adrian walked the figure back to the gate. He walked the figure through the alley. He walked the figure to the tech room door.

Alex was there. She opened the door. She looked at the figure. She looked at Adrian.

"You are early," she said.

"The leak ran," Adrian said. "I caught them."

***

Inside the tech room, Winn was at the console. Kara was standing beside him with the guard log in her hands.

The figure stood in the doorway. The figure looked at the screen. The figure looked at the paper. The figure looked at Kara.

"You know who I am," the figure said.

"I know your name," Kara said. "It is on the paper. I have been holding it for weeks. And I have been waiting. And I am done waiting."

The figure looked at the screen. The ghost code log. The footage. The walk-through records. The pattern. Everything Winn had pulled up, everything that had been built over weeks of watching, of writing, of waiting.

"It was Marcus," the figure said. "Marcus opened the door. Marcus let me in. Marcus fed me the information. Marcus is the one who told Lord where to put the devices."

"I know," Kara said.

The figure looked at her. "And you are going to use it."

"I am going to use it," Kara said. "All of it. The name. The pattern. The paper. The footage. The ghost code. The walk-through log. The information about Marcus. And the information about the devices. All of it. And I am going to Lord. And I am going to stop him."

The figure closed their eyes. The figure's shoulders went down. The figure had been holding something for weeks. And the thing was finally off.

"I am sorry," the figure said.

Kara looked at the figure. She did not say "it is okay." She did not say "I forgive you." She did not say anything that was not true.

"I am not going to forgive you," she said. "I am going to use what you gave me. And I am going to stop Lord. And that is what is going to happen. And you are going to help. Because you know where the devices are. And you are going to tell us. All of them. Every one. And then you are going to do what you need to do. And the law will do what the law does."

The figure nodded. "I will tell you. All of them. There are three. One on the west side. One on the east side. And one at the power station. The main grid. If he turns on the one at the power station, he can control the whole city. Not just traffic. Everything. Power. Communications. Emergency systems. Everything."

Kara looked at Alex. Alex was already at the console, pulling up the locations, sending the locations to the DEO team.

"We will get them," Alex said. "All three. Tonight."

***

At two-thirty, Kara, Alex, and Adrian were at the west side building.

The building was quiet. The security system was off. Alex had disabled it from the van in the alley. Adrian stood at the entrance, watching the street. Kara went up.

The rooftop was the same as the one from the day before. The equipment box. The Kryptonian lock. The thing that was running, pressing against the air, humming in the wires.

She broke it. The crystalline components shattered. The pressure was gone. The hum was gone. She came down.

"One," Alex said.

At three, they were at the east side building.

The same thing. The same rooftop. The same box. The same device. Kara broke it. She came down.

"Two," Alex said.

At three-thirty, they were at the power station.

This one was bigger. This one was connected to the main grid. This one was the one that could take the whole city.

Kara went in. She found it. She broke it. She came out.

The city went dark.

Not the whole city. Just the power station. The lights went out. The grid went down. The traffic lights went dark. The signals went dead.

And then the backup systems kicked in. The generators. The emergency power. The city came back, piece by piece, the way a city comes back when something has gone wrong and the people who run it are doing their jobs.

Kara stood at the edge of the power station. The city went on outside. The lights came back. The ordinary came back.

"It is done," she said.

"It is done," Alex said.

Adrian stood beside her. He was holding the band in his hand. He had resealed on the way down. The band was back at his wrist. Human-level. Ordinary.

"You unsealed," Kara said.

"I unsealed," Adrian said.

"You chose to."

"I chose to."

"And you are okay."

Adrian looked at her. He did not say yes. He did not say no. He just stood there. The city went on. The wind moved. The night went on. The lights moved across the dark. The ordinary went on the way it always went, not knowing what had just happened, not knowing what had been chosen, not knowing that a man who had chosen to be ordinary for years had just chosen, for a moment, to be something else.

"I am okay," he said.

***

At four, Kara went to Lord Technologies.

She did not go through the front door. She went to the roof. She landed on the building. She walked to the lab.

Maxwell Lord was there. He was at his desk. He was reading something. He looked up when she came in.

He did not look surprised.

"You found the devices," he said.

"I found the devices," Kara said.

"And you broke them."

"I broke them."

Lord set his pen down. He leaned back. He looked at her the way he always looked at her — the way a man looks at something he has been building, something he has been planning, something he thought he could use.

"You have the pattern," he said. "You have the paper. You have the name. You have the devices. You have everything. And you are here. And you are going to tell me that it is over."

"It is over," Kara said.

"It is not over," Lord said. "I have been building this for months. I have the information. I have the technology. I have the map. I have the devices. You broke three. I can build three more. You found the leak. I have other people. You stop me today. I start again tomorrow. It is not over."

Kara looked at him. She did not raise her voice. She did not argue. She did not rush. She just stood there and let him say what he was saying, the way you let a thing finish before you answer it.

"You are wrong," she said.

"I am not wrong."

"You are wrong about one thing," she said. "You think this is about the devices. About the technology. About the map. About the information. It is not. It is about the city. And the city is not yours. And the people in the city are not yours. And you cannot build a story with the city as the pages if the city does not let you."

Lord looked at her.

"You broke my devices," he said.

"I broke your devices," Kara said. "And I am going to keep breaking them. Every one you build. Every one you put in the grid. Every one you hide on a rooftop. I will find them. And I will break them. And you will keep building. And I will keep breaking. And eventually you will run out of rooftops."

Lord was quiet.

"And you will not have the city," Kara said. "Because the city has me. And it has Alex. And it has Winn. And it has Adrian. And it has people you do not know about, doing things you do not see, holding patterns you cannot break. You are not alone in this. You are alone. And you have been alone the whole time. And you did not know it."

Lord looked at her. His eyes were flat. His face was still.

"What are you going to do?" he said.

"I am going to do what I have been doing," Kara said. "I am going to hold the pattern. I am going to hold the paper. I am going to hold the names. And I am going to watch. And when you build the next device, I will find it. And when you find the next leak, I will use it. And I will keep doing that. Every day. Until you stop."

She turned and walked out.

She did not look back.

***

At five, Kara went home.

Non was at the kitchen table. He looked up when she came in.

"It is done," she said.

"It is done," Non said.

"The devices are broken. The leak is caught. Lord knows we are coming for him. And he cannot stop us. Because he is alone. And we are not."

Non nodded. "And you are okay."

"I am okay," Kara said.

She sat at the table. She looked at the broken device on the counter — the pieces from the day before, the pieces she had not given to Alex yet. She picked up a piece. She turned it over in her hand.

The city went on outside. The morning was coming. The traffic was starting. The ordinary was starting.

She set the piece down.

She had the pattern. She had the paper. She had the names. She had the devices. She had the people she trusted.

And she had stopped him. Not for good. Not forever. But she had stopped him. Today. And she would stop him tomorrow. And the next day. And every day after that.

That was the work. The ordinary work. The work of holding the pattern. The work of watching. The work of breaking the things that should not be there. The work of being the person who stands in the dark and does not let the city become someone else's story.

She sat there. The city went on. The morning went on. The sun came through the window and fell across the floor. The traffic started. The buses started. The people started their days, the way people start their days, without knowing what had happened in the dark, without knowing what had been broken, without knowing that someone had been holding the pattern and had held it long enough to use it.

She was not alone.

She had never been alone.

And she would keep going.

She would hold the pattern. She would watch the grid. She would find the things that should not be there. She would break them. And she would do it again the next day. And the day after that. Because that was the work. The ordinary work. The work of standing in the dark and not letting the city become someone else's story.

She picked up the piece of the device again. She looked at it. Then she set it down.

The morning went on. The city went on. And she went on with it.
