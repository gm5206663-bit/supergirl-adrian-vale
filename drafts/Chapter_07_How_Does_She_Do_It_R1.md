# Season 1, Episode 4 — How Does She Do It?

## Chapter 7 — How Does She Do It?

The drone followed her for three blocks before Kara decided it was not a hobbyist losing control of a toy.

It kept a steady distance, adjusted when she adjusted and held its position when she stopped above the river to look back at it. She reached for it. It tilted and moved away, faster than the model she had seen in the electronics department at CatCo.

She caught it over the water.

At the DEO, Alex turned it over in her hands while Henshaw watched.

“Not ours,” he said.

“I know,” Kara said. “I thought you should see it anyway.”

Alex pried open the casing. Inside, the components were compact and unfamiliar. No markings, no serial.

“Whoever built this wasn't trying to save money,” she said.

Kara looked toward Henshaw. “Could it be—”

“If Astra had this kind of surveillance in place before we brought her in, we would have seen it earlier,” he said. “We are still reviewing what her people left behind. For now, assume it belongs to someone who is interested in you, not her.”

The word *interested* landed differently after a week of holding rooms and second checkpoints. Astra was alive and contained. So were the two guards who had come with her, the Hellgrammite, Vartox and the man who had called himself Reactron. Keeping them separated took people. Reviewing the data they had carried took more.

“Keep the channel open,” Henshaw told Kara. “If it comes back, we want to know.”

***

At CatCo, Winn was answering Kara's desk phone with the expression of someone who had been given a second desk phone as a reward.

“CatCo, Kara Danvers' desk— no, she is in a meeting— yes, I will tell Cat you called to congratulate her—” He covered the receiver when Kara arrived. “Everyone wants to tell her she finally beat Lois Lane.”

Kara took the messages. “She won?”

“The Siegel Prize. Women in media. Ceremony in Metropolis tomorrow.” Winn handed her a stack. “She was going to cancel. Her mother won't watch Carter.”

Kara looked toward Cat's office. Through the glass, Cat was reading something on her tablet with the particular stillness that meant she was deciding whether to be pleased or to find a reason not to be.

Adrian set a folder on the edge of Kara's desk. Inside were the travel requirements, car, security and a note that the award presentation overlapped with the debut of Lord Technologies' new maglev train.

“Metropolis is tomorrow evening,” he said quietly. “The train event is tonight.”

“Does she know about the train?”

“She knows it exists. She does not know we were asked to cover it.”

Cat called Kara in before she could finish sorting the messages.

“I have been informed that I have won a prize,” Cat said.

“Congratulations.”

“For a story about Supergirl. Which means Lois Lane will be in the audience tomorrow, watching me receive it.” She set the tablet down. “My mother has decided that grandmothers who live in the same state are decorative. Carter is here. I am supposed to be in Metropolis to accept it.”

Kara thought about the drone, the holding corridor and the way Alex had said *assume it belongs to someone who is interested in you*. Then she thought about the boy who had been mentioned only in passing until now.

“I can watch him,” she said.

Cat looked at her.

“I know I am—” Kara stopped. “I know you need someone you trust. I will be here. He will be safer with me than with—”

“Than with Supergirl?” Cat finished. “You said he would be safer with you than with Supergirl.”

Kara felt her face warm. “Than with a stranger.”

For a moment Cat studied her as if checking whether there was an angle hidden in the offer. Then she nodded once.

“You will pick him up from school. You will bring him here. You will not feed him anything that comes in a color not found in nature. And you will not tell him that his mother is nervous.”

“I won't.”

Cat's mouth almost moved. “That was the correct answer.”

Outside, Adrian handed Kara the school address and the time.

“You are sure?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Then I will move the car for the train event to later. If you need to leave the office, call Winn first.”

He did not tell her not to take on more than she could handle. Alex would do that soon enough.

***

Carter Grant was smaller than Kara had expected and more serious.

He sat on the edge of the chair in Cat's office with his backpack on his knees and looked at her without smiling.

“You're Kara,” he said.

“I am. Your mom asked me to take you home tonight.”

“Because she won a prize.”

“Yes.”

He considered that. “Do you like Supergirl?”

Kara had prepared for questions about snacks and homework. She had not prepared for that one.

“I do.”

“Why?”

“She helps people.”

Carter nodded as if she had given an answer that could be graded. “My mom says she is trying. That is different from helping?”

“Trying is part of helping.”

He seemed to accept that. On the way out of the building, he asked whether she played Settlers of Catan. Kara said she had played once.

“My mom says I am almost as good as she is,” Carter said.

“I believe it.”

At home, she made him a sandwich and let him set up the board on the table. He played with a concentration that made it easy to forget he was nervous. When Alex called, Kara stepped into the other room.

“We have a location on the first bombing,” Alex said. “Subsidiary of Lord Enterprises. Lab that Knox worked in.”

“Ethan Knox?”

“Former employee. Fired two weeks ago. Reported missing by family. We are still pulling his records.”

Kara looked toward the living room, where Carter was arranging resource cards.

“I will be there after I drop him—”

“Kara.” Alex's voice softened. “You are supposed to be watching him. We have people on the lab.”

“I know. I just—”

“Call if you need us. Do not try to be in three places at once.”

She returned to the board. Carter had already decided on his first settlement.

Later, when she walked him to the window to show him the city, a second alert came in. This time the building was occupied.

She set the board aside, told Carter to stay by the door and left by the balcony.

The building across town was already smoking when she arrived. Inside, a device ticked against a support column. She lifted it, flew it clear over the water and watched it explode. The force knocked her backward. For a moment the sky and sea reversed.

Then cold water closed over her.

***

She woke in a chamber at the DEO with Alex's hand on her arm.

“You are all right,” Alex said. “You hit the water hard.”

Kara tried to sit. “Carter—”

“Winn has him. He is in Cat's office playing video games on her monitor array. He thinks you had to take a call.”

The chamber hissed and opened. Henshaw stood beyond it, looking at a tablet. When he glanced up, his eyes caught the light.

Kara blinked. For half a second she thought she saw red.

Then he looked away and the color was gone.

“We have a face,” Henshaw said. “Security footage from Lord's lab. Ethan Knox. Worked in the first lab that was attacked.”

Alex helped Kara out. “He was fired. Family says he has a daughter who is sick.”

“Is that why he is doing this?” Kara asked.

“We are asking Lord the same question,” Henshaw said.

At CatCo, Carter and Winn were indeed playing video games on Cat's monitor array. Winn looked up with relief when Kara came in.

“Where were you?” Carter asked.

“Work call,” Kara said. “Did you win?”

“Winn let me win.”

“I did not,” Winn said, but he was smiling.

Kara thanked him and took Carter to get food. In the café, Lucy Lane found her.

“Hey,” Lucy said. “You are Kara, right? James's friend?”

Kara nodded.

“I heard you were watching Cat's son. That is nice of you.” Lucy hesitated. “Can I ask you something? About James and Supergirl?”

The question was careful, but Kara heard what it was really asking.

“They work together,” Kara said. “He admires her.”

“Does he—” Lucy stopped. “Sorry. That is not fair to ask you.”

“It is all right. He is trying to figure out how to be here without being there. If that makes sense.”

Lucy smiled a little. “It does, actually.”

Upstairs, Alex and Henshaw were in Maxwell Lord's office. Lord listened to Alex describe the bomb with the expression of someone who had already read the report and wanted to see whether she had.

“The location that was attacked was a subsidiary,” Lord said. “We have many. That narrows nothing.”

“It narrows motive,” Alex said. “Knox worked there. He was fired.”

“People are fired every day. They do not all build bombs.”

“His daughter is sick,” Alex said.

Lord's gaze sharpened. “And you think that makes me responsible?”

“I think it makes you someone who should not hold a public event tonight with a bomber still out there.”

Lord smiled. “I think it makes me someone who will not be cowed by a terrorist. The train will run.”

On the way out, Henshaw told Alex to stay with Lord's security team. Alex told him she would, and then called Kara.

“Lord is going ahead with the debut,” she said. “We have a second team on the train. And Kara— you need to focus. Even the Girl of Steel might break if she tries to be everywhere.”

Kara looked at Carter, who was showing Winn how to trade sheep for ore.

“I know.”

***

Winn returned to Cat's office with pizza and found it empty.

“Carter?”

The backpack was still by the chair. The monitors showed a news report about the train station. Supergirl might be there because of the bombings, the anchor said.

Winn called Kara.

“He is gone. I looked away for a minute— he said he wanted to meet Supergirl—”

Kara was already moving toward the window. “Which station?”

“National City Central. The maglev debut.”

At the same time, a call came in about a bomb at the airport. Police had found a device near the departures level.

Kara stopped with one hand on the sill.

“Carter is on the train,” Winn said. “I think Knox is too.”

Alex's voice came through the other channel. “We have the airport device. James is here— Lucy was supposed to be on a flight back to Metropolis—”

Kara closed her eyes for half a second. Two places. Two bombs. One of them had a child in it who had asked her why she liked Supergirl.

“I will take the train,” she said. “You take the airport. Call Adrian for the train— Carter is Cat's son.”

Alex did not argue. “On it.”

The call reached Adrian while he was checking the route for Cat's car to Metropolis. He listened, asked for the platform and the direction the train would leave, then took his case to the private room he knew was empty.

The seal released beneath his fingers. The black suit, gloves, boots and mask followed, the coat settling around him as he checked that nothing of his ordinary clothes remained visible. He left by the service route and accelerated toward the central station.

The maglev train was already boarding when he arrived. Through the windows, he could see Carter near the front, talking to a man in a Lord Technologies jacket who was helping him onto the train to find his parents.

Adrian landed on the far side of the platform, out of the direct line of the station cameras, and moved toward the train without drawing attention to the coat.

Inside, Winn had followed Carter to the platform but had been stopped at the gate. He saw Adrian pass and did not call out. Adrian nodded once and kept going.

On board, the man in the Lord jacket— Maxwell himself, Kara realized when she arrived a moment later— was escorting Carter toward the middle cars.

“Carter!” Kara said, catching up.

Carter turned. “Supergirl!”

She knelt. “You need to move to the back with the others. Can you do that for me?”

“Is there a bomb?”

“There is someone we need to talk to. I need you to be brave and go with Mr. Lord and my friend to the back.”

Maxwell looked at her, then at the masked figure who had appeared at the end of the car.

“This is not part of the demonstration,” he said.

“No,” Kara said.

Adrian moved past them toward the front, checking the couplings as he went. The train began to move.

In the front car, Ethan Knox stood with his back to the door, a vest beneath his jacket, wires visible at his collar. When Kara opened the door, he flinched.

“Stay back.”

“I am not here to hurt you,” Kara said. “I know about your daughter.”

His hands tightened. “No one was supposed to know it was me.”

“I know you are trying to help her.”

“You do not know anything.” His voice cracked. “They said if I did this, she would get treatment. If I stopped, she wouldn't.”

“Who said?”

He shook his head. “It does not matter now. People know it was me. I cannot face her if they know it was me.”

Kara stepped closer. Behind her, Adrian was guiding passengers toward the rear, his movements precise, without the shoving that would have caused panic. Carter was among them, holding onto Maxwell's sleeve and looking back at her.

“Think about her,” Kara said. “She needs you.”

“She needs me not to be the man who—” He stopped. “No one was supposed to be on this train except—”

He looked toward the passengers, toward Carter, and his face changed.

“I am sorry,” he said.

He pressed down on a plunger.

A timer began to count. Thirty seconds.

Kara did not waste them arguing.

“Everyone to the back!” she shouted. “Now!”

Adrian was already there, bracing the door to the next car while people moved through it. His build was lean, not bulky, but when he set his feet the car steadied.

Kara ran to the coupling between the engine and the passenger cars. Through the floor, she could see the track blurring.

She looked at Knox. He was standing in the engine car alone now, hands at his sides.

“You can still—”

“Save them,” he said.

She used her heat vision on the coupling. Metal glowed, then parted.

The engine surged forward with Knox inside it. The rest of the train began to slow as Kara dug her feet into the track bed, sparks rising around her boots. Adrian moved to the rear, adding his weight and precise leverage to keep the cars from jackknifing.

Outside the city, over the water where the new bridge ended in open air, the engine exploded. Light filled the windows, then faded.

The train stopped.

For a moment, no one spoke. Then Carter's voice came from the back.

“Did we—”

“We did,” Kara said, breathing hard. “Everyone is all right.”

She turned to Adrian. He nodded once and stepped toward the side exit, leaving by the service ladder before the station cameras could find a clear angle on the mask.

***

At the airport, Alex found the bomb in a service corridor. Police had cleared the area, but James was still there, holding Lucy's bag while she argued with an officer that she needed to get to her gate.

“Lucy, you need to move back,” James said.

“I am not leaving—”

Alex cut in. “You both need to move back.”

Henshaw arrived, looked at the device and told Alex to get clear.

“Hank, no—”

“Go.”

Alex pulled James and Lucy back. From behind the barrier, she saw Henshaw kneel beside the bomb. His hands closed around the casing. His eyes glowed red as he peeled it open and reached inside.

The light inside the bomb went out.

Henshaw stood, holding the disarmed components, and walked back toward them.

“It was a dud,” he said. “Meant to distract from the train.”

Alex stared at the device in his hands. James stared at Henshaw.

Later, at the DEO lab, Alex turned the bomb canister over. Inside, beneath the wiring, was a small switch.

“A kill switch,” she said. “Remote failsafe. You do not put a failsafe on a fake bomb.”

Kara had returned from the train station, Carter handed off to Winn and Maxwell. She looked at the switch, then at Henshaw, who was reviewing footage of the train explosion.

“Why would Knox kill himself if his daughter is sick?” Kara asked. “How do bombings help a sick girl?”

Alex did not answer immediately. She was thinking about the drone, the way Lord had insisted the train run and the way Henshaw had told her to run before reaching into the bomb with his bare hands.

“We need medical records,” Kara said. “For Knox's daughter.”

***

Cat returned from Metropolis the next morning to find Carter asleep on the couch in her office and Winn asleep in a chair beside him.

She woke Carter first.

“Mom!” He sat up. “I met Supergirl! She was amazing. She stopped the train—”

“I heard,” Cat said, holding him. “Were you scared?”

“No. She was there.” He looked toward the window. “She is so much prettier in person.”

“She is also smart,” Cat said. “And brave. And kind. And a hero. Do not reduce her to her legs, Carter.”

He grinned. “I said her heart, Mom.”

Cat looked at Winn, who was pretending to still be asleep.

“Ms. Grant—” Kara began from the doorway.

Cat held up a hand. “Not now. Later, you and I will have a conversation about what it means to be spread too thin. For now, take him to school. He has already missed enough.”

Kara nodded. Outside, Adrian was sorting the morning deliveries. He had changed privately and re-engaged the seal before returning to his desk; the closed case was back in his work bag.

“How is he?” Adrian asked.

“Good. He wants you to know he is almost as good at Settlers of Catan as Cat.”

Adrian almost smiled. “I will add it to his file.”

Kara leaned against her desk. “The train—”

“You saved everyone you could.”

“I did not save Knox.”

“No.”

She looked down at the messages waiting for her. “Winn found something. Lord paid for his daughter's care.”

Adrian stopped sorting. “Did Lord admit it?”

“Not directly.”

That evening, Kara went to Lord's balcony. He was watching the city, a glass in his hand.

“Mr. Lord.”

He turned. “Supergirl. I was wondering when you would come.”

“I know about Ethan Knox's daughter. You paid for her treatment.”

“I have a foundation. We pay for many treatments.”

“You paid for his daughter's treatment and he planted bombs for you.”

Lord smiled. “That is a fascinating theory. Unprovable, but worth exploring.”

“You were testing me,” Kara said. “The drone, the bombs— agility, strength, speed. How does she do it?”

“An interesting question, is it not? How does one person do so much?” He set the glass down. “I have failsafes for every situation, Supergirl. Every experiment needs a control.”

“People died.”

“One man died because he made a choice. I did not press the plunger.”

Kara stepped closer. “You forced him to.”

“I offered a father a chance to save his child. What he did with that chance was his.”

“Then why a kill switch on a dud at the airport?”

Lord's smile did not waver. “You will have to ask the people who found it. Perhaps they are not as honest as they seem.”

She looked at him for a long moment, then turned to leave.

“This isn't over,” she said.

“No,” Lord agreed. “It isn't.”

At CatCo, Cat called Kara into her office.

“You asked me how I do it,” Cat said.

Kara remembered asking, days ago, before the bombings and the train.

“You have it all,” Kara had said. “How?”

Cat looked toward the couch where Carter had slept.

“You start small and work your way up,” she said. “You adapt. You learn which fires to put out first. And you accept that you will disappoint someone, no matter what you choose.”

Kara thought about the airport and the train, about choosing Carter and trusting Alex and Henshaw, about Adrian's quiet presence at the end of the car while she cut the coupling.

“I chose the train,” Kara said.

“You chose the child who was in front of you,” Cat said. “That is not a failure. That is a decision.”

Outside, James and Lucy were sitting on the edge of his desk, talking quietly. Lucy was going back to Metropolis in the morning, but tonight she was here.

Kara watched them for a moment, then went to find Alex.

At the DEO, Alex showed her the kill switch again.

“Hank said it was a dud,” Alex said. “But this—”

“I saw his eyes,” Kara said quietly. “When I woke up in the chamber. I thought I imagined it.”

Alex did not tell her she had imagined it.

“We need to know what happened to Jeremiah,” Alex said. “Eliza said—”

“I know what she said.” Kara looked toward the holding corridor, where Astra's room was dark. “We have a lot of people in custody. And someone out there who thinks testing me is a game.”

Alex nodded. “Non is still out there too.”

Kara thought about her aunt's words: *He won't wait forever for word from me.*

“We will find him,” Kara said.

She left the lab, checked her phone and saw a message from Winn: Carter wanted to know whether Supergirl liked pizza.

She smiled despite herself and typed back that she did.

Somewhere below, a siren began to rise. She listened long enough to hear the direction, then picked up her operations line and called it in before changing.

There would be another headline tomorrow. Tonight, there was work still waiting to be done.
