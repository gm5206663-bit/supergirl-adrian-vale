# Chapter 16: Childish Things R1 - 4000w+ Full Proper Prose Not Rushing

The morning started with a yo-yo.

Not the kind from a toy aisle. The news clip showed a prison yard at Van Kell Maximum Security, concrete and chain-link, guards moving in a routine that had been routine for six years. Then a man in his late fifties with neat hair and a soft voice flicked his wrist, and the yo-yo left his hand on a string that was not string but braided steel. The edge caught the light. It was a circular saw blade, balanced, sharpened, spinning at three thousand RPM. It cut through a security camera, through a lock housing, through a guard's baton as he raised it, and returned to the man's palm with a soft whirr.

Winslow Schott Sr. did not run. He walked.

By the time the second alarm sounded he was already at the gate, and the gate was already open because the yo-yo had cut the chain at the exact link that held tension.

Kara saw it on the break room television at CatCo. She was holding a chipped mug that had been there since Evelyn left. The mug had a theater mask magnet stuck to its side now, because Evelyn's niece had given it to her mother as a thank-you for covering a shift, and Evelyn had left it on the counter when she moved her things. Adrian had placed it back next to the coffee machine the morning after, ordinary, human-level, as if he had not lifted a beam off a car the night before.

Winn walked in, saw the television, and went pale in a way Kara had never seen. He reached for the remote and turned it off with a hand that shook.

"Winn?" Kara asked.

"It's nothing," he said, too fast. "Prison break. Old news."

Adrian was at his desk in the bullpen, hearing Winn's pen stop scratching. He could hear the heartbeat from across the room, a fast irregular thump, and the way Winn held his breath. He did not look up. Ordinary was a choice he made, and part of ordinary was not staring when someone wanted to not be seen.

Ten minutes later two people in dark suits arrived. FBI. Agent Cameron Chase, sharp eyes, sharper suit, the kind of person who had read every file before she walked into a room and still asked questions as if she had not.

"Winn Schott Jr.," she said. "We need to ask you about your father."

The bullpen went quiet. Cat was not yet in, but her office door was open and the light was on, and James was at his desk with a contact sheet, and Lucy was by the elevator with a resume folder because Cat had offered her a job the week before and she was deciding.

Kara tried not to listen. She failed. Kryptonian hearing in a quiet office is not a thing you can turn off without practice, and she had not practiced enough because the sun was still in her cells from the trip with Adrian and everything was louder.

"My father is Winslow Schott Sr.," Winn said, voice low, as if saying it quietly would make it less true. "He is the Toyman. He has been in Van Kell for six years for six counts of murder. I have not spoken to him since I was eleven."

Agent Chase said, "He broke out at 0600 with a weaponized yo-yo. He killed two guards on the way out. He has not used his bank accounts. He has not contacted his old associates. The only person he has ever called from prison, once a year on your birthday, is you, and you never picked up."

Winn's hands were fists on his desk. "Because I hate him."

"Why?" Chase asked, not unkindly, but directly.

Winn looked at the desk, at the chipped mug that was not his, at the theater mask magnet, at the monitor that was angled away from the door because he had closed the private Wraith clips after Alex told him private means private and Winn had learned that some things are not for showing Cat.

"Because he was my best friend," Winn said. "When I was a kid he made toys. Not for money, for me. He made a robot that could walk and a doll that could say my name. He worked for Chester Dunholtz at Schott Toys. Dunholtz stole his designs. All of them. The jumping jack that became a bestseller, the talking bear, everything. My dad was... he was not a brave man. He was cowardly. He bottled things up. He did not yell, he did not fight. He just... held it. And one day he snapped. He put a bomb in a teddy bear and mailed it to Dunholtz's office."

Kara's breath caught. Across the room, Adrian's pen stopped.

"The assistant opened it," Winn said. "Not Dunholtz. The assistant and five other people. Six counts. I was eleven. My mom left. We moved. I changed my last name legally but kept Schott for work because... because I did not want to pretend he did not exist. I wanted to be better."

Agent Chase nodded, made a note. "Has he contacted you since the escape?"

"No," Winn said, and it was a lie, and everyone who knew Winn knew it was a lie because his heartbeat spiked and he looked at his bag.

The bag buzzed.

Inside was a doll. Jester-style, 1970s costume, big forced grin, porcelain cracked at the corner of the mouth. It had been left at the front desk that morning with Winn's name on it in neat handwriting.

Winn opened it in the interrogation room Cat lent the FBI because Cat likes the FBI when they are polite and hates them when they are not. The doll said, in Winslow Sr.'s voice, soft, pleased, "Winn, my boy. My best creation. Meet me at our favorite spot. You know where."

Winn knew where. The boardwalk arcade at Santa Monica, where his father had taken him every Saturday before the murders. The place with the Superman rocket ride outside and the smell of salt and old electricity.

Kara was in the hallway, holding a file she did not need to hold, because she had heard the doll through the wall.

"I should come," she said.

"No," Winn said. "FBI sting. They will be there."

"I will be nearby," Kara said. "Not as... I will be nearby."

Adrian walked past with a stack of layouts for Cat, ordinary, human-level. He caught Kara's eye for half a second, and she saw that he had heard too, because he could hear a pen across the room and therefore he could hear a doll's speaker through a wall. He gave a tiny nod, not a plan, just an acknowledgment: I heard, I am here if needed, I will not make it a spectacle.

The arcade was exactly as creepy as an abandoned arcade should be. The lights were off but some machines were still plugged in, blinking. Robotic stuffed animals lined the walls, their eyes dark. The Superman rocket ride outside was rusted, its red paint peeling.

Winslow Sr. was waiting by the skee-ball lanes, hands in his pockets, looking smaller than his news photo. Henry Czerny's face, soft, intelligent, terribly calm.

"Winn," he said. "You came."

"You killed two guards," Winn said. His voice shook.

"I had to," Schott Sr. said. "They were between me and you. You are the best thing I ever made, Winn. Not the toys. You."

The FBI moved. Agent Chase gave a hand signal, and four agents stepped from behind machines, weapons raised.

"Winslow Schott Sr., hands where we can see them!"

Schott Sr. smiled, sad. "You should have come alone."

He pressed a button in his pocket.

The figure by the skee-ball lane flickered. It was not a man but a hologram projected onto glass, a trick of light. The real Schott had never been there.

The stuffed animals on the walls opened at the seams. Green gas hissed out, fast, heavier than air, pooling, then rising. Chlorine and something else, something that burned the nose and made the eyes water. The agents coughed, dropped to knees.

Winn shouted, "Dad, no!"

Kara was already moving. She had been outside as Kara Danvers, waiting by the rocket ride, pretending to be on a call. She stepped behind a dumpster, and the air bent for half a second where she changed, not a flash, just light folding. Supergirl ripped through the arcade's side wall, not the door, the wall, because the door was blocked by gas and she did not want to push gas toward Winn.

She inhaled.

It was not a small breath. Kryptonian lungs, filled with sun from the trip with Adrian where he had taken her above the atmosphere and held her hand while her cells drank light and remembered to overcompensate, could hold a gymnasium of air. She took in the entire cloud, every molecule of poison, in one vast vacuum breath that pulled posters off walls and made the stuffed animals collapse inward.

Then she shot straight up, through the roof, through the sky, past the cloud layer, past where commercial flights go, and exhaled. The gas dispersed in the upper atmosphere, harmless, thin, gone.

She landed back in the arcade, boots soft on broken glass, and looked at Winn.

"You okay?" she asked, quiet, the same quiet she used when she caught him after the train.

Winn was on the floor, coughing, eyes wet. "He was not even here."

"I know," Kara said.

Agent Chase was on her radio, calling for hazmat, for backup, for a perimeter. She looked at Supergirl with something like awe and something like suspicion. "How did you—"

"I was nearby," Supergirl said, and left before more questions could be asked.

At CatCo, Adrian had placed the chipped mug back on the counter. He had heard the gas alarm from three miles away? No, he had not. He had heard Kara's heartbeat spike on her comms? No. He had simply been at his desk, working, ordinary, and when Kara came back twenty minutes later, hair a little windblown, he did not ask where she had been. He handed her a file she had dropped.

"Thanks," she said.

He nodded.

That night, Alex bullied Hank into using his powers.

They were in the DEO, and Alex had a dress in a garment bag and a reservation at a restaurant she did not want to go to.

"Hank, we need to know what Lord is doing in Room 52," she said. "You saw the Jane Doe with black eyes. You saw the Red Tornado arm. You saw the Bizarro Galatea thing. We need pictures."

Hank—J'onn—looked at her, tired. He had been pretending to be human for so long that using his real powers felt like taking off a coat in winter. "Every time I shift, I lose a little control. Last week I was Supergirl to fool Cat. That was not harmless."

"I know," Alex said. "But Max Lord planted a camera on my purse last time? He has backup cameras we do not know about. He lists me as MATA HARI in his phone. Mata Hari was a French courtesan spy executed for double agency. He thinks I seduce targets for information. He is Lex Luthor with better hair. We need to know."

J'onn sighed, and his face rippled, and he became Maxwell Lord, perfect, every freckle, every smirk.

Alex whistled. "You are good at that."

"I am old at that," J'onn-as-Max said, and walked out.

Lord Industries at night was glass and light. Alex met the real Max Lord at the restaurant, wearing the dress, keeping him talking, asking about his work, about hard work and grit versus alien physiology, about how she had accomplished everything through work, not powers, and Max ate it up because he loves to be told he is self-made.

Meanwhile, J'onn-as-Max walked through Lord Industries, nodding to guards who had no idea. He reached Room 52, a biometric door. He tried the scanner. It failed, because biometrics read blood and bone and he was not Max.

So he phased. Martian phasing, molecules vibrating, stepping through solid matter as if it were air. It was a power he had not used in months, and it hurt, and it left him breathing hard on the other side.

Inside was the Jane Doe, strapped to a table, brain trauma, bracelet on wrist, eyes completely black, like a demon. Holographic projections floated around her: Red Tornado's torn-off arm, Bizarro's schematics, files labeled JANE DOE and BIZARRO and GALATEA.

J'onn took pictures with a camera Alex had given him, fast, quiet.

A guard burst in, gun raised, because Max had set off a silent alarm. J'onn-as-Max turned, smiled Max's smile.

"Just checking on my investment," he said.

The guard relaxed. "Mr. Lord, you scared me. I thought—"

"You thought what?" J'onn-as-Max asked, and the guard's eyes glazed a little, memory loss, a small Martian push.

"Nothing, sir. Sorry, sir."

J'onn left, phased back through the door, and made it to the hallway before he had to lean against a wall, exhausted.

He did not know Max had invisible backup cameras, hidden in the vents, battery-powered, not on the main grid. The camera saw a man who looked like Max walk through a door without opening it.

Max, at dinner with Alex, got a ping on his phone. He looked, smiled, and said nothing. He reached across the table and brushed a crumb off Alex's purse, planting a tiny camera, smaller than a grain of rice, in the seam.

Alex did not notice.

Supergirl went to the old Schott Toys factory after Winn told her where his father might be. Winn had figured it out, because he knew his father, because he was his father's son and he hated that he was.

The factory was abandoned, windows broken, assembly lines still, dust thick. Giant building blocks, six feet tall, stacked like a child's playroom but covered in cobwebs.

Winslow Sr. was there, sitting on a block, holding another doll.

"Supergirl," he said, almost delighted. "You are even more beautiful in person. My son has good taste in friends."

"Where is Winn?" she asked.

"Safe. For now. He is my best creation, you know. Better than any toy. I wanted to give him the world, but Dunholtz stole my designs. He stole his childhood. He stole my chance to watch him grow up."

Kara stepped forward, cape snapping in the draft from broken windows. "That does not give you the right to kill."

"Right? Right is for children," Schott said, and pressed another button.

The floor under Kara opened. Not a trapdoor, a vat. Thermite quicksand, a mixture that burned at four thousand degrees, hidden in a giant building block. It was not quicksand that drowned, it was quicksand that burned and sucked down, and it was rigged to detonate if she used heat vision to free herself. The chemical reaction would use the salt water in a standard IV—NaCl and H2O— to produce hydrochloric acid, lethal to humans, but for her it would be a thermal charge that would burn through boots and skin and keep her stuck while it burned.

She sank to her knees, then waist. The heat was immediate, even through the suit. She could feel her cells, still humming higher after the sun, baseline higher temporarily then settles higher than before cells remember overcompensate, trying to fight it.

Above her, on a track on the ceiling, a small wooden steamer trunk moved toward an industrial shredder, and inside it a child's voice screamed, "Help! Help me!"

Kara looked up, looked down, and made a choice.

She did not use heat vision. She used freeze breath.

She exhaled, not a gasp but a controlled, superpowered, proper, no-joke stream of cold that dropped the temperature in the vat from four thousand degrees to minus fifty in three seconds. The thermite froze, cracked, became brittle. She planted her hands on the edge and pulled, muscles in shoulders and back and legs, absorbing the momentum of her own weight, flight counter-rotation to keep from spinning, and ripped herself free. The frozen thermite shattered like glass.

She shot up, caught the steamer trunk an inch before the shredder, tore it open—

Inside was another doll, talking, laughing.

Schott was gone. A side door open, footprints in dust.

Kara stood in the factory, holding a doll that laughed, covered in frozen thermite, breathing hard, and she thought about how some traps are not meant to kill you but to make you feel stupid.

Winn was at Kara's apartment that night. He had not been there since before Red Tornado. He sat on her couch, holding a beer he did not drink, looking at the blanket Alex had left.

"I worry I will snap one day," he said, quiet, not looking at Kara. "Like him. I am so like him. Same cowardice, same anger. I bottle things up. I... I see the same genes as time bombs."

Kara sat next to him, not too close, because she had learned from James and from Adrian that closeness is a choice.

"Winn, your dad killing those people is similar to me losing all of Krypton," she said. "Both of our lives ended that day. When I was thirteen and my parents put me in a ship, when you were eleven and your dad mailed a bomb. But rather than giving in to rage and hate, we both strive to give back. You make things that help people. I... I try to."

Winn looked at her, eyes wet. "You are better than me."

"No," Kara said. "I am just... I have a sister who tells me when I am being an idiot. And a friend who places a chipped mug back on the desk next to a theater mask magnet and goes back to work ordinary human-level as if not helped."

Winn laughed, a little.

Then he leaned in and kissed her.

It was not a long kiss. It was a scared kiss, a bottled-up-for-years kiss. Kara pulled away immediately, gentle but firm, hand on his shoulder.

"Winn," she said.

"I know," he said, standing, embarrassed, sad, both. "I know. I just... I am done being a coward who hides his feelings and bottles everything up like my father did. I have loved you since before you were Supergirl. Since CatCo, since you smiled at me when I fixed your computer and you did not know how to turn it on."

Kara stood too. "I love you, Winn. As a friend. As my best friend. But I—"

"I know," he said again, and left.

Out in the hallway, a hand covered his mouth. Ether, sweet, chemical. Winslow Sr., who had been waiting, because fathers know where their sons go when they are sad.

When Winn woke, he was in the back of a van, hands tied with zip ties that were actually toy handcuffs, big and plastic and humiliating. His father sat across from him, holding a plastic gun.

"Chester Dunholtz will be at the National City Toy Convention tomorrow," Schott Sr. said, calm, as if discussing a shopping list. "Receiving a lifetime achievement award for designs he stole. I have made a special gun that will not set off metal detectors. You will go and shoot him in the face. If you do, we will be together. The family that kills together..."

"I will not," Winn said, voice hoarse.

"Then I will detonate ten bombs I have hidden in various toys throughout the convention floor," Schott said, showing him a phone with ten red dots. "Hundreds will die. Including you. Including your friend Kara, if she tries to save them. It is up to you. This murder by you will bring us closer together. You are the best thing I ever made, Winn."

Winn stared at the gun. It was light, plastic, toy-like, but the barrel was real.

The National City Toy Convention was bright, loud, full of children and parents and collectors and a giant display of Schott Toys through the years, including the jumping jack Dunholtz had stolen.

Chester Dunholtz was on the dais, old, smug, accepting an award.

Winn walked up the aisle, gun in his jacket, heart hammering so loud he was sure everyone could hear it. He had not texted Kara. He had not called the FBI. He thought, stupidly, that he could handle it alone, because he was his father's son and he wanted to prove he was not.

He reached the dais, pulled the gun, aimed at Dunholtz's face—

And could not.

He pointed it at the ceiling and fired. The plastic gun cracked, a small pop.

The crowd screamed.

Supergirl was already there. She had been following Winn since he left Kara's building, because Adrian had heard Winn's heartbeat go irregular in the hallway and had texted Kara a single word: hallway. And Kara had followed, quiet, no ticker, no WRAITH SEEN NEAR DEO TEST SITE, just a friend following a friend.

She saw Toyman hiding beneath the stage, phone in hand, thumb over detonate.

"Winn, bombs?" she asked, fast.

"Ten, in toys, throughout floor," Winn gasped.

Countdown: five seconds.

Kara did not try to collect them all. She could not, not in five seconds, not with hundreds of people panicking. She did what she had learned from Alex and from fighting Red Tornado and from being told she was strong before stronger than Kara.

She ripped the sprinkler pipe from the ceiling with one hand, absorbing the momentum of water pressure so it did not whip and hit a child, and activated the system. Water poured.

Then she used freeze breath, wide, controlled, proper superpowered, no jokes, and froze the water into an ice dome, thick, clear, sealing the crowd on the stage, sealing herself outside with the bombs.

The bombs went off. Ten small explosions, toy-sized, but together enough to kill.

The ice shattered, taking the entirety of the blowback, shards flying outward, melting into steam before they could cut anyone. The dome held for three seconds longer than physics said it should, because Kara held it with her own field, and then it fell, water and ice and steam, and everyone inside was wet and alive and staring.

Toyman was on the floor, caught by his own blast, leg broken, yo-yo gone.

Winn was on his knees, gun empty.

Supergirl landed next to him, quiet, you okay?

Winn nodded, crying now, not hiding.
