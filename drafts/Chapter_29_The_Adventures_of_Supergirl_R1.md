# Chapter 29: The Adventures of Supergirl

*Episode: S02E01 - The Adventures of Supergirl*

The pod was on the roof of the DEO, and Kara had opened it the night before.

She had stood there, looking at the thing that had brought her to Earth, looking at the seat, looking at the controls, and she hadn't gotten in. She had turned and walked off the roof and gone back to the work. But the pod had been there, open, waiting. And this morning, it had stopped waiting.

A second pod — identical to hers — had come down from the sky and landed on the street outside the DEO. Kara had felt it before she saw it, the way she felt the shift in pressure before a storm, the way she felt the signal before it reached her mind. She had flown up and found it, the Kryptonian metal, the same design, the same shape, and she had landed and walked to it and looked inside.

There was a man in it.

He was young.

He was unconscious.

He was Kryptonian, or he was close enough that the scanners couldn't tell the difference, and he had been in the pod, sleeping, for reasons no one could explain.

Kara had stood there with J'onn beside her, looking at this stranger who had come out of the same sky she had come out of.

She had felt the thing she had been feeling since she arrived on Earth. The reason she had become Supergirl. The reason she had held the pattern on paper. The reason she had watched the parking lot at two in the morning.

Another Kryptonian. Another one who had been through the same thing. Another one who might know what she knew, or might know what she didn't, or might know nothing at all.

"Bring him in," J'onn had said.

They had brought him to the DEO. The new facility — a skyscraper in National City, a floor of glass and steel, a place that didn't feel like the old underground bunker.

***

Kara was at her desk at CatCo.

The chipped mug Winn had given her beside the keyboard. The theater mask magnet from Adrian's desk beside that — the one Evelyn had left behind.

The small thing that had been sitting there since the day Evelyn left it.

The cartoon bandage was in her pocket.

The paper with the pattern was folded in her desk drawer.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He'd been holding the pattern for weeks. He'd keep holding it.

The names. The times. The parking lot. The back gate. The ghost code Marcus.

She had folded it and put it away the night the device had been released into the black. She hadn't needed to look at it since.

But it was there.

She looked at the things on her desk and thought about the man in the pod.

Another Kryptonian.

Another one who had come out of the sky.

She had been alone on Earth for so long.

Alone in the sense that she was the only one who had been born on Krypton and grown up on this planet. The only one who had the memories she had. The only one who had felt the loss the way she had felt it.

And now there was someone else.

Someone who might understand.

Cat walked in with her tablet, her earrings catching the light.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He didn't look up.

The pattern held. The paper didn't change. The ion blockers from Max were still in her ears, a habit she had kept even though the device was gone. She stopped at Kara's desk.

"You have two days," Cat said.

Kara looked up. "Two days for what?"

"Two days to figure out what job you want," Cat said. "You've been senior correspondent for a day. You can keep being my assistant. You can be a field reporter. You can do something I haven't thought of. But you have two days to tell me, because I'm not going to keep watching you float."

"I've been thinking about it," Kara said.

"You've been thinking about it for a year," Cat said. "Two days. Then I decide. And if you haven't decided by then, I'll decide for you, and I promise you won't like my decision."

Kara nodded.

She looked at the chipped mug.

She looked at the theater mask magnet.

She thought about the man in the pod. The two days. The job.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. He didn't look up. The pattern held. The paper didn't change.

She had spent the last three weeks holding a pattern on paper because the leak couldn't change it. Now the leak was somewhere she couldn't reach. The pattern was in a drawer.

Adrian was at the DEO. The paper open. The names. The times. The parking lot. The back gate. The ghost code.

The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He'd been holding the pattern for weeks. He'd keep holding it. The paper didn't change.

Adrian was at the DEO. The paper open. The names. The times. The parking lot. The back gate. The ghost code.

The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He'd been holding the pattern for weeks. He'd keep holding it. The paper didn't change.

And she had two days to decide what to do with the rest of her life.

***

James came by that evening with pizza and pot stickers.

It was supposed to be their first real date. Not the rushed thing they'd had before, not the interruptions and the near-kisses and the things that had always been interrupted. Just the two of them, in her apartment, with food and a movie and no emergencies. Kara had put on a dress. She had fixed her hair. She had told herself that this was going to be normal.

They were watching the launch of the Venture on TV.

Adrian had come by with pizza and the paper open beside the monitor. The pattern. The names. The ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He sat in the corner, ordinary, holding the pattern because the leak couldn't change paper.

The first commercial spacecraft. A private company's rocket heading into orbit. The kind of thing that used to only happen with government programs. James had his arm around her on the couch, and Kara was leaning into him, and it was almost normal.

Then the screen went white.

The Venture's engine had exploded. Hull breach. Catastrophic engine failure. The rocket was falling, the debris was coming down, and the news was calling it a disaster.

Kara was on her feet before the anchor finished the sentence.

She looked at James.

He looked at her, and he didn't say what she expected him to say.

He didn't say be careful.

He didn't say don't go.

He just looked at her.

She saw the thing in his eyes that had always been there. The thing that had been there since the day he had given her the Superman photograph.

The thing that had been there through every near-miss. Every interruption. Every moment when the truth had been one sentence away from being said.

"I'll be back," she said.

She flew.

***

Adrian was at the console in the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He didn't look up. The pattern held. The paper didn't change.

Superman was already there.

Kara arrived at the Venture's trajectory and saw him — the red and blue, the cape, the S-shield, the thing she had been waiting her whole life to see. He was holding the rocket, steering it away from the buildings, guiding it down to a clear space, the way she would have done it if she had been alone.

He looked at her when she landed beside him, and the look was the thing she had imagined a thousand times but had never actually seen. Family. Not just a cousin she had heard about. Not just a name in a database. Family, right there, in the air, holding the same thing she was holding, doing the same thing she was doing.

They worked together. No words, no plan, no discussion. He tilted one way, she tilted the other, and the rocket came down in a field, intact, the passengers safe, the mission saved. The crowd below was cheering, and Kara felt the thing she had been feeling since she had opened the pod that morning — the thing that had been missing and was now, for the first time, actually there.

She had a cousin. She had a team. She wasn't alone.

***

She brought him to the DEO.

The new facility was bright and clean, a floor of glass in a skyscraper, and everyone was there — Winn at his console, Alex at the scanners, Adrian at his console with the paper open and the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. the receptionist at the front desk, the bullpen humming. They looked up when Superman walked in, and Winn's face went wide, and Alex smiled, and the receptionist said something Kara didn't hear because the sound was lost in the buzz of the room.

J'onn was less enthusiastic.

He was standing at the back, near the holding room where the man from the pod was still unconscious.

Adrian was at the console across the room, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He was watching the back gate.

The pattern. The names. He'd been holding them for weeks. He'd keep holding them.

When Superman approached, the two of them stood apart. The way two men stand apart when there's something between them that neither of them wants to say out loud.

"I hear you've been keeping something," Superman said.

J'onn's face didn't change. "I've been keeping Kryptonite."

"I asked you to destroy it," Superman said.

"And I told you I needed it," J'onn said. "Against hostile Kryptonians. Against the kind of people we just stopped."

"The kind of people we just stopped were humans with drones," Superman said. "The Kryptonite was never for them."

The two of them stood there, the thing between them older than either of them, the thing that had been sitting under everything since the day J'onn had brought Kara the truth about herself. Kara stood nearby, feeling the tension, feeling the thing she had always felt when the two worlds — the Kryptonian world and the Earth world — met in the same room and didn't quite fit.

***

Winn found the lead.

He was at his console, the scanners running, the data flowing, and he looked up with the look he got when something clicked. "The Venture had a passenger manifest," he said. "One seat booked, one seat unused. Lena Luthor."

Kara looked at him. "Lena Luthor?"

"Lex Luthor's sister," Winn said. "She was supposed to be on that flight. She didn't show up. And the bomb was placed under her seat."

Alex was behind him, reading the data over his shoulder. "So the explosion wasn't random. She was the target."

Kara stood up. "Clark and I will go talk to her."

***

Lena Luthor's office was clean and bright, the kind of place that looked like it had been designed by someone who wanted the world to forget the name on the door. The company was being renamed — L-Corp, a fresh start, a clean slate, a woman trying to step out of a family's shadow.

Clark and Kara went in as journalists.

Kara had her press credentials, her reporter's notebook, the thing she had been pretending to be for a year.

Clark had his.

They asked their questions. Lena answered them.

Kara watched her. The way she sat. The way she spoke. The way she looked at them.

She felt the thing she had always felt when she met someone new. The thing that told her whether they were telling the truth.

Lena was telling the truth.

She gave them a drive with the data on the Venture's engine. She told them she was trying to build something new. She told them she had nothing to do with the explosion. And Kara believed her.

"Your brother hired someone," Clark said, when they were back in the car. "If the bomb was meant for Lena, and Lena didn't do it, then who did?"

"Lex," Kara said. "Lex hired someone to kill his own sister."

Clark didn't look surprised. "Luthors have a history of that."

***

The second attack came the next day.

Lena was in a helicopter, leaving the city, and the drones came out of nowhere — small, fast, armed, the kind of thing that looked like toys until they started firing.

They hit the helicopter, and it shuddered, and Lena looked out the window and saw the drones and the thing in her face wasn't fear.

It was the thing Kara had seen in Alex's face during the device — the thing that said I'm not going to be a victim again.

Kara and Superman arrived together. He went for the drones in the city, the ones targeting civilians. She went for the helicopter.

The drones were falling. The helicopter was stabilizing.

Kara landed on the roof of the building where the drones had come from. She found the man controlling them.

John Corben. A wanted criminal. A mercenary. A man who had been hired to do a job and wasn't going to stop until the job was done.

He looked at her and he smiled. "You're faster than they said."

"And you're less careful," Kara said.

She took the drones out.

She took his control panel out.

She didn't kill him. She didn't kill anyone.

But she stopped him. The helicopter went down safe. Lena was out of it. The city was safe.

Kara flew back to the DEO with the thing she had been carrying all day.

Adrian was at his console in the bullpen. The paper open. The names. The times.

The parking lot. The back gate. The ghost code. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor.

The theater mask magnet beside it. He looked up when she came in.

"The pattern's still holding," he said. "The names are the same. The paper doesn't change."

"The pattern's still holding," he said. "The names are the same. The paper doesn't change." The reason she had become Supergirl. The reason she had watched the parking lot at two in the morning. The reason she had held the pattern on paper.

Adrian was at his console in the bullpen. The paper open. The names. The times.

The parking lot. The back gate. The ghost code. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor.

The theater mask magnet beside it.

He looked up when she came in. "The pattern's still holding," he said. "The names are the same. The paper doesn't change."

She was doing the work. The work was real. And she had a cousin beside her.

***

But the work wasn't the only thing.

Back at CatCo, Kara found James at his desk. The date had been interrupted. The pizza was cold. The pot stickers were cold. And James was looking at her with the look he had been looking at her with since the Venture had exploded — the look that said he had been waiting for this conversation.

"You don't want this," he said.

Kara stood there. She didn't say anything. Because the thing he was saying was the thing she had been feeling for weeks. The thing she had been trying not to feel.

The thing that had been sitting under the pizza and the pot stickers and the dress and the movie. All the things that were supposed to be normal.

"I don't know what I want," she said.

"You know what you don't want," he said. "And it's me. Or it's this. Or it's the version of us that keeps getting interrupted by disasters and secrets and the thing you're not telling me."

Kara looked at him. She wanted to say the thing she had been holding for a year.

The reason she had kept the secret. The reason she had stayed ordinary in front of him.

The reason she had let every moment between them be ruined by the thing she couldn't say. But she didn't say it. She couldn't say it. Not now, not like this.

"I think we're better as friends," she said.

He didn't look surprised. He looked like he had been waiting for her to say it. He nodded. He picked up his things and he left.

Kara stood in the bullpen. She looked at his empty desk. At the chipped mug on her own desk. At the theater mask magnet. The cartoon bandage in her pocket.

The things that had been the texture of her life for a year.

And she felt the thing she had been feeling since the device.

The cost of the work. The cost of being Supergirl.

The cost of holding the pattern. Watching the parking lot. Doing the thing no one saw.

***

Cat found her later.

Kara was at her desk, the paper with the pattern in the drawer, the chipped mug in front of her, the theater mask magnet beside it. She was looking at the city through the window, the streets below, the people walking in their own steps, the city that was hers.

Cat stood in the doorway. She didn't come in. She just stood there, looking at Kara, and then she spoke.

"The question is, Kara, what the hell is wrong with you?"

Kara looked at her.

"You're young," Cat said. "You're brilliant. You have the world at your feet. You've saved this city. You've saved the world. You have a family. You have a team. And you're standing on the shore, afraid to dive in. In order to live, we have to keep daring. Keep diving."

Kara looked at her.

She thought about the man in the pod.

She thought about Superman.

She thought about the Venture and the drones and the helicopter and the work.

She thought about James.

She thought about the paper in the drawer, the pattern, the names, the parking lot, the back gate, the ghost code.

She thought about Adrian, at CatCo, ordinary, sealed, the man who had chosen the ordinary and was holding it because the device had tried to take it.

"I want to be a reporter," she said.

Cat didn't look surprised. She looked like she had known all along. "I knew that from the day you walked in here for the interview," she said. "I was just waiting for you to be ready to hear it."

***

The next day, Lena Luthor held the ceremony.

The L-Corp renaming, the press conference, the cameras, the thing Lena had been building toward — a new name, a new start, a woman stepping out of her brother's shadow in front of the whole city. Kara was there, as a reporter now, notebook in hand, the press credential on her chest. Superman was there too, standing back, watching.

And then Corben came back.

He was disguised as a policeman. The kind of thing that worked on people who weren't looking for it.

He moved through the crowd toward Lena. The bombs went off at the base of the building.

The structure started to fall.

Superman and Supergirl went for the building. Together. He on one side. She on the other.

The same thing they had done with the rocket. The same thing they had done with the drones.

The thing that felt like family. The thing that felt like the work was finally real.

The building came down safely. The crowd was safe. And Kara turned and saw Corben, his gun on Alex, Alex in his grip, the thing Kara had been trying to prevent happening right in front of her.

"Let her go," Kara said.

Corben turned, the gun on Alex's head, and he smiled. "Or what? You'll stop me again? You saved the building. You saved the city. You can't save everyone."

Kara didn't move. She couldn't move. The gun was on her sister.

The thing she had felt during the device was the same thing she was feeling now.

She could hold the pattern. Watch the parking lot. Do the work. But she couldn't do everything at once.

The one thing she couldn't afford to lose was the person on the other side of the gun.

And then Lena moved.

She had a gun. She had been carrying it, or she had found one, or she had always had one — the woman who had been targeted twice and had refused to be a victim twice. She raised it and she fired, two shots, into Corben's back.

He fell. The gun dropped. Alex was free.

Kara caught Alex. She held her sister, the way she had held her on the roof after the device, the way she had held her in the pod after the black. Alex was shaking. Kara was shaking.

"I'm okay," Alex said. "I'm okay."

"I know," Kara said. "I know."

***

That evening, Kara went back to the DEO.

The man from the pod was still unconscious. Winn had been hired officially — J'onn had made the call, and Winn had taken the job, the DEO tech room becoming his permanent desk, the scanners and the consoles and the data becoming his work. Alex was there, checking on the man, running the tests. Superman was there, standing by the window, looking at the city.

Kara stood beside him.

She looked at the city below, the lights, the streets, the people walking in their own steps.

She thought about the pod on the roof, the man inside it, the cousin beside her, the sister in the medical room, the team in the bullpen.

She thought about the paper in her desk drawer, the pattern, the names, the parking lot, the back gate, the ghost code.

She thought about the work.

"I'm going to stick around," Superman said.

Kara looked at him.

"For a while," he said. "Family. The city. The work. I've been on my own a long time. It's time I wasn't."

Kara nodded. She felt the thing she had been feeling since the morning — the thing that had been missing and was now, for the first time, actually there. She had a cousin. She had a team. She had the work. She had the ordinary — the desk, the mug, the magnet, the paper in the drawer, the things that had been the texture of her life for a year.

And she had the choice.

***

Later, Kara went to the holding room.

The man from the pod was still on the bed, still unconscious, still the mystery he had been since the morning. She stood by the door, looking at him, and she thought about the pod on the roof the night before, the thing she had been holding in her hand when she had opened it.

The ordinary. The choice. The thing a control device couldn't replicate.

She had made the choice. She was going to be a reporter. She had told James the truth, the truth she could tell. She had stopped the building and saved her sister and done the work with her cousin beside her. And the man in the pod was still there, still sleeping, still the thing that had come out of the same sky she had come out of.

She didn't know who he was. She didn't know what he wanted. She didn't know if he was a friend or a threat or something in between.

But he was Kryptonian. And she wasn't alone anymore.

***

At Project Cadmus, a woman stood over a table.

The body of John Corben was on it. He was dying — the bullets from Lena's gun had done their work, and the medical equipment was keeping him alive but not healing him. The woman looked at the readouts, looked at the man, and she made the call.

"Save him," she said.

The technicians moved. The equipment hummed. The body on the table was the body of a man who had been hired to kill Lex Luthor's sister and had failed.

The woman who had ordered it was standing in a facility that had been building toward this for a long time.

And the thing she was building was the thing that would come for Kara next.

John Corben opened his eyes.

But he wasn't John Corben anymore.

He was Metallo.
