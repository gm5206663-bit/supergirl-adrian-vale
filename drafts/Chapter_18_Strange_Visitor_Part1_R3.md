# Chapter 18: Strange Visitor

*Episode: S01E11 - Strange Visitor From Another Planet*

The forest fire wasn't supposed to be there.

A ridge of trees on the north side of the city had been green for forty years. It was a place people drove past and looked at and said it'd always there. It wasn't always there. Nothing was always there.

The fire started in the afternoon, in the dry grass beneath the trees. It spread fast. By the time the first report came in, the smoke was visible from the highway.

---

Kara was at her desk when the alert came through.

She looked at her phone. The DEO had sent a general alert. A forest fire on the north side. Evacuation in progress. All available personnel on standby.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was typing. The paper was open on his desk — the pattern, the names, the times, the parking lot, the back gate, the ghost code. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor.

The theater mask magnet beside it. He didn't look up. He didn't need to. He knew what the alert meant.

She stood up.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was typing. He didn't look up.

"There is a fire," she said.

"I saw the alert," he said.

"I'm going."

"You are going to the DEO."

Adrian stopped typing. He looked at her. His eyes were the steady, focused kind that came from three weeks of holding a pattern on paper. "Get the assignment. Get the authorization. Then go. I'll keep the ghost code log running here. If it spikes while you're in the field, I'll call Winn."

"I'm going to the fire."

Adrian stopped typing. He looked at her. "You are going to the fire before you go to the DEO."

"The DEO will tell me to wait. The DEO will tell me to stand by. The fire is already burning. People are already leaving their homes."

"And you're going to fly there and do what?"

"I'm going to fly there and stop it."

"You can't stop a forest fire. You can contain it. You can slow it. You can give people time to leave. You can't stop it."

"I can try."

Adrian looked at her for a moment. "Go to the DEO first. Get the assignment. Get the authorization. Then go."

"The authorization will take time. The fire won't wait."

"The fire won't wait. But if you go without authorization, you'll be seen. And if you're seen, it will be in the news by tonight. And if it's in the news by tonight, Lord will have something to point at."

Kara stood at her desk. Her hands were on the edge of the desk.

"I'm going to the DEO," she said.

"Go to the DEO," Adrian said. "Get the assignment. Then go."

He looked up from the paper then. His eyes were steady, the way they got when the work was real. "I'll keep the pattern running here. If the ghost code spikes while you're in the field, I'll know. I'll call Winn. He'll get to you."

Kara nodded. "Okay."

---

She drove to the DEO. She walked through the lobby and went to Alex's office.

Adrian was at the console in the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He looked up when she walked past.

"The pattern's still holding," he said. "I'll keep the log running while you're at the fire."

Alex was at her desk with a map spread out in front of her. The map showed the north side of the city. The ridge of trees. The fire. The evacuation zones.

"There you're," Alex said. "I was about to call you."

"I saw the alert."

"The fire is on the north ridge. It is moving south. It is moving toward the residential area. We're evacuating three neighborhoods. We need eyes above the fire. We need to know where it's going and how fast."

"I can do that."

"You can do that. But I need you to do it in the air, not on the ground. I need you to stay above the fire and report the movement. I don't need you to go down into it."

"I'll stay above it."

"Good. Report to the radio. Stay above. Don't go down. Don't go toward the fire. Don't go toward the smoke. Stay above and watch."

"I'll stay above."

Alex looked at her. "This isn't a rescue. This is a watch. Watch and report. Don't go down."

"I'll watch."

Kara turned and walked out. She went to the vehicle bay and got into the DEO aircraft.

Adrian was at his desk across the bullpen. He was typing. The paper was open. The pattern. The names.

He didn't look up when she left. He kept the ghost code log running.

---

She took the aircraft up. She climbed above the clouds and turned north.

The fire was visible from a long way off. A thick column of smoke rose above the ridge. The smoke was dark, heavy, and it moved with the wind.

Kara brought the aircraft to a hover above the fire. She looked down.

The trees were burning. The fire moved through the dry grass and into the canopy. It jumped from tree to tree. It moved fast. It moved the way fire moves when there's nothing to stop it.

The evacuation roads were below. Cars moved along them in a slow, steady line. People were leaving. The fire was coming. The people were going.

Kara watched the fire. She watched the wind. She watched the smoke. She reported the movement to the DEO radio.

Adrian was on the radio. "I'm watching the ghost code. It hasn't spiked. The pattern holds. I'll tell you if it does."

"Fire is moving south. Speed is increasing. Wind is from the north. The fire is feeding on the canopy."

"Acknowledged," Alex said on the radio. "Keep watching. Don't go down."

"I'm watching."

She stayed above the fire. She watched the fire move. She watched the smoke spread. She watched the evacuation roads.

Then she saw something below the fire.

A figure was on the ground. It was in the burned area, below the line of the fire. It was standing among the trees. It wasn't moving. It wasn't running. It was standing there.

Kara looked at it. She looked at it again.

It was a man. He was standing in the burned area with the fire behind him. He was wearing dark clothes. He wasn't running from the fire. He was standing in it.

"Alex," she said on the radio. "I've a person on the ground. In the burned area. South of the fire line. He isn't moving. He isn't running. He is standing."

"Can you see if he's injured?"

"I can't see clearly. He is too far. He is in the smoke."

"Stay above. Don't go down. We're sending a ground team."

"He is in the fire area. The ground team won't get to him in time if the fire moves."

"Kara. Stay above. Don't go down."

Kara looked at the figure. The fire was moving toward it. The smoke was thickening. The figure was standing still.

"He isn't running," she said.

"I heard you. Stay above."

Kara looked at the figure again. The fire was closer now. The figure was still standing. It wasn't moving. It wasn't running. It was standing in the smoke.

Adrian was on the radio. "I'm watching the ghost code. It hasn't spiked. I'll tell you if it does."

"I'm going down," she said.

"Kara. No."

"Adrian is watching the ghost code from here," Alex said. "If it spikes, he'll call."

"I'm going down. I'll get him and bring him up. I won't leave him in the fire."

"Kara. That isn't your assignment. Your assignment is to watch."

"I'm changing my assignment."

"You can't change your assignment."

"I'm already going down."

Kara brought the aircraft down. She went below the smoke and into the burned area. She brought the aircraft to the ground beside the figure.

Adrian was on the radio. "I'm watching the ghost code. It hasn't spiked. I'll keep watching."

The figure turned toward her.

He was a man. He was tall. He was wearing dark clothes. His face was ordinary. His eyes weren't. They were calm. They were too calm for a man standing in a forest fire.

"You shouldn't be here," he said.

"You shouldn't be here either," Kara said. "The fire is coming. Get in."

The man looked at the fire. He looked at the aircraft. He looked at Kara.

"I'm not leaving," he said.

"You are leaving. Get in."

"I'm not leaving."

Kara stepped out of the aircraft. She went to the man and took his arm. "Get in. The fire is moving. You won't survive it."

The man looked at her hand on his arm. He didn't pull away. He didn't move.

"I won't survive it," he said. "That isn't the same as not leaving."

"Get in," Kara said again.

The man looked at her. "You are Kara Danvers."

Kara stopped. "Yes."

"I know your mother," the man said.

Kara's hand stayed on his arm. "You knew my mother."

"I knew Alura Zor-El. I knew her well." The man looked at the fire. "She asked me to watch over you. She asked me to protect you. She asked me to be here when you needed me."

"Who are you?"

The man looked at her. "I'm Non."

---

Non. The name hit Kara like a weight.

Non was Astra's husband. Non was the general who had fought against Krypton. Non was the man who had led the rebellion. Non was the man who had been sentenced to the Phantom Zone.

Non was standing in a forest fire on Earth, wearing dark clothes, with calm eyes, saying he knew her mother.

"You were in the Phantom Zone," Kara said.

"I was."

"You escaped."

"I didn't escape. I was released."

"By whom?"

Non looked at the fire. "By the same people who released Astra. By the same people who brought her to Earth. By the same people who are working with Maxwell Lord."

Kara felt her hands tighten on his arm. "Lord released you."

"Lord didn't release me. Lord arranged for it. Lord has people on Krypton who work for him. He has people in the Phantom Zone. He has people everywhere. He used his people to bring Astra and me to Earth."

"Why?"

"Because he wanted weapons. He wanted Kryptonian technology. He wanted the power we've. He wanted us for what we could do for him."

"And Astra?"

"Astra refused. She told him no. She told him she wouldn't work for him. He kept her anyway. He kept her because he could." Non looked at Kara. "She is in a cell in the DEO. She is there because she refused him. She is there because she chose to be there rather than work for him."

"She killed people."

"She killed people who were trying to kill her. She killed people in a war. She isn't a child who doesn't know what she's doing. She is a general who made choices and is now paying for them."

The fire moved closer. The heat pressed against them. The smoke made Kara's eyes sting.

"You need to get out of here," she said.

"I'll go with you," Non said. "But not to the DEO. Not to a cell. I'll go with you to talk to your sister. I'll go with you to tell her what's happening. I'll go with you to tell her that Lord is building something and he's using us to build it."

"I'm taking you to the DEO."

"You are taking me to your sister. The DEO is where Astra is. The DEO is where Hank is. The DEO is where I don't want to go."

"Why?"

"Because the DEO isn't safe. The DEO has someone inside it. The DEO has someone who's working for Lord. The DEO has someone who has been feeding information to him."

Kara felt her stomach drop. "Who?"

"I don't know. I only know there's someone. Astra knew there was someone. She was trying to find them. That is why she was in the cell. That is why she didn't fight harder to get out. She was trying to find the leak before Lord used it."

The fire was very close now. The trees behind them were burning.

"We need to go," Kara said.

Non nodded. He got into the aircraft. Kara got in beside him.

She took the aircraft up. She climbed above the smoke and above the fire and into the clear air above the clouds.

She looked at Non. He was sitting beside her. He was calm. He was ordinary. He was a Kryptonian general who had fought against her mother and was now sitting in her aircraft telling her that there was a leak in the DEO.

"Where are we going?" she said.

"To your sister," Non said. "To Alex Danvers. I need to tell her what's happening. I need to tell her about the leak. I need to tell her that Lord is building something and he's using us to build it."

"And then?"

"And then you'll decide what to do with me. I'm not going to a cell. I'm not going to Lord. I'm going to your sister. And then you'll decide."

Kara flew the aircraft toward the city.

The fire was behind them. The smoke was behind them. The forest was behind them.

Ahead was National City. Ahead was the DEO. Ahead was Alex.

And somewhere in the DEO, there was someone working for Lord.

Kara flew toward the city. She didn't know who the leak was. She didn't know what Lord was building. She didn't know what Non was telling the truth about and what he wasn't.

She knew one thing. She had a Kryptonian general in her aircraft. He knew her mother. He knew about the leak. And he wasn't going to a cell.

She flew toward the city and let the rest of it wait.

---

She didn't go to the DEO. She went to her apartment.

She landed the aircraft on the roof of her building and took Non down the stairs to her apartment. He walked behind her without complaint. He didn't look around. He didn't examine anything. He sat at the kitchen table and put his hands on the surface, the way a man sits when he's something to say and wants to say it without being interrupted.

Kara stood by the counter. She hadn't turned on the light. The apartment was lit by the afternoon sun coming through the window.

"Wait here," she said. "I'm calling Alex."

"I don't need to wait," Non said. "I need to tell you everything before she comes. Because once she comes, she'll have opinions. And once she's opinions, the conversation will change."

"You can tell me. I'll tell her."

"You will tell her what you want her to hear. You will filter it. You will decide what's important and what isn't. I don't want you to filter it. I want you to hear it all, and then I want you to decide together what's important."

Kara looked at him. "You are asking me to listen to a Kryptonian general who fought against my mother without calling the DEO first."

"I'm asking you to listen to a man who knew your mother before she was a symbol. I'm asking you to listen to a man who has been on this planet for three months and has been watching you since he arrived. I'm asking you to listen to a man who knows there's a leak in the DEO and doesn't know who it's."

"Why should I trust you?"

"I'm sitting in your apartment. I'm not in a cell. I'm not fighting you. I'm not running. I came to you. I didn't have to. I could have stayed in the forest. I could have let the fire take me. I could have walked into the woods and let nobody find me. I didn't do that. I stood in the fire and waited for you to come down. Because I wanted to talk to you. Because I wanted to talk to Alex. Because I wanted to tell you what's happening before Lord finishes building it."

Kara looked at Non. He was calm. He was ordinary. He was a Kryptonian general. He was also a man who had been sitting in a forest fire waiting for her to come down.

She sat down across from him. "Talk."

---

Non told her about the Phantom Zone.

He told her about the years in the dark. The void. The nothing. The place where there was no time and no light and no sound and no hope.

He told her about the other prisoners. The ones who screamed. The ones who went quiet. The ones who stopped being people and became shapes in the dark.

He told her about the release. Not a release. A transfer. Someone had opened the Zone. Someone had selected Astra first. Someone had selected him second. Someone had sent them both to Earth.

He told her about the journey. A ship. A crew. Kryptonian technology. A landing in a forest on the north side of a city called National City.

He told her about Lord.

"Lord didn't find us," Non said. "We found him. Or rather, his people found us. He had people waiting for us. He had a ship ready. He had a place ready. He had a plan ready. He didn't build the plan after we arrived. He built it before. He was ready for us."

"How long has he been planning this?"

"Years. He has been planning this for years. He has been building a network. He has been buying people. He has been collecting technology. He has been preparing for the day when Kryptonians would come to Earth."

"Why?"

"Because he wants power. Not the power of a Kryptonian. The power of the people who control Kryptonians. He wants to be the man who stands behind the weapon and tells it where to point. He doesn't want to be the weapon. He wants to be the hand that holds it."

"And Astra?"

"Astra was the first. He brought her to Earth and offered her a deal. He would give her a place. He would give her resources. He would give her a way to rebuild Krypton. In exchange, she would work for him. She would use her power for his plans."

"And she said no."

"She said no. She told him she wouldn't be a weapon for anyone. She told him she wouldn't work for him. She told him she would rather be in a cell than be his."

"So he put her in a cell."

"He put her in a cell. Not because he couldn't use her without the cell. He put her in a cell because he wanted to break her. He wanted to keep her in the cell until she agreed. He has been keeping her in the cell and waiting for her to change her mind."

"And you?"

"He offered me the same deal. I said no. He didn't put me in a cell. He let me go. He let me go because he thought I'd come back. He thought I'd see that the only way to survive on this planet was to work for him. He thought I'd be afraid. I'm not afraid. I'm tired."

"Tired of what?"

"Tired of running. Tired of hiding. Tired of watching Lord build something that will hurt people. Tired of knowing there's a leak in the DEO and not knowing who it's. Tired of sitting in a forest fire waiting for your daughter to come down from the sky."

Kara didn't say anything.

"Your mother asked me to watch over you," Non said. "She asked me before the war. She asked me when I was a young officer and she was a young scientist. She asked me to protect her daughter if she couldn't. She asked me to be here when you needed me."

"You fought against her."

"I fought against her policy. I fought against her government. I fought against the council that sentenced Astra to death. I didn't fight against Alura Zor-El. I fought against the system she was part of. And the system won. And the system sent me to the Phantom Zone. And your mother didn't send me there. The council did."

"You were a general."

"I was a general. I was a soldier. I was a man who did what he was told and then did what he thought was right. And the thing I thought was right got me sent to the dark."

The sun moved across the window. The apartment got darker.

"You said there's a leak in the DEO," Kara said.

"There is a leak. Astra found it. She was tracking information that was getting to Lord. She traced it back to the DEO. She couldn't find the person. She could only find the path. The path goes through the DEO. The path goes through someone who has access to DEO systems and DEO information."

"Who has access?"

"Everyone. Hank. Alex. Winn. The tech team. The guards. The people who clean the corridors. Anyone who walks through the building can carry information out of it. But the leak isn't someone who walks out with a file. The leak is someone who's inside. Someone who has access to the systems. Someone who can send information without being seen."

"Winn has access to the systems."

"Winn has access. So does Alex. So does Hank. So does the tech team. So do I, if you let me in. The question isn't who has access. The question is who's using it. And Astra couldn't answer that question. She found the path but not the person."

"So you came to me."

"I came to you because you're Kara Danvers. You are Alura's daughter. You are the person your mother asked me to watch over. And you're the only person on this planet who has both the power to stop Lord and the reason to do it."

"I'm not a weapon."

"I'm not asking you to be a weapon. I'm asking you to listen. I'm asking you to tell your sister. I'm asking you to find the leak. I'm asking you to stop Lord before he finishes building what he's building."

Kara stood up. She went to the window and looked at the city.

"I need to call Alex," she said.

"Call her. Bring her here. I'll tell her the same things I told you. I'll tell her all of it. I won't filter it. I won't leave anything out."

"And then we decide what to do with you."

"And then you decide. I won't go to a cell. I won't go to Lord. I'll go where you and your sister tell me to go. But I won't be a prisoner."

Kara picked up her phone and called Alex.

"I need you to come to my apartment," she said.

"Why?"

"Because I've someone here who needs to talk to you. Someone you need to hear."

"Who?"

"Non."

There was a pause on the line. "Non. The Kryptonian general."

"Yes."

"You have Non in your apartment."

"I do."

"You brought Non from a forest fire to your apartment without calling the DEO."

"I did."

"Kara—"

"Come to my apartment. Hear what he's to say. Then decide what to do."

Another pause. Then Alex said, "I'm coming. Don't let him leave. Don't let him do anything. I'm coming."

"I'll be here."

Kara hung up. She set the phone on the counter.

Non sat at the kitchen table with his hands on the surface. He looked at the city through the window.

"Your sister will be angry," he said.

"My sister is always angry," Kara said. "It is how she shows she cares."

"That is a dangerous way to care."

"It is the way she cares. It is the only way she knows."

Non nodded. He didn't say anything more.

They sat there. The sun went down. The city lights came on. The apartment got dark.

Kara turned on the lamp beside the sofa. She sat in the chair across from Non.

They waited for Alex.

---

Alex arrived at six-thirty.

She came through the door with her DEO folder in her hand and the look on her face that meant she wasn't here to be polite.

"Non," she said. She didn't sit down. She stood by the door.

"Alex Danvers," Non said. He didn't stand up. He stayed seated at the kitchen table. "I've been waiting to meet you."

"You are a Kryptonian general who was sentenced to the Phantom Zone. You escaped. You came to my sister's apartment instead of coming to the DEO. You have ten seconds to tell me why I shouldn't call the DEO and have you picked up."

"Because I'm not a prisoner. And because I'm not here to be picked up. I'm here to tell you what's happening. And what's happening is bigger than me."

"Talk."

Non told her. He told her everything. He told her about the Phantom Zone. He told her about the release.

He told her about Lord. He told her about the leak in the DEO. He told her about Astra. He told her about the plan.

Alex stood by the door and listened. She didn't interrupt. She didn't sit down. She held her folder and she listened.

When Non finished, Alex was quiet for a long moment.

"You are asking me to trust a Kryptonian general who fought against my mother's government," she said.

"I'm asking you to trust a man who knew your mother before the war. I'm asking you to trust a man who's telling you the truth because he's no reason to lie. I've no power on this planet. I've no resources. I've no people. The only thing I've is information. And I'm giving it to you."

"Why?"

"Because your mother asked me to watch over her daughter. Because I've been watching. Because I've seen what Lord is building. Because if you don't stop him, he'll build it and he'll use it and people will die."

Alex looked at Kara. Kara looked back.

"What do you think?" Alex asked.

"I think he's telling the truth," Kara said. "I think he was sitting in a forest fire waiting for me to come down. I think he didn't have to. I think he chose to. And I think the information he's is real."

"And I think you're too quick to trust a Kryptonian general who fought against your mother."

"I'm not trusting him. I'm listening to him. There is a difference."

Alex looked at Non again. "You said there's a leak in the DEO."

"There is a leak."

"You don't know who it's."

"I don't know who it's."

"Astra was trying to find it. She couldn't."

"She found the path. She didn't find the person. She was in the cell before she could finish."

"And you think I can find the person."

"I think you and your sister can find the person. You have access. You have the systems. You have the people. You have the building. I don't have any of those things. I've a name and a path and a warning."

Alex closed her folder. "I'm not calling the DEO. I'm not having you picked up. I'm not taking you into custody. But I'm not letting you leave either."

"I'm not leaving," Non said.

"You are staying here. Kara will keep you here. I'll come back tomorrow. We will talk again. And in the meantime, you'll tell me everything you know. Everything. No filtering. No leaving things out. Everything."

"I'll tell you everything."

"Good." Alex looked at Kara. "Keep him here. Don't let him leave. Don't let him do anything. I'll come back tomorrow."

"I'll keep him here," Kara said.

Alex turned and walked out. The door closed behind her.

Adrian was at the console in the bullpen when Kara came back. The paper open. The ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He didn't look up from the work.

"The fire's still burning," he said. "I'll keep watching the code. If it spikes, I'll call Winn."

Non sat at the kitchen table. He looked at the door. Then he looked at Kara.

Adrian was at the console in the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. He didn't look up from the work. "The fire's still burning," he said. "I'll keep watching the code. If it spikes, I'll call Winn."

"Your sister is angry," he said.

"My sister is always angry," Kara said.

"She is angry because she's afraid."

"She is angry because she's Alex. It is the same thing."

Non nodded. He put his hands back on the table.

The lamp was on. The city lights were on outside the window. The apartment was quiet.

Adrian was at the console in the bullpen, the paper open, the ghost code log running. The chipped mug on the corner of the monitor. The theater mask magnet beside it. He didn't look up. He was holding the pattern. He was writing it on paper because the leak couldn't change it.

Kara sat in the chair across from Non. She looked at the man who had fought against her mother. She looked at the man who had waited in a forest fire for her to come down. She looked at the man who had information that could stop Lord.

She didn't know if she could trust him. She didn't know if he was telling the truth. She didn't know what would happen tomorrow when Alex came back.

She knew one thing. She had a Kryptonian general in her apartment. He had information about a leak in the DEO. And she was going to keep him there until she knew the truth.

She sat there. The night went on. The city went on outside the window.'''
