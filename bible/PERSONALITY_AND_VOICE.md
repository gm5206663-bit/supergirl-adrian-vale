# Personality and voice — working characterization

## Present baseline
Exhaustion is the author-chosen motive. Adrian seeks ordinary life; he is not automatically penitential, emotionally empty, cowardly or secretly waiting for applause. Competence, pleasure, irritation, attraction and grief can coexist. A patient present manner is behavior, not proof that historical instability never existed.

## Voice
He can speak directly and economically without making every answer a punchline. In professional scenes, he distinguishes facts from requests and does actual work. With a familiar colleague, warmth may appear in attention and practical help rather than serial flirting. When confronted with his past, he should not explain it away with a polished monologue.

## Useful internal contradictions
- He chose to limit himself, but still dislikes being misjudged.
- He values ordinary work while knowing he is concealing extraordinary facts from people who trust him.
- He wants a life without constant crisis, but that does not decide what he does when another life is at risk.
- He remembers his family as people and his victims as consequences; neither memory should become a repeated cinematic trigger in every chapter.

## Avoid
Universal charm; constant comparisons with Kara; every male colleague rendered jealous; every woman compelled to desire him; instant forgiveness; “he could destroy them all” inserted into mundane interactions; mandatory tremors/rage whenever the past is mentioned; needless cryptic speech.

## Relationship independence
Cat can criticize him accurately. Kara can disagree or fail to notice him. Winn has skills and concerns unrelated to him. James is not merely a rival. Alex's caution can be reasonable. None of these reactions automatically makes someone foolish or hostile to the protagonist.

The character may evolve. This document is a writing baseline, not a prohibition on later emotional change.
